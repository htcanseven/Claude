# Standards and test methods for oil aging of winding insulation and copper corrosion in oil-cooled EV traction motors: status update (checked 19 October 2026)

Reading note: "Evidence" says how far each item was verified. "Catalogue page" means only the bibliographic page was seen. "Preview" means the free IEC preview PDF was read; it shows the foreword, table of contents (TOC), introduction, scope, normative references and, for some documents, the bibliography, but no clause bodies. "Reseller listing (search)" means the fact came from a reseller or library catalogue shown in search results, and the page was not opened directly. No full text of any IEC, ASTM, SAE or Chinese standard was read, except the 7-page T/CAQI comment draft, which is public. Clause numbers given below are only those visible in the TOCs.

## 1. What is the current edition and status of each listed standard, and what changed since 2024?

### Takeaway
Of the documents cited in the existing review, five have changed:
- IEC 60851-5 now has a 5th edition (August 2026).
- ASTM D2244 and ASTM D714 now have 2025 editions.
- CEEIA 415-2019 was replaced by T/CEEIA 415-2023 (28 December 2023).
- IEC 60034-18-41 Ed 2.0 is still a draft: stage TCDV, forecast 1 January 2027. Its European parallel draft was deleted in July 2026.

IEC 60851-3 is at Ed 4.0 (2023); the review gave no edition for it. Unchanged: IEC 60851-4:2016, IEC 60034-18-42:2017+AMD1:2020, IEC 62535:2008, SAE J3200_202210, ASTM D8544-24, ASTM D1275-24 and ASTM D130-26 (already cited). The WCT is still the unpublished work item ASTM WK87553.

### Inferences
- The review's standards table needs these edition updates: ASTM D2244-23 → D2244-25; ASTM D714-02(2017) → D714-25; CEEIA 415-2019 → T/CEEIA 415-2023; IEC 60851-5 → Ed 5.0 (2026); IEC 60851-3 should be cited as Ed 4.0 (2023).
- For breakdown voltage and adhesion of rectangular or hairpin conductors after oil aging, the study should cite IEC 60851-5:2026 (Test 13; wider conductor range for the cylinder method) and IEC 60851-3:2023 (Test 8; clarified adhesion-loss measurement).
- The IEC 60034-18-41 revision appears to have been set back: the CENELEC enquiry draft was abandoned in July 2026 and the IEC stage is TCDV with a 2027 forecast, which suggests a second CDV. This is an inference from the stage data; no public statement was found. Until Ed 2.0 is published, the study should cite IEC 60034-18-41:2014+AMD1:2019.
- IEC 60851-4 Test 20 remains the only IEC winding-wire procedure for oil, and it is still written for transformer oil (no revision project).

### Gaps
- No public abstract was found for the technical changes in the IEC 60034-18-41 Ed 2.0 drafts, or for whether they address liquid-cooled machines.
- The changes in ASTM D130-26 (vs D130-19), D2244-25 (vs -23) and D714-25 (vs -02(2017)) were not found.
- The conflicting D1275-24 publication date was not resolved.
- The SAE MOBILUS revision history for J3200 was not opened, so a work-in-progress revision cannot be excluded.
- The full text of IEC 60851-5:2026 was not read. Whether its rectangular-wire procedures go beyond the conductor-size and continuity changes is unknown.

## 2. Which new or revised documents address fluid exposure of winding insulation, and what conditions do they specify?

### Takeaway
Three IEC TC 112 documents published in 2024–2026 now deal with insulation in liquids:
- **IEC 63177:2024** is the first IEC standard that explicitly covers *e-transmission fluids* and *liquid-cooled rotating machines used in electric vehicles*. It has a conditioning procedure for such machines and example tests for enamelled wire. It is a screening method only: no long-term aging evaluation and no threshold values.
- **IEC TS 62332-1:2026** (dual-temperature liquid–solid thermal evaluation) now covers enamelled wires and different sealing systems.
- **IEC TS 61857-42:2025** covers liquid exposure through a multifactor "EIS combined with a liquid" route and an informative annex.

The numerical conditions of all three were not visible in the public previews. The most explicit public conditions come from a Chinese 2025 group-standard comment draft (T/CAQI): ATF at 2000 ± 100 ppm water in a sealed 2 L stainless-steel tank, (155 °C/40 h → −40 °C/8 h) × 8 cycles, with retention-based pass criteria. This matches the cycle attributed to T/CEEIA 415-2019.

#### IEC TS 62332-1:2026, "Electrical insulation systems (EIS) – Thermal evaluation of combined liquid and solid components – Part 1: General requirements" (IEC TC 112)
- **Edition:** Ed 3.0, 2026-05; replaces Ed 2 (2011); technical revision. Developed through DTS 112/716/DTS and RVDTS 112/723/RVDTS. Listed as effective 26.05.2026 (EVS). Sources: [IEC TS 62332-1:2026 preview PDF via technickenormy.cz](https://www.technickenormy.cz/publicdoc/iec_previews/4236236.pdf); [SCC standards database, IEC TS 62332-1:2026](https://scc-ccn.ca/standardsdb/standards/2058695); [EVS, IEC TS 62332-1:2026](https://www.evs.ee/et/iec-ts-62332-1-2026).
- **Change list (foreword):** based on "an extensive test series", it updates expected aging times and temperatures and broadens the equipment covered. "The method now covers electrotechnical devices using different sealing systems, as well as devices using enamel covered wires" ([preview](https://www.technickenormy.cz/publicdoc/iec_previews/4236236.pdf)).
- **Scope:** a dual-temperature test procedure for thermal evaluation and qualification of EIS "containing solid and liquid components where the thermal ageing factor is dominant, without restriction to voltage class" ([preview](https://www.technickenormy.cz/publicdoc/iec_previews/4236236.pdf)).
- **Rationale (introduction):** earlier practice was either sealed-tube aging or full-scale models. Sealed tubes are impractical when components have very different thermal capability; for example, "a solid material with an RTI of 200 °C with a liquid having a 130 °C thermal capability cannot be performed efficiently". The model ages the solid and the liquid at separate temperatures in the same apparatus, using component ratios like those in real products ([preview](https://www.technickenormy.cz/publicdoc/iec_previews/4236236.pdf)).

### Inferences
- The review's statements "No international standard measures the compatibility of winding insulation with ATF or e-fluids" and "No method … was found at ISO, IEC …" are outdated. IEC 63177:2024 explicitly covers e-transmission fluids, liquid-cooled EV rotating machines and enamelled wire. It is a chemical-compatibility screening method, not an aging or life method, and sets no pass/fail thresholds.
- IEC 63177 cites IEC 60851-4:2016 normatively. This suggests its enamelled-wire examples (Table 6) build on the IEC 60851-4 procedures, possibly Test 20; this cannot be confirmed without the full text. If so, the study could present its oil arm as "IEC 63177 conditioning, with wire tests per IEC 60851 series" rather than as a deviation from Test 20.
- All three new IEC documents come from TC 112, which makes TC 112 the hub for liquid–solid insulation evaluation. TC 2 (IEC 60034-18-41/-42) and TC 55 (IEC 60851) have no fluid-specific projects.
- Chinese practice seems to share one thermal-shock protocol: sealed vessel, ATF plus water, 155 °C/40 h ↔ −40 °C/8 h, 8 cycles, retention criteria. Water levels differ: 0.5 % in the CEEIA-related sources, 2000 ppm in the T/CAQI draft, and 0.3 % in IEC 60851-4 Test 20 (from the review). A study that reports water by Karl Fischer at start and end can be compared with all three.
- The T/CAQI pass criteria (≥50 % tensile, ≥10 % elongation and edge-tear retention, no blistering, cracking or delamination) are the only explicit numerical acceptance criteria for an oil-cooled drive-motor insulation material found in a public standard-type document. They apply to composite paper, not wire.
- IEC TS 62332-1:2026's dual-temperature concept fits the planned study's problem: high-RTI films and extrudates (≥200 °C) aged in a fluid of lower thermal capability. It could be cited when justifying aging temperature in fluid.

### Gaps
- The conditioning parameters in IEC 63177 6.4 and Tables 4 and 6 (fluid volume, water, temperature, duration, cycles, specimen and measurements) were not visible. The full text is needed (from IEC; a BS EN IEC 63177 draft for comment existed in 2021, but a published EN adoption was not checked).
- The temperatures and periods in IEC TS 62332-1 (Tables 1 and 4) and its end-of-life criteria were not visible.
- The content of T/CEEIA 415-2023 was not accessible. It is unknown whether it keeps the 2019 oil-compatibility cycle, which water content it specifies, and its limit values. The lubricant-industry drafters suggest a fluid section (inference).
- No content was found for T/HBECP 0033-2026 or the electrical limits of NB/SH/T 6042-2021.
- No ISO, JASO or CEC activity was found. The ISO TC 22/SC 37 and TC 28/SC 4 work programmes were not checked directly; only web searches were run.

## 3. Does IEC TS 61857-42:2025 specify any fluid or oil conditioning?

### Takeaway
Partly. The TS explicitly includes compatibility with cooling fluids (oils) in three places:
- a "chemical compatibility considerations" clause (4.4);
- an optional multifactor route, "Thermal evaluation of an EIS when combined with a liquid (EIS_TA)" (11.2.3), cross-referenced to the IEC 62332 series (Table 11);
- informative Annex A on chemical compatibility with cooling fluids.

The preview does not show the conditioning parameters (fluid, water, temperature, duration). The core procedure is thermal aging with diagnostic subcycles of impulse PD, mechanical stress, cold and moisture exposure.

### Cited Findings
- **Bibliographic data:** IEC TS 61857-42:2025, Ed 1.0, published 2025-08-15, 51 pp., TC 112, ICS 29.080.30, ISBN 978-2-8327-0586-5, stability date 2027. ### Inferences
- Fluid exposure is optional (multifactor EIS_TA) and partly informative (Annex A), not a mandatory step of the base procedure. Any oil-conditioning procedure in Annex A is guidance only. The cross-reference to IEC 62332 suggests the liquid route borrows that series' liquid–solid aging approach, which is transformer-derived (dual temperature, ageing cells).
- The Annex B hairpin formette is a ready-made, citable specimen geometry for a hairpin study. Aging such formettes in drive-unit oil would correspond to the EIS_TA route.
- The worked aging cycles (24 h/235 °C, 96 h/215 °C, 288 h/195 °C) are well above typical ATF thermal capability. This is the case for which the IEC 62332 dual-temperature concept exists (inference).

### Gaps
- The bodies of 4.4, 11.2.3 and Annex A are not visible in the preview: fluid type, water content, temperature, duration, vessel, specimen and post-exposure diagnostics. The full TS (CHF 335) is needed to state whether it prescribes any numerical oil conditioning.
- It is unknown whether the moisture-exposure step (6.5/8.5) uses humidity or water immersion, or whether the hairpin formette is meant to be aged in fluid.

## 4. What is the status of the wire corrosion test work item, and has ASTM D8544 been revised?

### Takeaway
The WCT is still unpublished as ASTM WK87553.
- It was initiated 2023-08-18 under D02.09.
- It was withdrawn from ballot D02 (23-09) and marked "Will Reballot" after ballot D02 (24-05).
- It was reported "in full ILS" in September 2025.
- No ASTM designation exists yet.

ASTM D8544 has not been revised: D8544-24 (March 2024) is still the active edition. A ballot was reported "nearing" in September 2025, but no D8544-25 or -26 appears in any catalogue as of 19 October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **WK87553:**
  - **Title:** "New Test Method for the Determination of Wire Corrosion and Oxidation from Fluids in Liquid and Vapor States Within an Electrically Charged or Mechanical System".
  - **Scope:** monitors fluid corrosiveness through changes in the resistance of wires, where resistance change is proportional to diameter change. The wire may be copper or another material. Solution- and vapour-phase data are collected. Test temperatures run "from ambient to 180 °C for periods of up to 72 hours or more".
  - **Keywords:** Corrosion, oxidation, Copper, Wire, EV, Electric, Vapor. **Rationale:** "EV industry is having corrosion failures." **Subcommittee:** D02.09.

  Source: [ASTM work item WK87553](https://www.astm.org/workitem-wk87553), read via curl on 2026-10-19.
- **WK87553 history (embedded page data):**
  - Date initiated 08-18-2023.
  - Ballot D02 (23-09), item 028: "Withdrawn From Balloting".
  - Ballot D02 (24-05), item 041: "Will Reballot Item".
  - The page's content was last published 2025-07-24. ASTM states that work items stay online only 6 months after approval and publication.

  Source: [ASTM work item WK87553](https://www.astm.org/workitem-wk87553).
- **Status in September 2025:** WK87553 "in full ILS" ([F+L Magazine, 9 Sep 2025](https://www.fuelsandlubes.com/fli-article/astm-d02-97-identifies-critical-ev-fluid-test-gaps/)).
- **Scope difference from the review:** the work-item scope (ambient–180 °C, ≥72 h) is wider than the conditions in the review (original 130 ± 0.25 °C; commercial 80–150 °C, 72 h) ([ASTM WK87553](https://www.astm.org/workitem-wk87553)).
- **Vendor framing:** a TanEV WCT instrument page calls the method an "ASTM Method Under Development" with a typical test at 130 °C ([TanEV WCT instrument page](https://www.tannasking.com/tanev-wct-instrument/); search snippet).
- **D8544-24 scope and status:**
  - Active; "Last Updated: Mar 12, 2024"; D02.09.0G.
  - Scope: conductance changes from dendrites and deposits at 80–180 °C, typically 150 °C for up to 500 h, with applied voltage, in liquid and vapour. Note 1: testing up to 1000 h is optional and not covered.
  - 1.2: interim precision; an ILS was under way, with a full precision statement expected by December 2024.
  - 5.1: the test is "recommended in the SAE J3200".
  - The page shows no supersession notice.

  Source: [ASTM store, D8544-24](https://store.astm.org/d8544-24.html).
- **D8544 in September 2025:** "nearing ballot"; the article does not say whether this is a revision or a precision-statement ballot ([F+L Magazine, 9 Sep 2025](https://www.fuelsandlubes.com/fli-article/astm-d02-97-identifies-critical-ev-fluid-test-gaps/)).
- **No newer edition found:** searches for D8544-25 and D8544-26 found only D8544-24 ([NDLS record, ASTM D8544-24](https://ndls.org.cn/standard/detail/22b1f5b78a38435c70b3c3537aee8364); [normdocs catalogue, ASTM D8544-24](https://catalogue.normdocs.ru/catalog/com.normdocs.astm.card.d8544-24/Standard-ASTM-D8544-24)).
- **Peer-reviewed support for WCT and CDT:** Hunt, G., Choo, L. and Newcomb, T. (Lubrizol), "100 Years of Corrosion Testing—Is It Time to Move beyond the ASTM D130? The Wire Corrosion and Conductive Deposit Tests", SAE Int. J. Fuels Lubr. 17(1):17–35, 2024 (published online 2023-09-22), doi:10.4271/04-17-01-0002. The authors expect WCT and CDT to enter OEM specifications "over the next decade"; the abstract gives no standardisation status ([SAE MOBILUS](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/04-17-01-0002)).

### Inferences
- The study should cite the WCT as "ASTM WK87553 (proposed test method; not yet approved)", with the original method paper (Gahagan and Hunt 2016, already in the review), and should not give a D-number.
- The D8544 ballot reported in 2025 may lead to a D8544-26 or later. The study should cite D8544-24 and re-check the ASTM store before submission.
- D02's EV-fluid work (D02.97, D02.L0.12) is moving toward fluid-side electrical tests (conductivity, dielectric breakdown and dielectric constant of aged fluids), but none is published and none addresses winding-insulation compatibility.

### Gaps
- The WK87553 ILS results, ballot outcome after D02 (24-05), and target publication date were not found. The work-item page was last updated July 2025.
- It is unknown whether the D8544 ballot is a revision or a precision statement, and what its outcome was.
- The ASTM website shows a notice that using AI on ASTM standards and related intellectual property is prohibited. For that reason only public catalogue and work-item metadata were read; no ASTM full text was accessed or reproduced.
