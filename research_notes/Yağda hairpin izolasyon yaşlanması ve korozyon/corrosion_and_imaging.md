### Cited Findings

**EV-specific: Conductive Deposit Test (CDT) = ASTM D8544**
- ASTM D8544-24 "Standard Test Method for Determination of Conductive Deposits of Electrical and Mechanical Components from Fluids in Liquid and Vapor States within an Electrically Charged System" (Committee D02, subcommittee D02.09.0G). Scope: the apparatus "is utilized to monitor the formation of dendrites and deposits that are conductive"; oxidation conditions "can vary from 80 °C to 180 °C" and changes in conductance are monitored over time; temperature, voltage and time "can be altered according to the industry need". Typical testing is "150 °C over a period of up to 500 h with a monitored voltage applied"; testing to 1000 h is an option not covered by the method. "Both the liquid and vapor areas are monitored for this condition over time." A "reported index based on the rate of deposit formation may be implemented." Significance: "Fluids known to perform poorly in the field have been shown to fail this test"; the method is recommended in SAE J3200. Interim precision only; full ILS precision statement was expected by December 2024 (landing page/scope only opened; DOI 10.1520/D8544-24) [STD] — [ASTM D8544-24 store page](https://store.astm.org/d8544-24.html)
- Test-lab description of D8544: a printed circuit board with copper traces "stands in for" copper-containing components such as motor windings, exposed to the fluid with minimal flow and a closed but not sealed headspace; failure is signalled when resistance across the trace gap falls to a low value; speed of drop and final resistance indicate suitability; a Conductive Deposit Index (CDI) quantifies resistance variability that rises as deposits form; pass/fail limits are currently set by each OEM; ICP (ASTM D5185) of the oil before and after the CDT, reporting change in Cu and S, is commonly requested. In a dilution study one fluid was poor in the vapour phase and another poor in the oil phase. Page undated, internal evidence ~mid-2024 [VENDOR - Savant Labs] — [Savant Labs: Electric Driveline Fluid Testing Update](https://www.savantlab.com/testing-highlights/electric-driveline-fluid-testing-update/)
- STLE feature (Jan 2024): in the CDT a test PCB is heated in the fluid "for a period of time ranging from hundreds of hours to thousands of hours" with voltage on the copper traces; "If the resistance drops below a specific value, the fluid has failed"; end-of-test photographs of the board are assessed for deposit accumulation and dendrite formation; mechanism given is copper sulfide deposits bridging conductors at different potentials, causing shorts. Quote (G. Miiller, Savant Group): "The CDT offers an index value over time that can be utilized by the OEMs or specification setting organizations to determine critical limits." [TRADE - STLE TLT] — [Aikin, TLT Jan 2024, "Evolution of copper corrosion testing for electric vehicle lubricants"](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2024/01_January/Feature.aspx); reprinted 15 Apr 2024 — [Plant Engineering reprint](https://www.plantengineering.com/evolution-of-copper-corrosion-testing-for-electric-vehicle-lubricants/)

**EV-specific: Wire Corrosion Test (WCT) - Lubrizol origin, ASTM work item WK87553**
- WCT principle: thin copper wires of known length and resistance placed in the liquid and vapour phase, resistance monitored in real time at a set temperature for 72 h, giving a corrosion rate; Miiller: the "WCT detects corrosion and failures when the D130 test does not"; WCT and D130 "can provide distinctly different results"; the CDT "will capture conductive deposit failures that neither of the other two tests ... are able to directly assess"; corrosion rate usually rises with temperature "but surprisingly this is not always the case." [TRADE] — [STLE TLT Jan 2024](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2024/01_January/Feature.aspx)
- Test-lab description: "A thin wire is exposed to an oil and its vapor with a separate circuit for each phase"; 72 h at controlled temperature, typically 80-150 °C; wires suspended with no substrate to collect deposits and "spaced sufficiently far apart that no deposits would be able to bridge the gap" (i.e., WCT isolates metal loss from deposit bridging); from initial diameter and resistance, wire diameter and corrosion rate can be calculated at any time; hottest temperature is not always most severe, especially in vapour; duration can be extended for good fluids; ASTM D02 Subcommittee 9 planned an ILS on the WCT before the December 2024 ASTM meeting; Savant has offered the WCT since 2021 [VENDOR - Savant Labs] — [Savant Labs](https://www.savantlab.com/testing-highlights/electric-driveline-fluid-testing-update/)
- Lubrizol developers named as Gregory Hunt (senior research chemist) and Michael Gahagan; wire "about the thickness of a human hair"; "the removal of material increases resistance in proportion to the amount of material lost"; works for liquid and vapour phase; numerical, repeatable output; the method "is going through the ASTM accreditation process"; EP (extreme-pressure) additives that protect gears "can promote copper corrosion" and one OEM accepted a compromise retaining some EP chemistry based on WCT data; test also applicable to aluminium (busbars). Article undated [TRADE] — [Donaldson, E-Mobility Engineering, "Lubrizol develops award-winning wire corrosion test"](https://www.emobility-engineering.com/lubrizol-wire-corrosion-test/)
- Trade review of copper tests for EV lubricants (Shah et al., Koehler Instrument, PIN Feb/Mar 2023): WCT uses a thin copper wire suspended in lubricant and in its vapour "with a direct current of 1 mA applied" at constant temperature; resistance is normalised with Ohm's law (R = rho*l/(pi*r^2)) and copper's temperature coefficient of resistivity to a standard 1.01 m length, from which material lost is calculated. Summarises Hunt & Prengaman (SAE 2020-01-0561): five commercial hybrid/EV transmission lubricants at 110, 120, 130 and 150 °C; "Lubricant A in the vapor phase featured the highest amount of corrosion at 130 °C, yet much less corrosion at any other temperature, even higher temperatures", whereas in the solution phase "Lubricant C had the most corrosion at 150 °C". Also describes a Solenoid Test (Bares et al., SAE Int. J. Fuels Lubr. 13(3):251-264, 2020): solenoid immersed for 250 h, energised at 1 Hz, brass spacer and brass bearing photographed before/after at 120 and 150 °C; ATF least corrosive at 120 °C but "much more significant corrosion" at 150 °C; an automotive gear oil (AGO) corroded significantly at both. Notes EVs can contain up to ~183 lb copper, mostly in stator windings [TRADE/VENDOR] — [Shah, Schevon, Giannuzzi, Malinski, "The Possibility of Using Copper Corrosion Tests for Lubricants Used with Electric Vehicles", PIN Feb/Mar 2023 (PDF)](https://koehlerinstrument.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/The-Possibility-of-Using-Copper-Corrosion-Tests-for-Lubricants-Used-with-Electric-Vehicles.pdf)

**ASTM D130 and optical rating**
- ASTM D130 is criticised as subjective: ratings depend on operator judgement and vary between labs; it applies no voltage and rates only the end state; D130 temperatures/durations do not necessarily match EV conditions; "extended D130" and vapour-phase copper tests lengthen exposure but still end with a visual rating. J. Ayala (Ayalytical) notes 7-8% of people have some colour-vision deficiency. A draft optical method, CuDDI (Copper Digital Detection Imaging), uses a CCD camera, a stabilised LED source at ~4,500 K diffused at 45°, automatic strip-size detection, auto-rotation to record both faces, and an algorithm trained on ASTM D130 colour standards and Table 1 logic; it "has not yet been established as an ASTM test method" [TRADE] — [STLE TLT Jan 2024](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2024/01_January/Feature.aspx); [Plant Engineering reprint](https://www.plantengineering.com/evolution-of-copper-corrosion-testing-for-electric-vehicle-lubricants/)

**EV / ATF fluids - additive chemistry, temperature, vapour phase**
- Leeds PhD thesis (B. Warren, 2018; sponsor-linked work with Lubrizol co-authors in the derived paper): immersion coupon tests (FTIR, SEM, XPS) plus thin-wire resistance tests on ATF additive matrices. Findings: "increasing the temperature at which testing took place did not always speed up the corrosion process" - in one case higher temperature slowed it and in most cases it changed the mechanism; a thiadiazole corrosion inhibitor "showed good inhibition until 150 °C when it broke down and caused pitting on the copper surface"; additive antagonisms seen at 120 °C (dispersant 1 vs. an antiwear/antioxidant/friction-modifier mix) and at 150 °C (detergent 2 vs. the same mix); ASTM D130 rating did not correlate well with weight change, copper in used fluid or wire-radius change, and film formation was sometimes mistaken for corrosion [THESIS] — [Warren, "The Interaction of Automatic Transmission Fluid Additives with Copper", PhD, University of Leeds, 2018 (record + full PDF opened)](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/)
- Same thesis, discussion chapter: three fully formulated fluids produced flaking surfaces on copper (SEM); flaking is a known signature of copper sulfide layers; all flaking samples lacked the benzotriazole-type inhibitor ("corrosion inhibitor 2") and had higher levels of the thiadiazole inhibitor and a quaternary-amine dispersant - sulfur came from additive molecules (thiadiazole inhibitor, a dispersant, a detergent), not the base oil (<0.03 % S). In one fluid, FIB cross-sectioning plus EDX showed copper and sulfur as the main components of a cracked, porous surface layer; "copper sulfide is known to be partially conductive", which the author used to explain why wire-radius loss (2 µm) understated the 306 ppm copper found in the end-of-test fluid [THESIS] — [Warren 2018 full text PDF](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/1/Warren_BLM_Mechanical_Engineering_PhD_2018.pdf)
- Peer-reviewed derivative: thin-wire resistance method plus coupon tests on two commercial ATF corrosion inhibitors at 110, 120, 130 and 150 °C; "Both inhibitors were found to be equally as effective up to temperatures of 130 °C; however ... at 150 °C, the thiadiazole inhibitor is breaking down leading to severe pitting on the surface. Corrosion performance therefore, cannot be assumed to be effective at all temperatures" (abstract only, via Crossref) [PEER] — [Warren, Hunt, Bryant, Neville, Morina, Gahagan, Lubrication Science 2018, DOI 10.1002/ls.1422 (Crossref record)](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=1&query.bibliographic=Automatic%20transmission%20fluid%20corrosion%20inhibitor%20interactions%20with%20copper)
- Hunt & Prengaman (Lubrizol), SAE 2020-01-0561: five commercial electrified/conventional transmission lubricants measured simultaneously in vapour and solution phase with an updated wire-resistance test at 110-150 °C; per the secondary summary, vapour-phase and solution-phase rankings differed (e.g., one lubricant worst in vapour at 130 °C but much milder at higher temperature) [CONF, summarised via TRADE source; SAE full text not opened] — [PIN Feb/Mar 2023 review](https://koehlerinstrument.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/The-Possibility-of-Using-Copper-Corrosion-Tests-for-Lubricants-Used-with-Electric-Vehicles.pdf)
- Extreme-pressure (EP) additives that protect gears "can promote copper corrosion"; Lubrizol reports an OEM-accepted compromise keeping some EP chemistry based on wire-test data [TRADE] — [E-Mobility Engineering](https://www.emobility-engineering.com/lubrizol-wire-corrosion-test/)
- Antiwear, EP and friction-modifier additives compete with copper passivators (film-forming triazoles/thiadiazoles that chemically bond to the copper surface) for the metal surface; passivator treat rates <0.1 % usually suffice where exposed copper area is low; water, oxygen and "active sulfur" attack copper; operating metal temperature promotes corrosion [TRADE, quoting additive-supplier chemists from Functional Products Inc. and Vanderbilt Chemicals] — [STLE TLT Jan 2024](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2024/01_January/Feature.aspx)

**Conductive deposits (copper sulfide) - the EV-specific failure mode**
- Lubrizol patent US 12,529,004 B2 ("Method to minimize conductive deposits", priority 2022-04-06, granted 2026-01-20): "A conductive layer deposit is defined as the formation of solids created by corrosion or lubricant degradation by-products"; "the chemical identity of these layers has been investigated and determine[d] to be copper sulfide"; "Formation of this conductive deposit may result in catastrophic failure of the electric drive unit" via winding shorts or power-electronics failure. Test: copper PCBs immersed in the solution phase and positioned in the vapour phase of lubricant in a closed, vented vessel at 150 °C under 5 V, with real-time conductance (µS) and end-of-test visual inspection of boards. A lower-sulfur lubricant formed no deposits; a higher-sulfur comparator formed conductive deposits in solution and vapour phases simultaneously; a third fluid optimised for FZG wear and solution-phase copper corrosion still failed the deposit test. Claimed mitigation: borate ester (0.3-1 wt %) plus thiadiazole (0.01-1.0 wt %). The patent cites a figure in Hunt & Prengaman (SAE 2020-01-0561) showing an electric-motor failure caused by a conductive deposit shorting windings [PATENT] — [US12529004B2 (Google Patents)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12529004B2/en)

**Transformer analogue - corrosive sulfur, Cu2S formation and migration into paper**
- IEC 62535:2008 (TC 10) scope: detection of potentially corrosive sulphur in used and unused mineral insulating oil; "The test method uses a copper conductor, wrapped with one layer of paper, immersed in the oil and heated ... to evaluate the capability of the oil to yield copper sulphide and transfer it to paper layers"; ASTM D1275 Method B is also used; tests with and without paper are complementary and may give different results; stability date 2029 [STD] — [IEC webstore IEC 62535:2008](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7167)
- Doble application note (L. Lewand, 22 Oct 2007): CCD tests began development in 2004 to determine whether Cu2S deposits on the conductor or in the paper. Oxygen and temperature steer where Cu2S ends up: "most of the deposition found in the paper was in air breathing vials and not sealed vials"; "the sealed vials pushed the deposition towards the copper instead of the paper"; ageing at 150 °C pushed deposition toward the copper, whereas 140 °C for 4 days pushed it toward the paper. Deposits on paper "with a metallic sheen that reduce the dielectric strength of the paper" are considered fails; dull deposits can be oil/paper degradation by-products and are passes [VENDOR - Doble] — [Doble AppNote: Covered Conductor Deposition Tests (PDF)](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/Doble_AppNote_CCD_tests.pdf)
