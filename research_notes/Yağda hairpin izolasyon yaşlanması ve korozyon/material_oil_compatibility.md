# Material-level compatibility of electrical-insulation polymers with ATF / e-fluids / cooling oils, and standard immersion test methods

Scope of this file: non-electrical (material) changes after oil immersion, degradation mechanisms, and standardised immersion/compatibility test methods. Electrical-ageing data, copper corrosion/imaging and industry e-fluid protocols are covered by other researchers. Source-type tags: [peer-reviewed], [conference], [thesis], [standard], [patent], [vendor], [trade press], [secondary].

## Q1. Measured property changes of each insulation polymer after immersion in ATF, e-fluids, gear/transformer oils

### Takeaway
Independent, quantified, material-level oil-immersion data for the exact hairpin insulation candidates is sparse. The most useful peer-reviewed datasets are: (i) Oviedo (García et al. 2022): PEEK/PTFE/PA66 in four commercial-type ATFs at 100 °C for up to 672 h, where PEEK and PTFE barely changed; (ii) two Chinese "Insulating Materials" papers (2022, 2024) on impregnating resins, enamelled flat wire and aramid paper in ATF at 150 °C for up to 2 000 h. These show that water in the ATF (0.2–0.5 %) is the dominant degradation driver: epoxy-anhydride bond strength fell to 4.29 % of initial, one enamelled flat wire cracked and peeled at 1 500 h, and aramid-paper elongation retention fell to 18–32 %. Dry ATF alone was comparatively benign in both studies.

### Inferences
- The peer-reviewed data agree that dry ATF at 100–150 °C does little to PEEK, PTFE, polyester-imide resin or aramid paper over 672–2 000 h. Free or dissolved water (0.2–0.5 %), with acids formed by oil oxidation, is what separates good from bad materials, especially ester-containing networks (anhydride-cured epoxy) and some enamels. A study that ages hairpin specimens should therefore control and report the oil's water content (Karl Fischer), and preferably run a dry and a wet arm.
- The Oviedo PEEK DSC rise, from 31.6 J/g to as much as 76 J/g at 100 °C, is unexpected for ageing below PEEK's Tg (about 143 °C, to be confirmed from a datasheet). The DSC was a simultaneous TGA/DSC (SDT Q600) at 20 °C/min, and the ageing time for the DSC tables is not stated. Treat it as indicative, not as proof of oil-induced crystallisation.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed quantitative data yet found for extruded PEEK magnet wire, PEEK tube or PEEK heat-shrink in ATF/e-fluid with water control (only vendor statements; see Q4).
- The 2024 Chinese study does not name the ATF or the enamel chemistries (wires C and D), which limits its transferability.

## Q3. Standardised immersion / compatibility test methods (titles, editions, conditions)

### Takeaway
Only one standard targets enamelled winding wire in oil: IEC 60851-4:2016 Test 20. It has a 24 h / 150 °C hydrolysis check (dry oil vs oil + 0.3 % water, visual and adherence only) and a 1 000 h oil-resistance test (sealed vessel with oil + paper, at the wire's class temperature capped at 150 °C, followed by breakdown voltage and crack checks). ASTM D1676-24 has an analogous "Resistance to Insulating Liquids and Hydrolytic Stability" section. Everything else is generic: plastics (ASTM D543-21 / ISO 175:2010), rubbers (ASTM D471-16a(2021) / ISO 1817), oil-side screening (ASTM D3455-11(2019)) and sealed-tube methods from the refrigeration world. All of these were written for transformer oil, IRM oils or refrigerants, not ATF/e-fluids. ATF compatibility work in the literature borrows ASTM D7216 (engine oil vs seal elastomers) or transmission-OEM elastomer specifications.

- **Industry practice in China** (not a formal standard): sealed stainless-steel cans with ATF + water at 150 °C for up to 2 000 h. One variant adds a breather vent and periodic water dosing to hold 0.2 %. Material tests then follow GB/T equivalents of IEC methods: GB/T 4074.5 (≙ IEC 60851-5) for breakdown, GB/T 15022.2 for resins, GB/T 20629.2 for aramid paper, GB/T 6324.8 (Karl Fischer) for oil water — [Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008); [Wei Xiongqiang et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.07.008)
- **IEC 61033:1991 Method A** (twisted-coil bond strength of impregnating agents on enamelled wire) was used to track resin degradation after ATF ageing — [Wei Xiongqiang et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.07.008)
- **Patent practice** (Japanese wire makers; seen in search snippets only, patent full texts were not accessible): a 300 mm straight specimen in a closed SUS vessel with 1 300 g ATF + 6.5 ml water (≈0.5 wt %) at 150 °C for 500 h, followed by a bend test. Listed as an unverified lead under Gaps.

### Inferences
- For a hairpin study, IEC 60851-4 Test 20 is the closest "standard" anchor (sealed vessel, defined water dosing, 150 °C, dry and wet arms), but its fixed fluid is transformer oil. Substituting ATF or e-fluid, recording the fluid's water content by Karl Fischer, and extending to 1 000–2 000 h at 150 °C (as the Chinese studies do) would be a justifiable, documented deviation.
- Practice B of ASTM D543 and ISO 22088 (ESC) are the only standard routes found for strain-assisted attack. Strain matters for bent hairpins (the end-turn bend and twist region), so a strained specimen arm is worth considering.

### Gaps
- Full texts of ASTM D1676-24 §186–195, ASTM D3455 and ASTM D5642 could not be opened (ASTM pages are product pages only), so their temperatures, durations and ratios are unverified here.
- ISO 175:2010 and ISO 1817 (2022/2024/possible 2026 edition) pages returned HTTP 403, so their current editions are not verified here.
