# Material-level compatibility of electrical-insulation polymers with ATF / e-fluids / cooling oils, and standard immersion test methods

Scope of this file: non-electrical (material) changes after oil immersion, degradation mechanisms, and standardised immersion/compatibility test methods. Electrical-ageing data, copper corrosion/imaging and industry e-fluid protocols are covered by other researchers. Source-type tags: [peer-reviewed], [conference], [thesis], [standard], [patent], [vendor], [trade press], [secondary].

## Q1. Measured property changes of each insulation polymer after immersion in ATF, e-fluids, gear/transformer oils

### Takeaway
Independent, quantified, material-level oil-immersion data for the exact hairpin insulation candidates is sparse. The most useful peer-reviewed datasets are: (i) Oviedo (García et al. 2022): PEEK/PTFE/PA66 in four commercial-type ATFs at 100 °C for up to 672 h, where PEEK and PTFE barely changed; (ii) two Chinese "Insulating Materials" papers (2022, 2024) on impregnating resins, enamelled flat wire and aramid paper in ATF at 150 °C for up to 2 000 h. These show that water in the ATF (0.2–0.5 %) is the dominant degradation driver: epoxy-anhydride bond strength fell to 4.29 % of initial, one enamelled flat wire cracked and peeled at 1 500 h, and aramid-paper elongation retention fell to 18–32 %. Dry ATF alone was comparatively benign in both studies.

### Inferences
- The peer-reviewed data agree that dry ATF at 100–150 °C does little to PEEK, PTFE, polyester-imide resin or aramid paper over 672–2 000 h. Free or dissolved water (0.2–0.5 %), with acids formed by oil oxidation, is what separates good from bad materials, especially ester-containing networks (anhydride-cured epoxy) and some enamels. A study that ages hairpin specimens should therefore control and report the oil's water content (Karl Fischer), and preferably run a dry and a wet arm.
- The Oviedo PEEK DSC rise, from 31.6 J/g to as much as 76 J/g at 100 °C, is unexpected for ageing below PEEK's Tg (143 °C onset per ISO 11357-2 on the [Victrex XPI 174 datasheet](https://victrex.com/downloads/datasheets/victrex-xpi-174-polymer)). The DSC was a simultaneous TGA/DSC (SDT Q600) at 20 °C/min, and the ageing time for the DSC tables is not stated. Treat it as indicative, not as proof of oil-induced crystallisation.

## Q2. Documented degradation mechanisms

### Takeaway
The evidence points to water-driven chemistry, made worse by oil oxidation, as the dominant mechanism:
- hydrolysis of ester linkages (anhydride-cured epoxy; ester-containing networks) and of imide/amide linkages (PI, aramid);
- acid formation, from oxidation of the base oil/additives (accelerated by water, air and dissolved copper) or from hydrolysis of ester-type base oils, which catalyses further hydrolysis.

Purely physical effects (oil uptake/swelling, plasticisation, extraction, annealing/crystallinity change) are small for PEEK/PTFE but significant for silicone and nylon. Direct additive attack (amines on imide rings) is chemically plausible but undocumented at ATF additive concentrations. Environmental stress cracking of amorphous thermoplastics is documented only as vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
- **Hydrolysis of ester linkages in anhydride-cured epoxy** (peer-reviewed). After 2 000 h at 150 °C in Dexron ULV + 0.5 % water: OH band up, ester C=O (1 735 cm⁻¹) and C–O (1 300–1 000 cm⁻¹) bands down, O fell from 23.67 % to 10.71 % by XPS, and bond strength fell to 4.29 % of initial. The same resin in dry ATF behaved like the polyester-imide. The authors conclude "hydrolysis at high temperature is the main reason" — [Wei Xiongqiang et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.07.008)
- **Water + air → oil oxidation → acids → accelerated hydrolysis** (peer-reviewed). "Water and air can promote the hot oxygen ageing of ATF to generate acidic substances, which can accelerate the hydrolysis of material." Performance loss rose sharply from 0 to 0.2 to 0.5 % water in 384-h screening; for example, wire C breakdown retention went 95.5 → 88.5 → 52.3 % — [Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)
- **Acid from hydrolysis of ester-type base oils attacking enamel** [patent]. The inventors attribute film degradation mainly to acids (especially short dibasic acids such as adipic, sebacic and phthalic) formed when ester oil hydrolyses, not to water itself. Acid-scavenging Na/Ca particles in the film raised 2 016-h BDV retention from about 1.5 % to 66–77 % at 155 °C — [Google Patents US9349499B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9349499)
- **Hydrolysis of polyimide (condensation polymer)** [vendor]. Kapton "is made by a condensation reaction; therefore, its properties are affected by water". The rate rises with temperature, water content and pressure. Base sensitivity: 2 N NaOH for 10 min at 23 °C leaves 54 % elongation retention, versus 89–94 % for neutral solvents and 2 N HCl — [DuPont Kapton Summary of Properties](https://www.electro-wind.com/web-files/DuPont\Literature\Kapton-summary-of-properties.pdf)
- **Hydrolysis/H-bond disruption in meta-aramid** (peer-reviewed). Nomex 410 in transformer fluid at 170 °C for about 7 500 h lost only about 5 % tensile strength but 45 % (dry) to 70 % (wet) of elongation. This is attributed "probably" to hydrolysis and to water breaking and re-forming hydrogen bonds at high temperature, reducing plastic deformability — [Arroyo Fernandez et al. 2020, Polymer Testing](https://constellation.uqac.ca/id/eprint/5442/)
- **Amine attack on the imide ring (aminolysis)** (peer-reviewed; recycling context, i.e. neat amines, not additive levels). Kapton film was "fully degraded" in ethanolamine and ethylenediamine. With ethanolamine at 120 °C for 3 h, the 4,4′-ODA diamine yield was 91 %; "the C─N bond of Kapton film is completely destroyed in aminolysis". Kapton could not be degraded directly in HCl without the prior aminolysis step — [Chen et al. 2025, Advanced Science 12(13) 2414416](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11967771/)
- **Copper-catalysed oil oxidation** (peer-reviewed; transformer oil-paper). In accelerated ageing, Cu(I) and Cu(II) species were "the active sites for inducing intense free radical reactions", lowering activation energy and making the oil "more susceptible to oxidation". Copper nanoparticles on the paper fibres grew from 27.06 to 94.19 nm. Adding the antioxidant DBPC cut the ageing rates of dissipation factor, acidity, micro-water and BDV by 28.8 %, 43.2 %, 52.9 % and 46.7 % compared with the copper-only group — [Zhang et al. 2024, Molecules 29(19) 4690](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/molecules/molecules-29-04690/article_deploy/molecules-29-04690.pdf)
- **Physical uptake/swelling and plasticisation** (peer-reviewed):
  - Silicone (VMQ) took up hydrocarbon oil (new –CH₂– FTIR bands at 2 855/2 925 cm⁻¹, volume increase) and lost hardness and up to about 79 % of tensile strength in one ATF at 100 °C.
  - PEEK/PTFE showed −0.5 to −1 % volume change and about 3 Shore D softening, with no tensile-strength change.

  — [García-Tuero et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/api/records/10702164/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Elastomers.pdf/content); [García et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/records/10702226/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Structural%20Polym.pdf?download=1)
- **Crystallinity change / annealing in hot oil** (peer-reviewed). PA66 melting enthalpy nearly doubled (11.03 → up to 29.71 J/g) with −75 to −80 % elongation and +15 % tensile strength, i.e. embrittlement. PEEK melting enthalpy rose (31.60 → 45.89–76.16 J/g) without mechanical change. The authors note that Decker et al. found PA66 crystallisation after 2 000 h at 130 °C in ATF but more degradation in air, and Lann et al. attributed PA66 changes mainly to temperature — [García et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/records/10702226/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Structural%20Polym.pdf?download=1)
- **Reversible water absorption vs irreversible hydrolysis.** IEC 60851-4 Test 20 separates them by testing breakdown both as removed and after drying 30 min at 125 °C: "If only absorption has occurred, drying … will produce a recovery" — [IEC 60851-4:2016 text](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/143987489/IEC-60851-4-2016-en-fr.pdf)
- **Moisture loss in vented systems** (peer-reviewed). With a breather vent, ATF water fell from 0.2 % to about 0.12 % within 12 h at 150 °C, so a "sealed" lab test can overstate water exposure and a vented one understate it unless water is replenished — [Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)

### Inferences
- For hairpin insulation, the chemistries most at risk in wet, hot ATF are those with hydrolysable linkages in the main chain or network: ester-containing resins (anhydride-cured epoxy, polyester and polyester-imide enamels, PET in DMD/NMN), imides/amides (PI, PAI, aramid) and esters in ester-based e-fluids. The materials with no hydrolysable linkages (PEEK, PPS, PTFE/FEP/PFA, parylene) should be governed mainly by physical effects (uptake, annealing) and by thermal oxidation. This follows from chemistry and is consistent with the data above, but has not been directly demonstrated for every material in ATF.
- The polyester-imide impregnating resin, which also contains ester groups, survived ATF + 0.5 % water while the anhydride-cured epoxy failed. Network design and cure state therefore matter more than the label "ester-containing". Ranking by chemical class alone is unreliable; testing is needed.
- Because acids from oil oxidation drive hydrolysis, specimens aged with bare copper exposed (cut ends and stripped hairpin legs) may age faster than oil-only controls through copper catalysis. A copper-in/copper-out comparison would separate this effect.
- The neat-amine aminolysis result shows that the imide C–N bond is chemically vulnerable to primary amines. Whether succinimide dispersants or amine antioxidants/friction modifiers in ATF (typically a few wt %) cause measurable aminolysis of PI/PAI at 150 °C over thousands of hours is not established in the sources found.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that isolates the effect of specific ATF additive chemistries (ZDDP, sulfurised EP agents, succinimide dispersants, amine antioxidants, friction modifiers) on PI/PAI/polyester-imide enamels or PEEK.
- No peer-reviewed ESC data for PEI/PPSU (or other amorphous thermoplastics) in ATF/e-fluids were found. The only lead is a SABIC vendor statement seen in search snippets: ULTEM PEI resists stress cracking in "automotive and aircraft fluids", "unlike most other amorphous thermoplastics". The SABIC page failed to load (redirect loop), so this is unverified. A search-result summary of the ISO page says ISO 175:2010 excludes ESC (covered by the ISO 22088 series), but iso.org returned HTTP 403, so this is also unverified.
- Ester-based e-fluids vs Group III/PAO e-fluids: only the patent evidence above links ester-oil acids to enamel failure; no peer-reviewed comparison was found.

## Q3. Standardised immersion / compatibility test methods (titles, editions, conditions)

### Takeaway
Only one standard targets enamelled winding wire in oil: IEC 60851-4:2016 Test 20. It has a 24 h / 150 °C hydrolysis check (dry oil vs oil + 0.3 % water, visual and adherence only) and a 1 000 h oil-resistance test (sealed vessel with oil + paper, at the wire's class temperature capped at 150 °C, followed by breakdown voltage and crack checks). ASTM D1676-24 has an analogous "Resistance to Insulating Liquids and Hydrolytic Stability" section. Everything else is generic: plastics (ASTM D543-21 / ISO 175:2010), rubbers (ASTM D471-16a(2021) / ISO 1817), oil-side screening (ASTM D3455-11(2019)) and sealed-tube methods from the refrigeration world. All of these were written for transformer oil, IRM oils or refrigerants, not ATF/e-fluids. ATF compatibility work in the literature borrows ASTM D7216 (engine oil vs seal elastomers) or transmission-OEM elastomer specifications.

### Cited Findings
- **IEC 60851-4:2016 (Edition 3.0, 2016-07), "Winding wires – Test methods – Part 4: Chemical properties"** [standard; full text read via third-party copy]. It replaces ed. 2 (1996) + A1:1997 + A2:2005. Main changes: Test 16 (refrigerants) and Test 17 (solderability) revised, and new Annex A on alternatives to R 22; Test 20 was not revised. Contents: Test 12 resistance to solvents; Test 16 resistance to refrigerants; Test 17 solderability; Test 20 resistance to hydrolysis and to transformer oil — [IEC 60851-4:2016 text](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/143987489/IEC-60851-4-2016-en-fr.pdf)
  - Test 12 (solvents): applies to enamelled round wire > 0.250 mm and to rectangular wire. Precondition 10 min at 130 °C, immerse 30 min at 60 °C in standard solvent (60 % white spirit, 30 % xylene, 10 % butanol) "or solvent as agreed", then pencil-hardness test within 30 s.   - Test 20, general: hydrolysis resistance "is expressed by appearance and adherence after exposure of the specimens to transformer oil in the presence of water under pressure and at elevated temperature".   - Test 20 equipment: glass tubes 25 mm × 300 mm capable of being sealed; stainless-steel pressure vessel 400–500 ml rated 6 × 10⁶ Pa; transformer oil to IEC 60296; paper to IEC 60554-1 type 1 — [IEC 60851-4:2016 text](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/143987489/IEC-60851-4-2016-en-fr.pdf)
  - Round-wire hydrolysis (6.2.3.1): six straight pieces in the pressure vessel, de-aerated dry transformer oil filling (52.5 ± 2.5) % of vessel volume, (150 ± 3) °C for (24 ± 1) h, visual examination.   - Round-wire oil resistance (6.2.3.2): 10 twisted pairs (or straights), 3 mandrel-wound specimens plus extra wire. Volume fractions: transformer oil (65 ± 5) %, paper (4 ± 1) %, coating (0.275 ± 0.075) %, steel by agreement. Oil and paper are dried and de-aerated at 2 kPa for 16 h at 90 °C or 4 h at 105 °C. Hold at the wire's class temperature ±3 °C, or (150 ± 3) °C if the class is higher, for (1 000 ± 10) h. Five pairs are tested for breakdown at 105 °C in air as removed, five after drying 30 min at 125 °C; mandrel specimens are checked for cracks per IEC 60851-3 — [IEC 60851-4:2016 text](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/143987489/IEC-60851-4-2016-en-fr.pdf)
  - Rectangular-wire hydrolysis (6.3.3.1): two sealed glass tubes, each with five straight pieces in 80 ml de-aerated dry transformer oil, plus (0.24 ± 0.01) ml distilled water in one tube (≈0.3 vol %), 24 h at (150 ± 3) °C, visual check of appearance and adherence. Rectangular oil resistance (6.3.3.2): four U-shaped specimens and two mandrel-bent specimens plus extra wire (same Table 2 fractions), class temperature or 150 °C for (1 000 ± 10) h. Two U-shapes get breakdown testing at 105 °C as removed, two after drying 30 min at 125 °C; bends are checked for cracks per IEC 60851-3 clause 5.1.2 — [IEC 60851-4:2016 text](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/143987489/IEC-60851-4-2016-en-fr.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a hairpin study, IEC 60851-4 Test 20 is the closest "standard" anchor (sealed vessel, defined water dosing, 150 °C, dry and wet arms), but its fixed fluid is transformer oil. Substituting ATF or e-fluid, recording the fluid's water content by Karl Fischer, and extending to 1 000–2 000 h at 150 °C (as the Chinese studies do) would be a justifiable, documented deviation.
- ASTM D543 Practice B (exposure under standardised applied strain) is the only verified standard route found for strain-assisted chemical attack; the ISO 22088 ESC series exists but was not verified here. Strain matters for bent hairpins (the end-turn bend and twist region), so a strained specimen arm is worth considering.
- IEC TS 61857-42:2025 is the first IEC document aimed specifically at EIS in traction drivetrains. A publication should cite it as the system-level thermal-evaluation context, while noting (unless its text shows otherwise) that oil/ATF exposure is not part of its abstract-level scope.

### Gaps
- IEC 61857-1 current edition: a search-result summary indicates 2008 (ed. 3, replacing 2004), but the page was not opened.
- ISO 1817 edition: search-result summaries indicate ISO 1817:2024 (ed. 8, March 2024) cancelled ISO 1817:2022, and one summary suggested a 2026 revision (ISO/CD 1817 in development). iso.org returned HTTP 403, so this is unverified.

- Full texts of ASTM D1676-24 §186–195, ASTM D3455 and ASTM D5642 could not be opened (ASTM pages are product pages only), so their temperatures, durations and ratios are unverified here.
- ISO 175:2010 and ISO 1817 (2022/2024/possible 2026 edition) pages returned HTTP 403, so their current editions are not verified here.

## Q4. Independent (non-vendor) data for PEEK and PI in ATF/e-fluids versus vendor claims

### Takeaway
For PEEK, the only independent, peer-reviewed ATF immersion dataset found is the Oviedo study: bulk PEEK sheet in four ATFs at 100 °C for up to 672 h, with no water control. It shows essentially no change (tensile strength unchanged, volume −0.5 to −1 %, elongation +7 to −15 %). No independent data were found for PEEK-insulated magnet wire, PEEK tube or heat-shrink in ATF at the 150 °C+ temperatures and 1 000–3 000 h durations typical of e-drive validation. For polyimide (Kapton tape, PI enamel), no independent ATF/e-fluid immersion data were found. The evidence is indirect: vendor hydrolysis statements, a peer-reviewed hydrothermal study (imide hydrolysis by ATR-FTIR) and a neat-amine aminolysis study. Vendor claims for both are qualitative ("excellent chemical resistance to … ATF", "no interaction" with motor oil) and come without fluid, temperature or water-content details.

### Cited Findings
- **Independent PEEK data (peer-reviewed).** PEEK sheet (116 MPa, 15 % elongation) in four ATFs at 100 °C, 168–672 h: tensile strength unchanged; elongation +7 to −15 % at 168 h; volume 0 to −1 %; hardness about −3 Shore D; DSC melting enthalpy up from 31.60 to 45.89–76.16 J/g. Rated "high compatibility" — [García et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/records/10702226/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Structural%20Polym.pdf?download=1)
- **PEEK thermal reference (vendor datasheet).** Victrex XPI 174 (PEEK extrusion grade for wire), last updated 17 March 2026: Tg (onset) 143 °C (ISO 11357-2); Tm 343 °C (ISO 11357-3); water absorption at saturation, 23 °C, 0.45 % (ISO 62). Claim: "Excellent chemical resistance to a broad range of ATF, oils and cooling fluids", with no test conditions or retention data given — [Victrex XPI 174 datasheet](https://victrex.com/downloads/datasheets/victrex-xpi-174-polymer)
- **PEEK generic chemical resistance (vendor).** Unfilled PEEK bars immersed ≥ 7 days; "A – No interaction" for motor oil at 23, 100 and 200 °C, transformer oil (23/100 °C), petroleum oils, hydraulic and lubricating oils, and water up to 200 °C. ATF is not listed — [Victrex chemical resistance guide (2016)](https://www.victrex.com/-/media/downloads/technical-guides/victrex_chemical_resistance_en_us.pdf)
- **PEEK wire/secondary insulation in ATF (vendor data via trade press).** KetaSpire PEEK magnet wire with ProPreg E240 epoxy: adhesion "maintained" up to 1 500 h of ATF exposure, with a 150 °C / 2 000 h programme "being developed". Tests were done by Eltek International Laboratories. No ATF identity, water content or numbers were extractable — [Kunststoffe international 2/2022](https://res.cloudinary.com/sternwald-systems/raw/upload/v1/hugoprd/ARTIKEL_ATTACH/002E9ADD_C9FA4B214D64/d124670c4b1ba66a64ab0bfbd6a44033a79ea62c/PEEK%20and%20Epoxy%20Provide%20Efficient%20Insulation%20for%20High%20Voltage%20E-Motor%20-%20KUint%2002%202022.pdf)
- **PI: vendor hydrolysis statements.** Kapton's properties "are affected by water". The rate rises with temperature, water content and pressure. Alkali is particularly damaging (2 N NaOH, 10 min, 23 °C: 54 % elongation retention). No oil or ATF data are given — [DuPont Kapton Summary of Properties](https://www.electro-wind.com/web-files/DuPont\Literature\Kapton-summary-of-properties.pdf)
- **PI: independent hydrothermal evidence (repository record; abstract-level).** Braun, Nam, de la Mata, Harynuk, Chung and Dolez (2022), "Hydrothermal Aging of Polyimide Film" (DOI 10.7939/r3-j8ra-eh89): accelerated hydrothermal ageing of "a commercial polyimide film", tracking ultimate tensile strength, with degradation attributed to hydrolysis of the imide groups (chemometric ATR-FTIR). Conditions and numbers were not visible on the record page — [U Alberta repository record](https://ualberta.scholaris.ca/handle/123456789/17234)
- **PI: amine vulnerability (peer-reviewed, neat amines).** Kapton fully degraded in ethanolamine and ethylenediamine; 91 % diamine yield at 120 °C after 3 h — [Chen et al. 2025, Advanced Science](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11967771/)
- **Enamelled flat wire in wet ATF (independent lab, chemistry undisclosed).** One of two commercial enamelled flat wires (enamel about 0.08 mm) cracked and peeled after 1 500 h at 150 °C in ATF + 0.2 % water; the other kept about 80 % BDV at 2 000 h. This is the closest independent analogue to an enamelled hairpin test, but the enamel types are not disclosed — [Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)

### Inferences
- The independent PEEK evidence was gathered at 100 °C, below PEEK's Tg of about 143 °C. Oil-cooled hairpin windings typically see 150–180 °C hot-spots, above Tg, where oil diffusion, plasticisation and further crystallisation of an amorphous extruded PEEK layer could behave differently. This is a clear gap a new study could fill: dry vs wet ATF at, for example, 150 °C and 180 °C, with DSC crystallinity, mass uptake and adhesion to copper.
- Extruded PEEK wire and PEEK tube/heat-shrink are often deliberately low-crystallinity as processed. Measuring DSC crystallinity before and after ageing, separately from oil effects, would be needed to attribute changes (annealing vs oil) correctly. A dry-oven control at the same temperature and time is the obvious separating arm.
- For PI (Kapton tape, PI enamel), the combined indirect evidence predicts a water- and amine-sensitive material. An ATF ageing study with controlled water (0 / 0.2 / 0.5 %) would be novel and publishable, as no independent ATF data were found.

### Gaps
- No independent PEEK-in-ATF data above 100 °C, with water control, or on wire, tube or heat-shrink forms.
- No independent PI (Kapton/PI enamel) data in ATF or e-fluids.
- Vendor PEEK/PAI (KetaSpire/Torlon) ATF data, reportedly from the 2017 Solvay CTI programme, were not obtainable (Solvay PDF returned HTTP 404). Search snippets claim little or no tensile change after 1 500 h, but no page was opened that confirms it.
- IEEE 2024 thermal-ageing comparison of PEEK vs nanofilled-enamel wires (air ageing, electrical diagnostics; IEEE Xplore document 10462135) was not opened. It is in the electrical-ageing researcher's scope.
