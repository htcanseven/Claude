## 1. PD and breakdown in oil-wetted, sprayed, drained and vapour/bubble conditions, and the PD-free margin at 800, 1200 and 1600 V

### Takeaway
Oil raises PDIV only while it fills the gap and stays cool. A thin ATF film raised the AC PDIV of aramid insulation by 39% at 20 °C, but the gain disappeared near 100 °C and turned into an 8–9% loss at 120–150 °C. The hot wetted value was therefore 43% below the room-temperature wetted value. Conductive surface layers (−32%), Cu2S deposits (−22% to −43%), metal particles and bubbles lower PDIV or BDV further. These oil-state penalties are about as large as the 1.5× (1200 V) and 2× (1600 V) steps in voltage from 800 V. PD-free margins therefore have to be qualified hot, drained (thin film) and aged, not in fresh flooded oil at room temperature.

Sinusoidal voltage was chosen because it gives a conservative PDIV compared with PWM. — [Chen et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/CN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.03.009)

It was not retrievable (see Gaps). — [Chen et al. 2022, ref. 12](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/CN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.03.009)

**Particles vs bubbles in flowing oil (transformer-oil analogue for spray/flow with entrained gas or wear debris)**
- Citation: Zhao Yongjin, Zhang Yongze, Wang Gepeng, Guo Jiayuan, Shi Maomao, Tang Ju (XD Transformer; Chongqing University), "Discharge characteristics of flowing transformer oil containing impurities", *Insulating Materials* 2022, 55(9):40–46, DOI 10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.09.007 **[FT, Chinese]** — [Zhao et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.09.007)
- Oil flowed at 0.30 m/s and bubbles were sized with a 3.0 mm mesh. PDIV (1 kV/s ramp, first PD >100 pC) was **8.4 kV with metal particles vs 23.2 kV with bubbles**. BDV (2 kV/s, mean of 10) was **42.0 kV with particles vs 31.6 kV with bubbles**. The treated clean oil gave 75.1 kV in a standard oil cup, a different cell from the defect tests. In short, particles control PD inception and bubbles control breakdown. — [Zhao et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.09.007)
- Bubble PD clustered in the negative half-cycle (236°–305°) and excited only VHF signals. Particle PD spread over the whole cycle and excited UHF signals. — [Zhao et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.09.007)

**Conductive surface layer on enamel (relevant to conductive deposits on oil-wetted wire)**
- Citation: Liu Wei, Lin Xiyun, Ye Guoqing et al. ### Inferences
- In Chen et al., 0.02 mL spread over 40 × 40 mm is an average film of ~12.5 µm (calc.). Their "oil-coated" condition is therefore closer to a **drained or spray-wetted thin film** than to a flooded gap. This makes it the best available analogue for the "drained" state in the planned study.
- PDIV ratios from Chen et al. Table 2 (calc.), relative to dry PDIV at 20 °C (3.44 kV):

| State (0.25 mm aramid, AC) | PDIV (kV) | Ratio to dry 20 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Oil film, 20 °C | 4.80 | 1.40 |
| Oil film, 80 °C | 3.49 | 1.01 |
| Dry, 150 °C | 2.99 | 0.87 |
| Oil film, 120 °C | 2.86 | 0.83 |
| Oil film, 150 °C | 2.72 | 0.79 |

- If the same PD mechanism holds, the voltages produced by the converter scale linearly with V_DC (same topology, overshoot and rise time). A 1200 V or 1600 V machine therefore needs 1.5× or 2× the PDIV/RPDIV of an otherwise identical 800 V machine (calc.). The hot-oil penalty relative to a room-temperature wetted measurement (4.80/2.72 = **1.76×**) is larger than the 800 → 1200 V step (1.5×) and close to the 800 → 1600 V step (2×). A PD-free claim based on room-temperature measurements in oil could thus be wrong by more than the gain in bus voltage.
- Worked example (inference): suppose an 800 V design is "just PD-free" on dry PDIV at room temperature. To stay PD-free in the hot wetted state (×0.79), it needs ≥1.27× that PDIV at 800 V, ≥1.90× at 1200 V and ≥2.53× at 1600 V. If the conductive-surface (×0.68, Liu) or heavy-Cu2S (×0.57, Liao, Section 3) effects multiplied on top (untested; different materials and set-ups), the hot, contaminated PDIV would be only ~0.45–0.54× the dry room-temperature value.
- Ranking of states from most to least favourable for PD, assembled from different studies (inference): flooded and cool > wetted and cool > dry and cool > dry and hot > wetted and hot ≈ spray-wetted and hot > hot with bubbles, particles or conductive deposits. The **spray-cooled end winding at the hot spot** combines thin films, vapour/bubbles and deposits, so it is the most likely worst case at 1200–1600 V. This agrees with the spray results in the earlier reports.
- Where gaps really are flooded, PD is suppressed and the limit moves to breakdown and creepage. Zhao et al. show that bubbles lower BDV more than particles do. At 1600 V the BDV of oil-filled end-winding gaps with entrained gas, not PDIV, may therefore set the design limit (inference).
- For the planned study (suggestion): measure PDIV, and RPDIV if possible, on each insulation type in four wetting states (dry; drained film after a set drip time; flooded; drained and heated) at room temperature and at the ageing temperature, before and after oil ageing. Record oil water content (Karl Fischer) and drip time. Without these, PDIV values from different states cannot be compared across 800/1200/1600 V requirements.

### Gaps
- No openly readable study measured RPDIV of twisted pairs, hairpin pairs or formettes under repetitive impulses or PWM while immersed in, wetted with or sprayed with ATF/e-fluid at several temperatures with controlled water. Chen et al. used AC on aramid paper, not turn insulation. The group's 2020 turn-insulation-in-cooling-oil paper (Dong Han et al., *Insulating Materials* 53(11):38–44) is behind CNKI. Several URL patterns for it returned no PDF.
- The effect of water content in ATF on PDIV has not been measured; Chen et al. only propose bubble formation from trace water as a hypothesis.
- No PDIV/RPDIV data in oil at voltage levels relevant to 1200–1600 V buses were found.
- IEC 60034-18-41 stress-category values and enhancement factors could not be checked: the VDE preview PDF gave no extractable text and the EASA summary is paywalled. They should be taken from the standard (2014 + AMD1:2019) directly.
- The Kikuchi 2023 and Bologna preprint findings were seen only as search excerpts.

## 2. Electrical or electro-thermal ageing of winding insulation in the presence of oil

### Takeaway
No public study from 2015–2026 was found that applies AC, PWM/impulse or DC voltage to winding insulation while it is immersed in or sprayed with ATF/e-fluid and tracks its ageing. The available "electrical ageing with oil" evidence concerns the fluid itself (1000 breakdown arcs), energised copper circuit boards at 5 V (Section 3), or thermal ageing in oil followed by electrical tests. A combined voltage + temperature + oil arm would therefore be new, and Chen et al.'s ~100 °C crossover shows it must run at service temperature.

### Cited Findings
*Pointers only (already covered):* corona life of wire after an ATF cycle ([US 12,159,731 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12159731B2/en), patent data); current-heated motorettes in ATF and vapour ([Sun et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/CEIDP61745.2024.10907300)); thermal ageing in e-fluid then PDIV and dielectric spectroscopy ([HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf)).

**Electrical ageing of the fluid (EV thermal-fluid base oils)**
- Citation: Tormos B., Bermúdez V., Ruiz S., Alvis-Sanchez J. (CMT, Universitat Politècnica de València), "Degradation effects of base oils after thermal and electrical aging for EV thermal fluid applications", *Lubricants* 2023, 11(6):241, DOI 10.3390/lubricants11060241 **[FT; numerical results only in figures]** — [Tormos et al. 2023](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/lubricants/lubricants-11-00241/article_deploy/lubricants-11-00241.pdf)

Resistivity of Group III, diester and polyolester fell after electrical ageing, while PAO stayed stable. Thermal oxidation lowered resistivity in all oils. Even a small rise in water caused "a significant decrease in resistivity". — [Tormos et al. 2023](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/lubricants/lubricants-11-00241/article_deploy/lubricants-11-00241.pdf)

**Thermal ageing in oil followed by DC space-charge and PD tests (transformer analogue)**

(Chongqing University), "Space charge behavior in paper insulation induced by copper sulfide in HVDC power transformers", *Energies* 2015, 8(8):8110–8120, DOI 10.3390/en8088110 **[FT]**. **Breakdown morphology useful for post-test imaging**
- In enamelled twisted pairs, breakdown points were mostly *not* at the line where the two wires touch. They appeared as inverted cones with stepwise enamel thinning and were covered by loose carbon powder that can be wiped off to reveal a central carbonised channel. — [Liu et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/CN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.06.017) [FT]

### Inferences
- Electrical ageing in oil probably has two regimes (inference). (a) Below PDIV (flooded, cool): ionic conduction and electrochemistry dominate, so ageing shows up as leakage current, deposits and electrochemical migration (ECM) at exposed copper (Section 3), not PD erosion. (b) Above PDIV (hot, drained film, bubbles): PD erosion as in air, possibly made worse by hydrocarbon vapour (see the earlier spray results). Chen et al.'s crossover near 100 °C suggests a test at 150 °C sits in regime (b) for thin films, while a test at room temperature in flooded oil would show little electrical ageing.
- Tormos et al. show that discharges in ester oils raise water and lower BDV and resistivity. In a sealed ageing vessel with PD or arcing, the fluid's own properties would therefore drift during the test (inference; Tormos used breakdown arcs, not PD). Water and resistivity of the oil should be measured before and after (suggestion).
- Minimal electro-thermal arm for the planned study (suggestion): put pairs of specimens with cut ends in the oil vessel under a fixed DC potential difference, with both polarities, scaled to phase-to-phase stress at 800/1200/1600 V. Add a sinusoidal or impulse arm below dry PDIV, and run unenergised controls in the same vessel. Log leakage current continuously, and photograph breakdown or deposit sites using the cone-and-carbon morphology above to tell PD/breakdown damage from chemical attack.

### Gaps
- No voltage-endurance (life vs voltage) curves, PWM/impulse ageing or DC-bias ageing of magnet wire or hairpins in ATF/e-fluid were found in open sources. Candidates may sit in CNKI (*Insulating Materials*, *High Voltage Engineering*), IEEJ, SAE or recent CEIDP/EIC full texts, none of which could be accessed.
- No study separates PD erosion from oil-chemical attack under combined stress. No data for taped, glass-fibre or film + glass insulations under electrical stress in oil.

## 3. Effect of voltage, polarity and field on copper corrosion, conductive deposits, dendrites and ECM in e-fluids, and what larger potential differences mean for cut ends, stripped zones and welds

### Takeaway
All publicly described energised e-fluid tests use low voltage. Lubrizol's conductive-deposit patent runs at 5 V; ASTM D8544 applies a "monitored voltage" left to "industry need"; a Tannas/Lubrizol patent lists 100–1000 V as an option but reports no data at it. The dependence of deposits and dendrites on voltage and polarity in e-fluids is therefore unquantified. Separately, wire and transformer studies show that once conductive products form, they lower PDIV by ~22–43% and raise PD magnitude. Both effects matter more as potential differences grow at 1200–1600 V.

### Cited Findings
*Pointer (already covered):* Infineum's energised copper-grid board with a capillary cover gap and dendrite video ([Infineum, CTI 2025](https://cti-symposium.world/leading-the-way-in-e-fluid-developments/)); Cu2S raising the oil-gap field ([Cong et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/TDEI.2024.3466113)).

- Conductive surface on enamel: PDIV 975 → 665 V (Section 1). — [Liu et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/CN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.06.017)

### Inferences
- **Field gap between tests and machines (calc.):** 5 V across 0.1 mm is 0.05 kV/mm. 800 V across 1 mm is 0.8 kV/mm (16×) and 1600 V across 1 mm is 1.6 kV/mm (32×). Even the Tannas 100–1000 V option stops below the potentials of a 1200–1600 V bus. No published deposit or dendrite test covers 1200–1600 V-class potential differences.
- **Bridging power (calc.):** for a given deposit resistance R between phases, P = V²/R. At 1200 V the heating is 2.25× and at 1600 V 4× that at 800 V. This strengthens Hunt's failure path in which a deposit bridges phases, heats and decomposes (inference).
- **Polarity:** if Afton's description holds, dendrite and ECM growth needs a sustained net drift of Cu ions towards the cathode. Symmetric bipolar PWM between phases should give less net drift than a **DC component** (inference). DC components can arise from DC-link-referenced potentials or rail insulation faults, and they are what makes DC-bias tests representative. With an isolated HV system, an insulation fault on one rail can leave windings at up to the full V_DC relative to chassis (inference; not verified in sources).
- **Zones on hairpins (inference):** (a) weld and connection-side areas of different phases are bare copper facing bare copper across oil or vapour gaps. This is the geometry the CDT/SIR boards imitate, and both Savant and Tannas show failures can occur in the **vapour only**, so weld zones above the oil level or in the headspace need separate attention. (b) At stripped zones and cut ends, a conductive deposit or film on the insulation surface next to the bare copper extends the electrode. Following Liu et al. and Liao et al., this would lower PDIV and raise PD magnitude at the insulation edge, the oil–insulation–copper triple point. (c) Under taped or glass-wrapped conductors, crevice Cu2S (earlier report) becomes electrically active through Liao's space-charge mechanism if any DC component is present.
- For the planned study (suggestion): energise some specimens so that cut ends of adjacent specimens face each other at a DC potential difference in both polarities. Include an ASTM D8544-type witness board or a bare interdigitated copper comb in the same vessel. Photograph anode and cathode ends separately, since dissolution should appear at the anode and deposits/dendrites at the cathode. Log leakage current. Run ICP on Cu and S in the oil before and after.

### Gaps
- The ASTM D8544 voltage, board geometry and pass limits are not public; the store page and Savant give no values.
- No public data show how deposit or dendrite rate scales with voltage (5 V vs 100–1000 V), gap or polarity, or how AC/PWM compares with DC, in e-fluids. Infineum and Afton have not published their test conditions or results.
- No study was found on energised corrosion of welded or stripped hairpins, or under tape or glass wraps, in oil.
- No study was found showing that an electric field accelerates Cu2S formation in EV fluids. Transformer work found covers effects of Cu2S under DC (space charge), not field-driven formation.

## 4. Dielectric properties of fresh and aged e-fluids/ATF relevant to high-voltage design

### Takeaway
ATF and e-fluids are weakly conducting insulators. Legacy ATFs measured 0.9–8 × 10⁻⁹ S/cm at 100 °C (≈1.3–11 MΩ·m). An Infineum e-fluid patent targets ≥44 MΩ·m at 80 °C. A commercial e-fluid measured 1.9 × 10¹⁰ Ω·cm (1.9 × 10⁸ Ω·m) at room temperature. Conductivity rises with polar additives, oxidation, water and temperature. As a result, hot ATF relaxes charge in ~20–200 µs, so under DC and low-frequency stress an oil-wetted surface acts more like a resistive electrode extension than an insulator. No openly published BDV or permittivity values of commercial e-fluids versus water and temperature were found.

### Inferences
- **Unit conversions (calc.):** 0.9–8 × 10⁻⁹ S/cm = 0.09–0.8 µS/m, i.e. ρ ≈ 1.1 × 10⁷ to 1.25 × 10⁶ Ω·m at 100 °C (legacy ATFs). Infineum's ≥44 MΩ·m at 80 °C is 4.4 × 10⁷ Ω·m. Sanyo's commercial e-fluid is 1.9 × 10⁸ Ω·m at room temperature. These are different fluids and methods, so no temperature coefficient can be derived. Taken together, they are consistent with resistivity falling by roughly one or more orders of magnitude from room temperature to 80–100 °C, and with newer e-fluids being formulated more insulating than legacy ATFs (inference).
- **Charge-relaxation time (calc., εr ≈ 2.1):** τ = ε0·εr·ρ ≈ 23 µs (1.25 × 10⁶ Ω·m), 0.21 ms (1.1 × 10⁷), 0.82 ms (4.4 × 10⁷) and 3.5 ms (1.9 × 10⁸ Ω·m). The matching crossover frequencies 1/(2πτ) are ≈ 6.9 kHz, 770 Hz, 195 Hz and 45 Hz. Hot ATF therefore behaves capacitively only for fast components such as switching edges and carrier harmonics above ~10 kHz. For DC, slow common-mode components and possibly the electrical fundamental, it behaves resistively (inference).
- **Field distribution consequence (inference):** for fast PWM edges, oil-filled gaps share the voltage by permittivity. Oil at εr ≈ 2.1 is close to aramid at 2.5–2.7, so oil filling greatly relieves the gap compared with air (εr = 1). Under DC or slow stress, the oil is many orders of magnitude more conductive than polymer insulation. The oil gap then carries almost no field, the solid layers carry nearly all the voltage, and an oil-wetted surface extends the potential of the bare copper along the insulation. Field then concentrates at insulation cut-backs (stripped zones, cut ends, weld transitions). This is consistent with the conductive-surface PDIV drop of Liu et al. and makes creepage at these edges more critical at 1200–1600 V.
- **Water and ageing:** water (Tormos; earlier Newcomb), oxidation (McFadden) and polar S/P additives (Sanyo) all lower resistivity. A fluid that is safely insulating when fresh can therefore become conductive enough in service to raise leakage current, feed ECM and cut relaxation times further. The planned study should measure resistivity (IEC 60247 or ASTM D1169) and Karl Fischer water of the oil before and after ageing (suggestion).
- **Ester content trade-off (inference):** ester base stocks help magnet-wire compatibility (earlier Afton patent data) but have lower resistivity and higher losses (Tormos). Higher bus voltages make this trade-off sharper.

### Gaps
- No open BDV values for commercial e-fluids or ATFs as a function of temperature or water were found; Farfán-Cabrera gives no numbers in the abstract and Tormos shows values only in figures.
- No permittivity or dielectric-loss spectra versus temperature and frequency for ATFs or e-fluids were found, beyond the εr ≈ 2.1 model value in the earlier report.
- No creepage or surface-flashover data along ATF-wetted enamel or hairpin surfaces under 50 Hz, PWM or DC, and no guidance on creepage distances for oil-wetted hairpin connections at 1200–1600 V.
- Resistivity of service-aged e-fluids and OEM limits are not public.
