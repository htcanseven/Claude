# Horizon Europe Cluster 5 Work Programme 2026–2027 — Detailed Analysis

**Document analysed:** `wp-8-climate-energy-and-mobility_horizon-2026-2027_en.pdf` — Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, Part 8 "Climate, Energy and Mobility", European Commission Decision **C(2025) 8493 of 11 December 2025**, 339 pages, 94 topics.
**Analysed for:** Canseven (LUT) × Sarma (Durham) — PMSG/PMSM design, cooling, fault-tolerant drives × condition monitoring, FBG sensing, diagnostics.
**Date:** 27 July 2026. All quotations below are verbatim from the work programme text.

---

## 1. Headline conclusions

1. **The wind topic (D3-03, deadline 15 Sep 2026) is not realistically enterable as a core partner now — but its cascade-funding mechanism is designed almost exactly for a team like yours**, and the work programme says a single entity may win **more than one** such grant. This is the single most important finding in the document.
2. **A better-sized topic exists that the first-pass research did not surface: HORIZON-CL5-2027-03-D5-03** (e-mobility circularity, 2ZERO). It names "**rotors, stators of electric motors**" and "**power electronic converters**" explicitly, asks for "**predictive maintenance**" and monitoring of "**fatigue and thermal stress**" to extend component lifespan — and funds **4 projects at €5–7M each**, not one mega-project. Deadline **14 April 2027**. For your combined profile this is arguably the best direct fit in the entire work programme.
3. **The ocean energy topic (D3-09) contains a written invitation to import wind-sector monitoring expertise**: "Apply recent advances in condition and structural health monitoring from other sectors to ocean energy." Deadline 31 Mar 2027, but it is a single €43M project — consortium-joining only.
4. The work programme explicitly points proposers to **RISEnergy** for research-infrastructure access in both the wind and grid topics, confirming that route is aligned with Commission expectations.
5. Wind priorities in this work programme are set by **ETIP Wind's SRIA 2025-2027** and **EERA's "NeWindEERA" programme (Sept 2024)** — these are the documents to write against, and EERA Joint Programme Wind is a network worth joining.

## 2. The wind topic in full: HORIZON-CL5-2026-09-D3-03

*"Innovative technologies and solutions to improve wind energy systems supporting the Strategic Energy Technology (SET) Plan on wind"* — Call: BATTERIES and ENERGY. **Opens 5 May 2026 → deadline 15 September 2026.**

### Hard parameters (verbatim)
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| EU contribution | "around EUR 93.00 million" — **one project** |
| Type of action | Innovation Actions |
| TRL | "expected to achieve TRL 6-8 by the end of the project… may start at any TRL" |
| Page limit | **increased to 60 pages** "due to the complexity of the topic" |
| FSTP | **Mandatory** — "Beneficiaries must provide financial support to third parties (FSTP)… maximum amount to be granted to each third party is EUR 1 000 000" |
| Satellite data | If used, Copernicus and/or Galileo/EGNOS must be used |

### The nine priority R&I areas — and where you fit

The topic derives its priorities from the SET Plan Implementation Working Group on wind, ETIP Wind and EERA. Areas **a**, **b**, **d**, **e** and **f** touch your work directly:

- **(a) Industrialisation, scale-up and competitiveness** — includes "**design for reliable and lasting products**" → design-for-reliability of PM generators (Hüseyin).
- **(b) Optimisation and further digitalisation of operation and maintenance** — "integration of artificial intelligence (AI), robotics, advanced repair methodologies and **new sensor technologies**, interoperability among wind farm sub-systems…" → **this is the bullseye**: FBG sensing (Nur) + ML diagnostics (both) + converter-signal monitoring.
- **(d) Sustainability and circularity** — "material substitution for decarbonisation, material reduction, recycling methods, **lifetime extension via re-using, refurbishing and re-purposing**" → generator lifetime extension; also relevant to rare-earth reduction in PM machines.
- **(e) Floating offshore wind** — "Development of optimised floating wind energy systems through the **integrated design of the different elements of the system, including the floater and the generation part**" → the phrase "the generation part" is the generator: co-design of the electrical machine with the floating platform is explicitly in scope.
- **(f) Research and technology infrastructures for wind energy** — "upgrade of existing and establishment of new infrastructures and test centres… An area of particular interest… large-scale testing facilities for offshore floating wind energy systems" → relevant given LUT's and Durham's lab assets, and the nearby The Switch (18 MW) and ORE Catapult (15 MW) benches.

Areas c (system integration), g (climate/wake/geophysics), h (environmental/social) and i (emerging concepts, e.g. multirotor, ducted turbines) are outside your core.

### Structure — why the core consortium is out of reach, and the FSTP route is not

The project is split into three "challenges":

| Challenge | Who can do it | Budget notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Breakthrough** | Core consortium **and third parties** | **Up to €8M** reserved for FSTP calls; "The first FSTP call for the breakthrough challenge should be published in the first year of the project" |
| **Validation** | Core consortium **and third parties** | **Up to €16M** for "consecutive FSTP calls"; validates technologies "developed in the project **but also developed previously and separately in other European and national projects**" |
| **Demonstration** | **Core consortium only** — "solely be implemented by the core consortium" | Max **€50M**; ≥3 distinct demonstrations, each running "for a continuous period of at least 6 months"; go/no-go milestones requiring permits, engineering plans, business plan and financial close |

The demonstration challenge requires permits, financial close and industrial deployment capability — which is why the core consortium will be led by industry and large RTOs, and why entering it now, seven weeks before the deadline, is unrealistic.

**But the FSTP design is unusually favourable to you.** Verbatim from the work programme:

> "A share of the EU funding will be implemented through financial support to third parties and used to support **research organizations, academia**, start-ups, scale-ups, SME's, industries and other entities and to enable use-cases that are not yet identified in the proposal stage."

> "The FSTP breakthrough challenge should aim at **attracting innovative entities that does not necessarily have the capacity to apply directly for EU grants**."

> "…the maximum amount to be granted to each party needs to be sufficiently high to cater for cases where **a given legal entity may receive more than one FSTP grant**."

> "The consortium should consider specifically elements within the FSTP schemes to address **broad EU geographical balance**."

Practical reading for you:
- Grants of **up to €1M**, projects of **12–18 months**, open to universities, awarded through open competitive calls run by the winning consortium.
- The **validation challenge explicitly accepts technology developed in earlier national projects** — so work funded by the Research Council of Finland or EPSRC between now and 2028 can be matured through an FSTP grant. That is a direct pipeline from the national grants recommended in the main report into EU money.
- **Multiple grants to the same entity are anticipated**, so a first small win can be built on.
- Geographical balance is a stated selection consideration — a Finland–UK pairing is not a disadvantage.
- Timing: the winning project starts around mid/late 2027; **the first breakthrough FSTP call lands in its first year, so expect the first opportunity roughly late 2027–2028**, with validation calls later.

**Action:** identify the winning consortium when results are announced (expect early–mid 2027), get on its mailing list, and be ready to bid into the first FSTP call. Meanwhile write the ETIP Wind SRIA language into your national proposals so the eventual FSTP application reads as continuous work.

## 3. The topic the first pass missed: HORIZON-CL5-2027-03-D5-03

*"Data-driven circular economy for e-mobility ecosystem (2ZERO Partnership)"* — Call: MOBILITY. **Opens 15 Dec 2026 → deadline 14 April 2027.** Innovation Action. **Total €24M, €5–7M per project, 4 projects expected to be funded.**

Why this matters: the funded project size (€5–7M) and the number of grants (4) make it far more accessible than the single €93M wind project, and the scope text names your subject matter directly. Verbatim, Scope area A:

> "**Circular economy for Electric Vehicle (EV) powertrains and infrastructure**: Improving the circularity of EVs drivetrain components (such as **power electronic converters, rotors, stators of electric motors**, gear assemblies and battery components excluding battery cells)… Through durable design, **predictive maintenance**, refurbishment, etc, it is possible to **extend the lifespan of critical components** reducing material demand and waste, without jeopardising on the needed performance."

and further:

> "…**advanced monitoring** […] fatigue, and thermal stress to optimise circularity strategies and extend the lifespan of components."
> "Develop novel design concepts and processes to optimise material usage and extend components' lifetime such as design for (automated) dismantling, **in particular to reduce dependency on critical raw materials** or enable use of alternatives."

The fit is almost uncomfortably good: monitoring of thermal stress and fatigue in stators (Nur's FBG sensing measures exactly strain and temperature in windings), design for lifetime extension and reduced rare-earth dependency (Hüseyin's machine design, including ferrite/low-rare-earth work in the LUT group), and predictive maintenance of converters and machines (your joint ML diagnostics).

**Caveat:** it is an Innovation Action in the 2ZERO partnership, so an automotive-industry-led consortium is expected, and the framing is circularity rather than wind. Your route in is as the monitoring-and-lifetime work package within a consortium built around an OEM/Tier-1. This diversifies you out of wind without leaving your science — and the deadline (14 Apr 2027) gives a realistic 8 months to find a consortium.

## 5. Strategic documents and networks named in the work programme

The wind topic states its priorities are "as defined by the Implementation Working Group (IWG) on wind energy, the European Technology and Innovation Platform on wind energy (**ETIP Wind**) and the European Energy Research Alliance (**EERA**) joint programme on wind energy", citing:

- **ETIP Wind Strategic Research & Innovation Agenda 2025-2027** (November 2023)
- **NeWindEERA – A New Research Programme for the European Wind Energy Sector** (September 2024)

Two consequences. First, these are the documents to mine for language and priorities when writing any wind proposal — Commission evaluators recognise their vocabulary. Second, **EERA Joint Programme Wind membership is a concrete, low-cost networking action** for LUT (which is not yet an EAWE member either, per the main report) and a way into the community that will form the D3-03 core consortium and later run its FSTP calls.

The work programme also encourages, in the wind topic and again in the grid topics, use of "services offered by European research infrastructures… particularly **RISEnergy**", and for ocean energy "the **Marinerg-i** distributed infrastructure specialising in sea applications". This confirms the transnational-access route recommended in the main report is exactly what the Commission expects proposers to use.

## 6. What this changes in the plan

Adding to the main report's roadmap:

1. **Sept 2026 – unchanged priority:** the MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowship (9 Sep) remains the highest-value near-term action. Do not divert effort to the 15 Sep wind topic — the core consortium is beyond reach at this notice.
2. **New target — D5-03 (deadline 14 Apr 2027):** begin consortium-hunting now for the e-mobility circularity topic. Route in: 2ZERO partnership channels, an automotive OEM/Tier-1, or an existing EV-circularity consortium needing a monitoring/lifetime work package. This is the most realistically winnable Cluster 5 topic for your specific pair.
3. **New pipeline — D3-03 cascade funding (~late 2027 onward):** track the winning consortium from early 2027; design the national-grant work (RCF/EPSRC) so its outputs are "previously developed" technology ready for the validation challenge, which the work programme explicitly welcomes.
4. **Networks:** read the ETIP Wind SRIA 2025-2027 and NeWindEERA; pursue EERA JP Wind participation; keep RISEnergy access applications running (calls roughly every 6 months to Sept 2027).
5. **2027 fallbacks:** D3-09 (ocean energy, condition monitoring explicitly invited) and D3-25/D3-26 (grid observability, Durham-led) as consortium-joining opportunities.

## 7. Caveats

- 2027 topic details (budgets, dates, scope) can still change through work programme amendments until each call formally opens — re-verify on the Funding & Tenders Portal before committing.
- Budgets quoted are "expected EU contribution"; the work programme notes throughout that this "does not preclude submission and selection of a proposal requesting different amounts".
- The FSTP timing for D3-03 ("first year of the project") is inferred to mean roughly late 2027–2028 based on a typical grant-signature timeline; the exact date depends on when the winning project starts and is not stated in the work programme.
- This analysis covers Cluster 5 only. Clean Aviation, Clean Hydrogen and other Joint Undertakings publish separate work programmes.
