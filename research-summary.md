## Core research area

Permanent magnet synchronous machines (PMSMs) at high power density — the electromagnetic design,
the losses that limit it, the cooling that enables it, and the faults that threaten it.

## Five active threads

### 1. High-specific-power PMSM design and loss physics
The dissertation core. Analytical and FEA modelling of the effects that dominate as machines are
pushed toward higher specific power.

- Eddy-current losses in slitted rotor cores of PMSMs — a novel method (*IEEE TEC*, 2024)
- Impact of stator core magnetic asymmetry on a high-specific-power PMSM (*IEEE TIA*, 2024)
- Extending the Carter factor to slot openings with semimagnetic slot wedges (*IEEE TEC*, 2025)

### 2. Thermal management and direct cooling
The fastest-growing thread. Direct liquid/oil cooling of windings as the enabler of compact,
high-power-density machines.

- Advanced direct cooling arrangements for machines with hairpin windings (*IEEE Access*, 2025)
- Direct liquid cooling in compact machines: advantages and limitations
  (*Applied Thermal Engineering*, 2025) — most-cited recent work
- Hollow hairpin winding with direct oil cooling (*IEEE TIA*, 2026)
- Flow-directed oil cooling of an outer-rotor PMSM for electrohydraulic applications
  (*Applied Thermal Engineering*, 2026)

### 3. Test systems, experimental validation and drive-level integration
Building the rigs and converter-side analysis needed to validate MW-class, high-fundamental-frequency
machines.

- Alternative test system for high-specific-power PMSMs using passive rectification and compensating
  capacitors (*ICEM*, 2024) → experimental validation (*IEEE TIA*, 2026)
- System-level switching-frequency trade-offs in a high-fundamental-frequency MW-class PMSM drive
  (*IEEE Access*, 2026; with F. Blaabjerg)

### 4. Fault diagnosis and condition monitoring with machine learning
The clearest new direction — and the one that has moved beyond electrical machines into robotics and
autonomous systems.

- Fault detection and diagnosis for multi-faults of PMSM-drive systems using a hybrid ML method
  (*IET Power Electronics*, 2026)
- Advanced detection of PM faults in offshore wind turbine generators using FEA and deep transfer
  learning (*Machines*, 2026)
- Demagnetization in PMSMs: modelling, diagnosis and future directions — review, in Turkish
  (*Bilecik Şeyh Edebali Üniv. Fen Bilimleri Dergisi*, 2026)
- Event-driven short-circuit fault classification in SCIG with confidence-weighted aggregation
  (*ECAI*, 2026)
- Onset-aware alarm-triggered fault diagnosis in collaborative robots (*IEEE Access*, 2026)
- Operational fault diagnosis of autonomous underwater vehicles via a hybrid descriptor-temporal
  stacking framework (*Applied Ocean Research*, 2026)

### 5. Short-circuit transients and electro-mechanical coupling
Faults treated as a multiphysics problem rather than a purely electrical one.

- Comparative analysis of three-phase short-circuit dynamic behaviour in interior vs. surface magnet
  PMSMs (*IEEE Trans. Magnetics*, 2026)
- Transient torsional rotordynamic analysis in the case of short circuit in PMSMs
  (*Int. Conf. on Rotor Dynamics*, 2026; with J. Sopanen's rotordynamics group)

## Trajectory

The through-line from the PhD to now is a widening of scope around the same machine:

**machine physics** (losses, geometry, asymmetry) → **thermal limits** (direct/hollow-conductor
cooling) → **system level** (converter, switching frequency, test rigs) → **reliability**
(faults, diagnosis, drivetrain coupling).

Two shifts stand out in 2025–2026 work:

1. **From design to diagnosis.** Data-driven and deep-learning fault detection is now the largest
   single cluster of output, and it generalises beyond PMSMs — induction generators, cobots, AUVs.
2. **From component to system.** Recent papers reach outward into the converter, the drivetrain
   (torsional rotordynamics), and the application (offshore wind, electrohydraulics).
