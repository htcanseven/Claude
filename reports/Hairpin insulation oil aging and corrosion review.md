# Oil Aging and Corrosion of Hairpin Winding Insulation: Literature Review

A similar study exists only in part: insulated wires have been aged in oil and re-measured electrically, but the planned study as a whole has not been published. The closest examples are the studies by [Tre Tau](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/DEIS-magazine-MayJune2024.pdf) (2024), the [Bologna HEFT project](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf) (2024), [Guilin CEMT](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008) (2024), [Castrol](https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202212/6I70323978C501.shtml) (2022, manufacturer claim) and [RWTH Aachen IEM](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf) (2022–2024). They use straight wire, twisted pairs or back-to-back rectangular conductors, and compare at most two conductor insulation technologies. No study was found that compares enamel, extruded PEEK, PEEK tube, PEEK heat-shrink, Kapton tape, epoxy powder and parylene on formed hairpins under one protocol with calibrated photography. This conclusion was reached without searching Scopus, IEEE Xplore or Web of Science; a final database check is needed before submission.

## Electrical aging in oil: findings

After oil aging, PDIV and capacitance change little; the damage shows up in breakdown voltage, tan δ spectra and adhesion. Water, sulfur-containing ATF additives and conductive particles accelerate degradation.

### PDIV rises or falls depending on the state of the oil

A fresh ATF film raised the PDIV and RPDIV of twisted pairs about threefold ([Yang et al. 2022, AEE](https://journals.pan.pl//Content/124108/PDF/art16_i.pdf)). In wetted specimens the failure mode shifted from PD to breakdown; according to the authors, the limiting quantity for a wetted winding is BDV, not RPDIV. The oil displaces the air in the narrowest gap and reduces field intensification (εr ≈ 2.1 in the model) ([Yang et al. 2023](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2023LYPreliminary.pdf)).

In the opposite direction, ATF sprayed at 60 °C onto a 180 °C conductor lowered PDIV by about 10–15% ([Martinez et al. 2025](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/c037e7e3-7f76-4690-a5cf-b13fdf36a5ab/unpaywall-bitstream-1951351576.pdf)). The authors attribute the drop to hydrocarbons in the vapor enhancing ionization; the body of the paper leaves the magnitude unclear. The effect matters for phase-to-phase gaps in the end winding.

Conductive particles lowered PDIV the most. Copper particles of 5 µm at 1.2 g/L reduced the Weibull PDIV from 3677 V to 2364 V (a 36% drop) ([Yang et al. 2022, AEE](https://journals.pan.pl//Content/124108/PDF/art16_i.pdf)). RPDIV fell from 6608 V to 3578 V (a 46% drop).

The same study found 10–100 nm particles and 186 mg/kg of iron in an e-motorcycle oil after 10,000 km.

In specimens that were drained and cleaned with n-heptane, PDIV changed little. After 1008 h at 180 °C, PDIV was the same in the oil and air groups and dropped slightly ([Yang et al. 2022, ICEM](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)). After 2 weeks in Castrol ATF at 160 °C, PAI showed no significant PDIV drop at 20, 180 or 210 °C ([HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf)).

### Breakdown voltage, tan δ and adhesion are more sensitive

PDIV and capacitance cannot detect oil penetration that degrades mechanical properties ([Yang PhD thesis, RWTH](https://www.iem.rwth-aachen.de/cms/iem/forschung/publikationen/publikationen1/~povh/details/?file=981658&lidx=1), abstract only). RWTH notes that oil-compatibility testing has no recognized end-of-life criterion and needs a multi-method assessment ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)). A study relying only on PDIV and capacitance risks reporting "no effect" (inference).

Breakdown voltage, by contrast, drops markedly. After 2000 h in Shell ATF at 150 °C, BDV retention was 30% for PAI and ~50% for PFA ([Zeynalova et al. 2024](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/DEIS-magazine-MayJune2024.pdf)). The authors manufacture the PFA wire, and no independent replication confirms the result.

In wet ATF (0.2% water, 150 °C), flat wire C fell from 12.8 kV to 2.4 kV at 1000 h and cracked at 1500 h ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)). Wire D fell from 13.1 kV to 10.5 kV at 2000 h. The enamel chemistry of the two wires was not disclosed.

Patents show a similar spread. In the Afton protocol (150 °C, 5 days, ~1500 ppm water), PEsI/PAI twisted pairs gave 4.8–15.2 kV depending on the fluid, and polyester gave <1 kV ([US 12,227,716 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12227716B2/en), patent data). After a CEEIA-type ATF cycle, a commercial corona-resistant composite wire dropped to 5.53 kV and a corona life of 3 h 18 min ([US 12,159,731 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12159731B2/en), patent data).

In the same patent, a single-layer corona-resistant PAI kept 10.82 kV. Its corona life, however, fell from 109.6 h to 59.2 h, a reduction of about 46% ([US 12,159,731 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12159731B2/en), patent data).

Dielectric spectroscopy captured changes that PDIV missed. New loss peaks appeared at ~10 Hz in the G720E resin and at a few hundred kHz in XQ8763 ([HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf)). For XQ8763, the loss increase at 10 kHz was flagged as a risk of overheating and, in the worst case, thermal runaway.

In a water-based lubricant, the εr of PEI/PAI rose by ~20% after 1008 h at 90 °C, while PDIV changed little ([Yang et al. 2023](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2023LYPreliminary.pdf)). In the same study, the phase-to-phase IR of a motorette (1000 V DC, R60s) fell from >260 GΩ dry to 70 GΩ with the end winding wetted and to 12.5 GΩ with the whole winding wetted. After 48 h of full wetting, ~0.9 MΩ was measured, below the 5 MΩ minimum that the paper cites from IEC 60034-27-4.

Because ATF is conductive compared with air, IR can be unexpectedly low even in a healthy system ([Martinez et al. 2025](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/c037e7e3-7f76-4690-a5cf-b13fdf36a5ab/unpaywall-bitstream-1951351576.pdf)). Used ATF is more conductive than fresh ATF; 2000 ppm of water raised the conductivity, which returned to normal when the water was driven off by heating ([Newcomb 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2023.1139385/full)).

### Water and additive chemistry accelerate degradation

Water in the oil is a first-order variable. In Castrol's CEEIA 415-2019 results, BDV retention fell step by step as water rose from 0% to 0.2% and 0.5% ([Castrol/Gasgoo 2022](https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202212/6I70323978C501.shtml), manufacturer claim). Enamel aged in ATF looked darkened, slightly cracked and porous; the author attributes this to hydrolysis.

In a 384 h screening, the breakdown retention of wire C at 0%, 0.2% and 0.5% water was 95.5%, 88.5% and 52.3%, respectively ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)). The corresponding values for wire D were 98.6%, 93.1% and 63.2%. Afton also adds ~1500 ppm of water to the oil to make its test more severe ([US 12,227,716 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12227716B2/en), patent data).

In Proterial's test at 120 °C with 0.4 vol% water, polyester (PEW) degraded fastest, followed by IMW ([Proterial wire guide](https://www.proterial.com/products/electrical_wire/pdf/magnet_wire.pdf), manufacturer claim). AIW and KMK-22A were much more durable. The same guide states that oil resistance hardly differs between enamel types.

The additive package appears to matter more than the base oil. XPS found 0.4–0.5 at% sulfur in the enamel only in the ATF group, and ATF embrittled PEI/PAI earlier than the e-fluids did (772 h versus 1008 h) ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)). Afton also reports that the additive package makes a marked difference to wire performance ([Kwak et al. 2023](https://saemobilus.sae.org/articles/magnet-wire-compatibility-test-electric-drivetrain-fluid-development-2023-32-0145), abstract only).

In the Afton patent, fluids with a detergent-metal (ppm) to ester-content (mol%) ratio of ~70 or below gave ≥10 kV ([US 12,227,716 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12227716B2/en), patent data). When the ester share of the base oil rose from 10% to 80%, BDV rose from 9.3 kV to 15.2 kV.

### Temperature and recovery are little studied

Oil-aging temperatures range from 90 °C ([Yang et al. 2023](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2023LYPreliminary.pdf)) to 180 °C ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)). No oil-aging data at several temperatures (Arrhenius) were found. HEFT stayed at 160 °C because the oil's fire point is 180 °C ([HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf)).

The measurement temperature also changes the result. Dry hot BDV fell by 13% for PAI and 10% for PFA from room temperature to 240 °C ([Zeynalova et al. 2024](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/DEIS-magazine-MayJune2024.pdf)). [HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf) measured PDIV at 20, 180 and 210 °C.

A dry control at the same temperature is essential to separate thermal effects from oil effects. In air at 180 °C, surface oxygen rose by 25.5% in 336 h, and the oil initially suppressed this oxidation ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)). In HEFT, conductors kept in air at 220 °C for one week also delaminated extensively ([HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf)).

No study compared the in-oil, drained and re-dried states of the same specimen. Protocols measure after draining, n-heptane washing and drying ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf); [US 12,227,716 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12227716B2/en)). IEC 60851-4 Test 20 measures BDV as removed and after drying for 30 min at 125 °C, which separates absorption from permanent hydrolysis ([IEC 60851-4:2016 text](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/143987489/IEC-60851-4-2016-en-fr.pdf)).

Hermetic motors offer a warning. After 500 h of refrigerant exposure, baking for 24 h at 150 °C increased the loss of dielectric strength to as much as 70% ([Doerr and Kujak 1992](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/7048840)). Rapid desorption produced blisters, cracks and delamination ([OSTI 6837374](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/6837374)).

### Insulation types that degrade most

Polyester and polyester-based enamels are the most vulnerable group. A polyester twisted pair fell below 1 kV after 5 days at 150 °C in a water-containing fluid ([US 12,227,716 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12227716B2/en), patent data). According to Suzhou Jufeng, class-200 corona-resistant PEsI/PAI composites are restricted in oil-cooled motors because of poor ATF resistance ([US 12,159,731 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12159731B2/en), patent data).

For PAI, adhesion and delamination stand out. PAI showed signs of delamination after 2 weeks at 160 °C in ester and mineral ATF ([Martinez et al. 2025](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/c037e7e3-7f76-4690-a5cf-b13fdf36a5ab/unpaywall-bitstream-1951351576.pdf)); however, the same problem also occurred in air at 220 °C ([HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf)). At Tre Tau, PAI cracked, formed voids and changed color from burgundy to black; PFA turned gray but did not crack ([Zeynalova et al. 2024](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/DEIS-magazine-MayJune2024.pdf)).

No independent electrical data for PEEK in oil were found. Victrex states that PEEK wire retains its electrical properties in ATFs for up to 2000 h at 180 °C, but names no fluid, method or values ([Victrex e-motor page](https://www.victrex.com/en/emotor-solutions), manufacturer claim). Honda used a PEEK-based layer over enamel for durability in an ATF environment ([SAE 2016-01-1221](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/2016-01-1221), abstract only).

According to the Bologna group, the permittivity and conductivity of PEEK rise sharply above 160 °C and lower the PDIV ([Martinez et al. 2025](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/c037e7e3-7f76-4690-a5cf-b13fdf36a5ab/unpaywall-bitstream-1951351576.pdf)). No electrical data in oil were found for extruded PEEK, PEEK tube, heat-shrink, Kapton tape, epoxy powder or parylene.

### Degradation mechanisms

In wet, hot oil the dominant mechanism is hydrolysis. After 2000 h in Dexron ULV + 0.5% water, the FTIR OH band of the epoxy grew and the ester C=O band at 1735 cm⁻¹ weakened ([Wei Xiongqiang et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.07.008)). XPS oxygen fell from 23.67% to 10.71%; the authors identify hydrolysis of ester bonds at high temperature as the main cause.

The PEsI resin, which also carries ester groups, survived the same conditions. This suggests that network design and cure state matter more than the chemical class (inference). A ranking by chemical class is unreliable; experiments are essential.

Polyimide is a condensation polymer and its properties are affected by water; the rate increases with temperature, water and pressure ([DuPont Kapton properties](https://www.electro-wind.com/web-files/DuPont\Literature\Kapton-summary-of-properties.pdf), manufacturer claim). In hydrothermal aging of a commercial PI film, degradation was attributed to hydrolysis of the imide groups ([Braun et al. 2022](https://ualberta.scholaris.ca/handle/123456789/17234), abstract only). In meta-aramid, water at high temperature breaks hydrogen bonds and lowers elongation ([Arroyo Fernandez et al. 2020](https://constellation.uqac.ca/id/eprint/5442/), abstract only).

Acids feed hydrolysis. Water and air accelerate the thermal oxidation of ATF and form acidic products ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)). According to a Hitachi patent, what actually degrades enamel in ester oils are small dibasic acids such as adipic, sebacic and phthalic acid released by ester hydrolysis ([US 9,349,499 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9349499), patent data).

Copper accelerates oil oxidation. In transformer oil, Cu(I) and Cu(II) species initiated intense free-radical reactions ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/molecules/molecules-29-04690/article_deploy/molecules-29-04690.pdf)). Compared with a copper-only group, the antioxidant DBPC reduced the aging rates of dissipation factor, acidity, micro-water and BDV by 28.8%, 43.2%, 52.9% and 46.7%.

Specimens with exposed cut ends may therefore age faster than oil-only controls (inference). Comparing vessels with and without copper separates this effect.

Additives penetrate the enamel: XPS found 0.4–0.5 at% sulfur only in the ATF group ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)). Neat ethanolamine degraded Kapton within 3 h at 120 °C, giving a 91% diamine yield ([Chen et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11967771/)). At ATF additive levels, however, amine attack (aminolysis) on PI or PAI has not been demonstrated.

Physical effects are small in PEEK and PTFE and large in silicone and nylon. Silicone absorbed hydrocarbon oil and swelled; new –CH₂– bands appeared in FTIR at 2855 and 2925 cm⁻¹ ([García-Tuero et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/api/records/10702164/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Elastomers.pdf/content)). In PA66, the melting enthalpy rose from 11.03 J/g to as much as 29.71 J/g and the material embrittled ([García et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/records/10702226/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Structural%20Polym.pdf?download=1)).

The melting enthalpy of PEEK rose from 31.60 J/g to 45.89–76.16 J/g, but its mechanical properties did not change. Aging was at 100 °C, below the 143 °C Tg of PEEK ([Victrex XPI 174](https://victrex.com/downloads/datasheets/victrex-xpi-174-polymer)). At 150–180 °C, oil diffusion and crystallization may differ; the DSC result is an indication, not proof (inference).

No peer-reviewed data on environmental stress cracking in ATF were found. A SABIC claim that ULTEM PEI resists cracking in automotive fluids could not be opened and is unverified. [ASTM D543-21](https://store.astm.org/d0543-21.html) Practice B defines chemical exposure under applied strain, a suitable route for bent hairpins (inference).

The water content changes during the test. In a vented vessel at 150 °C, ATF water fell from 0.2% to ~0.12% within 12 h ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)). A sealed test may overstate water exposure, while a vented test without top-up may understate it.

## Corrosion: copper and exposed conductor zones

In an oil-cooled motor, the main threat to copper is the sulfur chemistry of the additives and the copper sulfide it produces. On insulated hairpins the risk concentrates where the insulation ends or is damaged and copper is exposed (inference).

### Sulfur chemistry and copper sulfide

Gear oils with EP additives destroy bare copper quickly. At 130 °C, two EP gear oils completely dissolved a 64 µm copper wire in 30 and 60 h, while four ATFs left the wire intact ([Gahagan and Hunt 2016](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsaeijae/7/4/7_20164092/_pdf)). The films formed without a metal deactivator were not protective.

Corrosion inhibitors can also break down. Two ATF inhibitors were equally effective up to 130 °C; at 150 °C a thiadiazole inhibitor decomposed and caused severe pitting ([Warren et al. 2018](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=1&query.bibliographic=Automatic%20transmission%20fluid%20corrosion%20inhibitor%20interactions%20with%20copper), abstract only). Copper corrosivity depends not on total sulfur but on the type of sulfur compounds ([ASTM D130-26](https://store.astm.org/d0130-26.html)).

In the Leeds thesis, higher temperature did not always accelerate corrosion and in most cases changed the mechanism ([Warren 2018, PhD thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/)). Three fluids without a benzotriazole-type inhibitor produced flaking copper sulfide surfaces. The sulfur came from the additives, not the base oil (<0.03% S in the base oil).

An FIB-EDX cross-section showed a cracked, porous Cu–S layer ([Warren 2018 thesis text](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/1/Warren_BLM_Mechanical_Engineering_PhD_2018.pdf)). The 2 µm radius loss calculated from resistance understated the 306 ppm of copper found in the oil. The author attributes this to copper sulfide being partly conductive.

### Vapor phase, temperature cycling and conductive deposits

The vapor phase and the liquid phase give different results. One lubricant corroded most in the vapor phase at 130 °C and much less at higher temperatures ([Shah et al., PIN 2023](https://koehlerinstrument.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/The-Possibility-of-Using-Copper-Corrosion-Tests-for-Lubricants-Used-with-Electric-Vehicles.pdf); trade-magazine summary of SAE 2020-01-0561). Another lubricant was most corrosive in the liquid phase at 150 °C.

Among immersion-cooling candidates, even PAO and a diester showed marked vapor-phase corrosion after 336 h at 130 °C ([Tormos et al. 2024](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/batteries10080285), abstract only). According to Lubrizol, temperature cycling produced more corrosion than isothermal tests predicted ([STLE 2023 program](https://www.stle.org/images/PDF/STLE_ORG/TFC_EV/2023_STLE_EMobility_Program_Guide_10.18.23.pdf)). Fully immersed specimens at a single temperature may miss the worst case (inference).

The EV-specific failure mode is conductive deposits. A Lubrizol patent identifies the deposits as copper sulfide and states that they can cause catastrophic failure of the drive unit ([US 12,529,004 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12529004B2/en), patent data). In a circuit-board test at 150 °C and 5 V, a low-sulfur oil formed no deposits, while a high-sulfur oil formed deposits in both phases.

Even a fluid optimized for FZG wear and liquid-phase corrosion failed this test ([US 12,529,004 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12529004B2/en), patent data). Copper sulfide deposits can bridge conductors at different potentials and cause short circuits ([Plant Engineering 2024](https://www.plantengineering.com/evolution-of-copper-corrosion-testing-for-electric-vehicle-lubricants/)).

### Transformer analogy: crevice corrosion under wraps

The transformer literature shows what can happen to a wrapped conductor. Cu₂S contaminates the paper and lowers its breakdown strength; 1–2 µm flakes (up to 8 µm) detached from the copper can bridge the insulation ([Griffin and Lewand 2007](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/2007-Understanding_Corrosive_Sulfur.pdf), manufacturer claim). Enamel is a barrier to sulfur, which gives low-voltage equipment with enameled conductors an advantage.

Oxygen and temperature decide where the Cu₂S goes. Sealed vials pushed the deposits onto the copper and vented vials onto the paper; 150 °C directed them to the copper and 140 °C for 4 days to the paper ([Doble CCD application note](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/Doble_AppNote_CCD_tests.pdf), manufacturer claim). Sealed units are more susceptible because oxygen competes with sulfur for reaction sites ([Griffin and Lewand 2007](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/2007-Understanding_Corrosive_Sulfur.pdf)).

Crevice corrosion under paper wraps is documented. In a failed 500 kV reactor and under IEC 62535 conditions, corrosion-free gaps alternated with stripe-shaped deposits ([Gao et al. 2019](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2894125), abstract only). Corrosion became more severe as the number of paper layers increased.

When paper-wrapped and bare wires were aged in oil at 130 and 150 °C, Cu₂S greatly reduced the breakdown strength of the oil–paper insulation ([Li et al. 2011](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=1&query.bibliographic=Influences%20of%20Corrosive%20Sulfur%20on%20Copper%20Wires%20and%20Oil-Paper%20Insulation%20in%20Transformers%20Energies), abstract only). A metal passivator prevented this corrosion.

For an EV fluid, the only data on corrosion under insulation come from Castrol. The copper under enamel aged in ATF had corroded, and EDS showed sulfur ([Castrol/Gasgoo 2022](https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202212/6I70323978C501.shtml), manufacturer claim). In HEFT, by contrast, a corrosive-sulfur test with paper-wrapped copper (4 days at 140 °C, also at 160 °C) found no Cu₂S in Castrol ATF ([HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf)).

### Where corrosion is expected on insulated hairpins

Copper exposed to oil is limited to cut ends, stripped or welded zones, and coating defects (inference). Photographing only the outer surface of the insulation will miss corrosion in these zones. The table summarizes the zones and how to inspect them.

### Corrosion test methods and conditions

[ASTM D130-26](https://store.astm.org/d0130-26.html) visually rates the relative corrosivity of petroleum products using a copper strip. For EV use it is criticized because it is operator-dependent, applies no voltage, and its temperature and duration do not match EV conditions ([STLE TLT 2024](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2024/01_January/Feature.aspx)). In the Leeds thesis, D130 ratings did not correlate well with mass change, copper in oil or radius loss ([Warren 2018](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/)).

[ASTM D1275-24](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/astm/ca64b1cb-2b34-4bf0-9884-c67570d10657/astm-d1275-24) detects corrosive sulfur with bare copper: 150 ± 2 °C, 48 h, 220 mL of oil in a sealed 250 mL bottle. The oil is first purged with nitrogen at 0.5 L/min for 5 min; the specimen is a 6 × 25 mm copper foil bent into a V. Results are not comparable with the pre-2006 method.

[IEC 62535:2008](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/7167) measures the ability of an oil to form copper sulfide and transfer it to paper, using copper wrapped with one layer of paper. The CIGRE A2.32 practice uses duplicate specimens, air-saturated oil, capped vials, 150 °C and 72 h ([Doble CCD application note](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/Doble_AppNote_CCD_tests.pdf)). Tarnish levels 4a–4c on the copper and metallic, lustrous deposits on the paper count as failure.

[ASTM D8544-24](https://store.astm.org/d8544-24.html) (CDT) monitors conductive deposit and dendrite formation on an energized circuit board with copper traces. Conditions span 80–180 °C; a typical test runs at 150 °C for up to 500 h, with the liquid and vapor zones monitored separately. End-of-test photographs of the board are evaluated, and before/after ICP analysis of the oil (ASTM D5185) is often requested ([Savant Labs](https://www.savantlab.com/testing-highlights/electric-driveline-fluid-testing-update/)).

The wire corrosion test (WCT) holds a 1 m, 64 µm copper wire in oil at 1 mA DC and logs its resistance every 10 s ([Gahagan and Hunt 2016](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsaeijae/7/4/7_20164092/_pdf)). The original test runs at 130 ± 0.25 °C; the commercial version runs for 72 h at 80–150 °C with separate circuits for the liquid and vapor phases ([Savant Labs](https://www.savantlab.com/testing-highlights/electric-driveline-fluid-testing-update/)). The diameter loss is calculated from R = ρl/A; the current status of ASTM work item WK87553 could not be verified.

Methods on the winding-wire side rely on electrical retention. In the oil-bomb test described by Essex, wire is held at ~150 °C for ~2000 h and then re-tested for BDV, PDIV and cracking ([US 9,543,058 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9543058B2/en), patent data). Hitachi's sealed-tube hydrolysis test runs for 1000 h at 140 °C with 0.4 mL of water in a 400 mL tube and reports the remaining BDV ([US 8,193,451 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8193451B2/en), patent data).

Placing a bare-copper witness in every aging vessel separates insulation damage from fluid aggressiveness (inference). A D1275-type strip or a WCT wire is suitable for this, and the approach also matches the reporting format of D8544 and the WCT.

## Imaging and surface characterization

Imaging in this field is mostly qualitative: visual color ratings of copper strips, end-of-test photographs and SEM/EDS of copper wires. No calibrated before/after photography protocol has been published for insulated conductors aged in oil.

### What has been used so far

For bare copper, the main tool is SEM/EDS. Lubrizol worked with a benchtop SEM (Hitachi TM-1000) and EDS at ×1500 magnification, with a 50 µm scale bar and a new-wire reference ([Gahagan and Hunt 2016](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsaeijae/7/4/7_20164092/_pdf)). The images distinguished pitting, films, flaking and dissolution; the only quantitative measures were diameter loss from resistance and ICP.

In the Leeds thesis, SEM morphology was more discriminating than FTIR peak groups ([Warren 2018 thesis text](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/1/Warren_BLM_Mechanical_Engineering_PhD_2018.pdf)). XPS separated Cu(I) and Cu(II) species (Cu₂O, CuO/Cu(OH)₂, CuSO₄), and an FIB cross-section revealed the Cu–S layer. According to earlier work cited in the thesis, the D130 colors 1a–3a reflect oxide film thickness, and corrosion is not linear across the ratings.

Imaging of insulated conductors is even more limited. Tre Tau described the color change in words: PAI turned from burgundy to black and PFA from orange to gray ([Zeynalova et al. 2024](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/DEIS-magazine-MayJune2024.pdf)). Wei Chen photographed the cracked film ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)), and Castrol used SEM and SEM-EDS ([Castrol/Gasgoo 2022](https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202212/6I70323978C501.shtml), manufacturer claim).

On the transformer side, paper was examined with SEM/EDX at 400–8000×; Cu₂S coats the fibers and bridges between them ([Griffin and Lewand 2007](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/2007-Understanding_Corrosive_Sulfur.pdf), manufacturer claim). The tarnish can turn gray and be confused with carbon left by overheating, and unwrapping the winding reveals additional tarnish. Because deposits are not evenly distributed, several zones must be examined.

The CCD test rates paper in visual classes: dull deposits pass, metallic lustrous deposits fail ([Doble CCD application note](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/Doble_AppNote_CCD_tests.pdf), manufacturer claim). The note includes example photographs, and SEM/EDX, dielectric strength and resistivity are offered as optional confirmation.

Industry relies on end-of-test photographs. CDT boards are assessed for deposits and dendrites from photographs ([STLE TLT 2024](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2024/01_January/Feature.aspx)); in a solenoid test, brass parts were photographed after 120 and 150 °C ([Shah et al., PIN 2023](https://koehlerinstrument.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/The-Possibility-of-Using-Copper-Corrosion-Tests-for-Lubricants-Used-with-Electric-Vehicles.pdf)). Infineum states that after a unit test exceeding 1000 h the windings looked almost new ([Infineum 2025](https://www.infineuminsight.com/en-gb/articles/balancing-efficiency-and-durability/), manufacturer claim).

Quantitative color measurement is only now emerging. The draft CuDDI method photographs both faces of the strip with a CCD camera in a closed light box with diffuse LED light at ~4500 K and 45° ([Plant Engineering 2024](https://www.plantengineering.com/evolution-of-copper-corrosion-testing-for-electric-vehicle-lubricants/)). Copper strips in fuel that were all visually Class 1 were separated by colorimetry with ΔE*ab differences of 0.4–11.1 ([Vlčková et al. 2026](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=1&query.bibliographic=Colorimetric%20Assessment%20as%20a%20Complementary%20Approach%20to%20Visual%20Copper%20Corrosion%20Evaluation%20in%20Automotive%20Fuels), abstract only).

Some aging studies provide no images at all. PEI/PAI wire aged for 15,000 h at 40 °C and 93% RH was assessed only by Weibull breakdown, TGA and FTIR ([Kerkarine and Nedjar 2024](https://www.iieta.org/journals/mmep/paper/10.18280/mmep.110423)).

### What is missing

No validated, color-calibrated before/after photography protocol was found for insulated conductors aged in oil. ΔE has been applied only to copper strips in fuel, not to oil-aged winding-wire insulation. No post-oil images were found of conductors insulated with extruded PEEK, PEEK tube, heat-shrink, Kapton tape, epoxy powder or parylene.

No sources were found for confocal or 3D profilometry or FTIR mapping either. These methods could add novelty to the planned study, but they have no precedent in the literature (inference).

### Recommended imaging protocol

The protocol below is a recommendation assembled from the sources; it is not a published or validated protocol. Do not use color measurement on its own: the relationship between D130 colors and surface chemistry is not linear, so pair ΔE with SEM/EDS ([Warren 2018](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/)).

| Step | What to do | Setting or criterion | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fixed-geometry photography | Both faces of each specimen; same magnification and distance; closed light box | Diffuse, stabilized ~4500 K LED at 45°; specimen position fixed by a jig; raw (RAW) files (recommendation) | CuDDI precedent ([STLE TLT 2024](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2024/01_January/Feature.aspx)) |
| 2. Color reference | Color and gray reference card in every frame; fixed camera settings | Images are calibrated against the reference (recommendation) | [ASTM D2244-23](https://store.astm.org/d2244-23.html) framework |
| 3. Color difference | CIELAB in predefined regions: straight section, bend crown, cut end, stripped zone, overlap | ΔE00 for differences up to ~5 ΔE*ab; ΔL*, Δa*, Δb* also reported; metamerism checked per ASTM D4086 | [ASTM D2244-23](https://store.astm.org/d2244-23.html); [Vlčková et al. 2026](https://api.crossref.org/works?rows=1&query.bibliographic=Colorimetric%20Assessment%20as%20a%20Complementary%20Approach%20to%20Visual%20Copper%20Corrosion%20Evaluation%20in%20Automotive%20Fuels) |
| 4. Optical or stereo microscopy | Cracks, crazing, blisters, delamination | Blister size and density on a scale similar to the ASTM D714 photographic references | [ASTM D714](https://store.astm.org/d0714-02r17.html) |
| 5. SEM/EDS | Cut end, stripped/welded zone, deposits, crack roots | Cu, S, O and the additive elements Zn, P, Mo, B; new-specimen reference; fixed magnification and scale bar | [Gahagan and Hunt 2016](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsaeijae/7/4/7_20164092/_pdf); [Castrol/Gasgoo 2022](https://auto.gasgoo.com/news/202212/6I70323978C501.shtml) |
| 6. Cross-section | Metallographic or FIB section; cut end, tape/tube overlap, under defects | Look for under-film and crevice corrosion | [Warren 2018](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/22729/); [Gao et al. 2019](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2894125) |
| 7. Oil and witness analysis | Cu and S in the oil by ICP; visual rating + ΔE of a bare-copper witness strip | ASTM D5185 or ASTM D4951 | [Savant Labs](https://www.savantlab.com/testing-highlights/electric-driveline-fluid-testing-update/); [Gahagan and Hunt 2016](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsaeijae/7/4/7_20164092/_pdf) |
| 8. Cleaning | A fixed, reported procedure before imaging (e.g., n-heptane rinse or no cleaning) | Loosely bound films can be lost by wiping | [Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf); [Gahagan and Hunt 2016](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsaeijae/7/4/7_20164092/_pdf) |

Take the dry (unaged) photographs and the photographs at every checkpoint on the same specimen and in the same orientation (recommendation). Identify each specimen with a permanent, oil-resistant mark so that ΔE is tracked in the same region.

Industry protocols fall into two families: fluid qualification with bare copper (standardized) and fluid–insulation testing with enameled wire (not standardized). None uses formed hairpins or compares insulation materials; the variable is always the fluid (inference). Details of in-house methods, such as Lubrizol's potentiostat test that measures coating voids, have not been published ([STLE 2023 program](https://www.stle.org/images/PDF/STLE_ORG/TFC_EV/2023_STLE_EMobility_Program_Guide_10.18.23.pdf)).

Standardization is under way: ASTM D02 CS 97 coordinates work on EV fluids, and D15.26 is developing methods for coolant corrosion and elastomer compatibility ([BAR presentation](https://www.bar.ca.gov/pdf/bag/202401/ev.pdf)). No method for e-fluid and motor-insulation compatibility was found at ISO, IEC, DIN, CEC or JASO. The [F+L Magazine 2024](https://www.fuelsandlubes.com/fli-article/global-drive-for-standardisation-in-ev-fluids-and-technologies/) review of standardization also mentions no such work at these bodies.

## Novelty and publication positioning

By combining pieces that are scattered across the literature into a single protocol, the planned study can make seven concrete novelty claims. Each claim rests on an absence documented in the research notes; a final database check should confirm them.

The literature is split into two disconnected communities. Lubricant reviews treat insulation as a black-box "compatibility" requirement ([Newcomb 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2023.1139385/full)). An oil-cooling review states that standard test protocols will be "essential" ([Gao et al. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/mechanical-engineering/articles/10.3389/fmech.2026.1951365/full)), and much of the hairpin know-how is not public ([Goetz et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15830)).

### Research questions and hypotheses

RQ1: Under the same oil protocol, which insulation system and application method best preserves its electrical properties? RQ2: How closely is visual change (ΔE, cracks, blisters) related to electrical loss? RQ3: In which zones of an insulated hairpin does corrosion start, and how does it depend on the fluid?

| Code | Hypothesis | Basis | How to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Systems with hydrolyzable bonds (PAI, PI, PEsI, epoxy) lose markedly more BDV and adhesion in wet oil | [Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008); [Wei Xiongqiang et al. 2022](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2022.07.008) | Dry and wet (0.2–0.5% water) arms |
| H2 | In PEEK, PFA and parylene the changes remain physical (oil uptake, annealing) | [García et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/records/10702226/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Structural%20Polym.pdf?download=1); inference from chemical structure | DSC, mass, dry-oven control |
| H3 | PDIV and capacitance are the least sensitive measurements; BDV, tan δ, adhesion and ΔE change earlier | [Yang PhD thesis](https://www.iem.rwth-aachen.de/cms/iem/forschung/publikationen/publikationen1/~povh/details/?file=981658&lidx=1); [HEFT D2.4](https://site.unibo.it/heftproject/en/publications/d2-4-analysis-and-validation-of-machine-insulation-for-800v-dc-bus.pdf/@@download/file/D2.4%20Analysis%20and%20validation%20of%20machine%20insulation%20for%20800V%20DC%20bus.pdf) | All measurements on the same specimen |

## Recommendations for the study design

The design should be comparable with published protocols: 150 °C as the main level, dry and wet arms, sealed vessels, a dry-oven control and a copper witness in every vessel. The recommendations are based on the literature; details without a source are marked as "recommendation" or "calculation".

### Design decisions

| Decision | Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Fluid selection | At least one ATF and one e-fluid with sulfur-free additives; the fluid of the target motor first | Additives matter more than the base oil; ATF sulfur entered the enamel ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)) |
| Fluid documentation | Product name, base-oil type, viscosity, fire point, batch number; water, acidity, ICP, conductivity, tan δ and BDV of the fresh oil | An unnamed ATF limits transferability ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)); Oviedo reported base-oil and additive fractions ([García et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/records/10702226/files/2022%20ApplScie%20-%20Compatibility%20Autom%20Transm%20Fluids%20Structural%20Polym.pdf?download=1)) |
| Temperature (°C) | Main level 150; a second level of 180 for PEEK, PI and parylene, only if the fire point allows | 150 °C is the IEC Test 20 upper limit and the typical D8544 value; 180 °C in [Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf); PEEK Tg 143 °C ([Victrex XPI 174](https://victrex.com/downloads/datasheets/victrex-xpi-174-polymer)); HEFT stayed at 160 °C because of the fire point |
| Additional temperature for corrosion (°C) | Optional 130 arm or headspace specimen | For one lubricant, vapor-phase corrosion peaked at 130 °C ([Shah et al., PIN 2023](https://koehlerinstrument.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/The-Possibility-of-Using-Copper-Corrosion-Tests-for-Lubricants-Used-with-Electric-Vehicles.pdf)) |
| Duration and checkpoints (h) | 0, 168, 500, 1000, 2000; non-destructive measurements on the same specimens, destructive ones on a separate subgroup at each checkpoint (recommendation) | 500–2000 h ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)); 1000 h in IEC Test 20; 2000 h ([Zeynalova et al. 2024](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/DEIS-magazine-MayJune2024.pdf)); fracture in ATF at 772 h ([Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)) |

### Number of specimens and statistics

Use at least 5 specimens per condition, and preferably 10–20 for BDV and PDIV. IEC Test 20 uses 10 twisted pairs (5 + 5) and 4 U specimens ([IEC 60851-4:2016 text](https://www.normsplash.com/FreeDownload/143987489/IEC-60851-4-2016-en-fr.pdf)). RWTH used 20 twisted pairs per group ([AEE 2022](https://journals.pan.pl//Content/124108/PDF/art16_i.pdf)), and Tre Tau used 10 specimens per wire and 5–20 BDV measurements ([Zeynalova et al. 2024](https://www.doble.com/wp-content/uploads/DEIS-magazine-MayJune2024.pdf)).

Report BDV and PDIV by the Weibull scale parameter (63.2%); this is how RWTH showed the particle effect ([AEE 2022](https://journals.pan.pl//Content/124108/PDF/art16_i.pdf)). Give results as percentage retention relative to both the unaged specimens and the dry-oven control ([Doerr and Kujak 1992](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/7048840); [Yang et al. 2022](http://bib.iem.rwth-aachen.de/IEMpublications/2022LYMaterial.pdf)). Separate the effects of material, fluid, water and duration with multi-factor analysis of variance or a mixed model (recommendation).

The example matrix contains 8 systems: PAI and PI enamel, extruded PEEK, PEEK tube, PEEK heat-shrink, Kapton tape, epoxy powder and parylene. 8 systems × 2 fluids × 2 water levels × 4 checkpoints × 5 specimens = 640 aged specimens (calculation). With the dry-oven control (8 × 4 × 5 = 160) and the unaged reference (8 × 5 = 40), 840 specimens are needed in total (calculation).

If this workload is too large, run a short water screening first and then narrow the long experiment. The Wei Chen group compared 0%, 0.2% and 0.5% water in a 384 h screening ([Wei Chen et al. 2024](https://castjournals.cast.org.cn/joweb/jycl/EN/PDF/10.16790/j.cnki.1009-9239.im.2024.02.008)). Limiting the 180 °C level to systems in high thermal classes also reduces the workload (recommendation).
