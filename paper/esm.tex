\section{Computation details}\label{sec:s-computation}

\emph{Feature extraction.} Scans are reduced to a grid of 2$\times$4-pixel medians after a luminescence filter (connected components of at least 20,040 pixels); calibration 0.0769\,mm per pixel across the scan line, 0.1581\,mm per line and 0.00775\,mm per height unit. The cup-bottom plane is fitted robustly to the plateau at least 10\,mm inside its edge, and all depths are taken from it. The flange comprises the points 20--40\,mm below the bottom plane, at least 8\,mm from the cup wall and 3\,mm from the outer edge. Wall positions are interpolated where the binned (0.5\,mm) radial profile reaches 10, 15 and 20\,mm depth; the wall angle is taken between 10 and 20\,mm. The arm angle is fitted to the longest run of the profile whose slope stays below 15$^\circ$, shortened by 1\,mm at both ends and at least 4\,mm long. Flange widths are medians over bands $\pm$2\,mm about the mid-lines; corner tips are the outline points within 0.3\,mm of the extreme diagonal projection. Simulated parts are mirrored from the quarter model and processed with the same definitions on a 1\,mm grid.

\emph{Floors and effects.} Batches of $B=50$ consecutive parts; batches with fewer than 80\,\% valid values are dropped; centres are 10\,\% trimmed means; $\alpha=0.95$. Intervals: 2000 resamples of the batches within alternatives (seed 1), pairs of copies of one batch excluded, and 2000 resamples of whole alternatives. Scatter floor: 50 random permutations of the parts within each series. Components: rational subgroups of five consecutive parts; successive-difference standard deviation $\sqrt{\overline{\Delta^2}/2}$. Protocol (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}): $\sigma_\mathrm{w}$ is the pooled standard deviation within batches of 50 and $\sigma_\mathrm{b}$ the standard deviation of the batch means corrected for $\sigma_\mathrm{w}^2/50$. Lag floors (Table~\ref{tab:lags}): the 95\,\% quantile of the differences between batch centres a given number of batches apart. Between-series floor: the 95\,\% quantile of the differences between batch centres of different series of the same geometry and blank-holder force.

\emph{Decision rules.} Adequacy share $p=0.95$; per-geometry floors; requirements below zero are left out for these non-negative characteristics. The largest-residual margin is the $\lceil (n+1)\cdot 0.9\rceil$-th smallest absolute residual of $n$ calibration alternatives, the largest when this exceeds $n$. M1s: ordinary least squares of $q_{95}$ on the nominal simulation $s_0$ over the calibration alternatives, M1 when fewer than two alternatives or no spread of $s_0$. M5 and M5n: Gaussian-process regression with a constant times anisotropic squared-exponential kernel plus white noise, normalised targets, three optimiser restarts (random state 0), length scales in $[0.1, 100]$; the noise variance is bounded below by the larger of the mean squared block-bootstrap standard error of the calibration alternatives' $q_{95}$ and the pooled variance of the targets between the calibration alternatives of one geometry and blank-holder force, relative to the variance of the targets; descriptors are a convex-geometry indicator, the blank-holder force in units of 100\,kN and the lubrication rank (coarse 0, medium 1, fine 2, the order of the oil amount), with descriptors that are constant over the calibration set dropped. NN: mean $q_{95}$ of the calibration alternatives of the target's geometry at the nearest blank-holder force (both neighbours when equally near; the other geometry when the family has none). Mc: for each lubrication pattern, the friction coefficient of the DDACS grid at 0.99\,mm whose simulated mid-side and corner draw-in, in floors, are closest in least squares to the centres of the calibration alternatives with that pattern (all calibration alternatives if none has it). M3 and M3n (M4 in the code): histogram gradient boosting (maximum depth 3, 200 iterations, learning rate 0.08, random state 0) on the geometry indicator, blank-holder force, two lubrication indicators, sheet thickness and oil film; 500 incoming conditions are drawn for the new alternative from calibration parts with the same lubrication pattern, with in-sample residuals pooled over the calibration alternatives resampled. Their jackknife+ interval \citep{barber2021predictive} uses the $n$ leave-one-out fits over the calibration alternatives: each predicts the new alternative and leaves a residual on the alternative it left out; with the median residual as offset and the absolute deviations of the residuals from it as scores, the interval runs from the $\lfloor 0.1(n+1)\rfloor$-th smallest prediction minus score to the $\lceil 0.9(n+1)\rceil$-th smallest prediction plus score, the extreme values when $n$ is too small.

\emph{Distances and intervals.} The shares of correct and wrong verdicts are evaluated exactly at the exclusion distances of the cases, on a grid of 0.05 floors up to 600 floors. Intervals: 1000 resamples of the held-out alternatives within each geometry (seeds derived from the scope), each with one of 1000 block-bootstrap replicates of every alternative's $q_{95}$ (batches of 50 resampled within the series); the resamples are shared by all rules of a scope. Refitting bootstrap: 200 resamples in which the three lubrication patterns of each geometry are drawn with replacement (at least two distinct ones), all forces kept; every alternative of a drawn pattern enters as often as its pattern was drawn, a target's copies never calibrate it, and every alternative-level rule is refitted, so that each case keeps its evidence relation. The decomposition of Section~\ref{M-sec:res-drivers} is a two-way analysis of variance of the log decisive distance over the ten calibrated rules and the relations, without replication, so the interaction is the residual.

\emph{Procedure.} The guard band $g$ of a rule and relation is the smallest margin on a grid of 0.25 floors up to 20 floors beyond which at least 95\,\% of the decided verdicts are correct, the verdicts being those at the requirement grid of the paper (0.5 to 10 floors in steps of 0.5, then 12, 15 and 20 floors, on both sides of the truth); the margin of a verdict is $(R-U)/F$ for meets and $(L-R)/F$ for fails. Step 6 widens the rule's interval by $g$ floors on both sides, and its distances are computed with the exact estimator. The guard bands are chosen and evaluated on the same cases.

\emph{Budget, guard, scenarios, robustness.} The budget uses all subsets of $k=1,\dots,8$ calibration alternatives of the family (M5 and M5n from $k=2$), bands from 200 resamples of the held-out alternatives. The guards and the tolerance scenarios use the stored intervals of every rule. ISO~2768-1 angular rows by the shorter leg: the wall (about 28\,mm) and the concave arm (about 18\,mm) over 10 up to 50\,mm, the convex arm (about 9\,mm) up to 10\,mm. Capability-anchored requirements: the upper limit at the centre of the parts plus $3P_\mathrm{pk}$ long-term standard deviations. The temperature correction subtracts $b\,(T-\bar T_a)$ from each characteristic, with $b$ the pooled within-alternative slope on the punch temperature $T$. The smoothed simulation replaces the simulated values of each geometry and force by a quadratic response surface in sheet thickness and friction coefficient. Calibration series of $n$ parts: the calibration alternatives' $q_{95}$ and its standard error from their first $n$ parts, the truth from all 500.

\emph{Software.} Python 3.11 with NumPy 2.4, pandas 3.0, SciPy 1.17, scikit-learn 1.9 \citep{pedregosa2011scikit} and Matplotlib 3.11.
