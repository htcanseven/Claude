\begin{abstract}
Vibration-based condition monitoring of induction motors is increasingly
attractive on low-cost micro-electro-mechanical-systems (MEMS) sensors suitable
for wireless Internet-of-Things (IoT) nodes; however, the low sampling rates of
such sensors and the evaluation practices common in the literature cast doubt on
reported performance. In this work, a publicly available smartphone-acquired
vibration dataset---six health states, three supply frequencies, and two load
conditions, sampled at $100$~Hz---is used to establish a deployment-realistic
baseline for IoT-based motor condition monitoring. The near-perfect accuracies
obtained under random splitting of overlapping windows are shown to be largely a
data-leakage artefact: under a leakage-free, recording-wise protocol the accuracy
of standard classifiers falls by approximately $40$ to $45$ percentage points,
from essentially $100\%$ to $54$--$61\%$. The smartphone MEMS channel is
characterised as a measurement instrument, establishing that its usable band is
confined below $50$~Hz and that the on-device gravity compensation discards
diagnostically useful low-frequency content, so that the raw acceleration
channels are the more informative. An accuracy--resource trade-off analysis
further shows that a resource-frugal configuration---$25$~Hz sampling, three raw
channels, and a $4$~s window---raises leakage-free accuracy from $54\%$ to $74\%$
while reducing the raw data volume roughly eight-fold, and a communication link
budget together with an edge-model footprint identifies deployable operating
points for Bluetooth Low Energy, Zigbee, NB-IoT, and LoRaWAN nodes. Under honest
evaluation, therefore, ultra-low-bandwidth sensing is not merely tolerable but
advantageous, and the reproducible pipeline released with this study provides a
deployment-honest reference for low-cost IoT-based condition monitoring of
induction motors.
\end{abstract}
