\begin{abstract}
Vibration-based condition monitoring of induction motors is increasingly
attractive on low-cost micro-electro-mechanical-systems (MEMS) sensors suitable
for wireless Internet-of-Things nodes; however, the low sampling rates of such
sensors and the evaluation practices common in the literature raise questions
about real-world performance. In this work, a publicly available
smartphone-acquired vibration dataset---six health states, three supply
frequencies, two load conditions, sampled at $100$~Hz---is used to establish a
deployment-realistic baseline for IoT-based motor condition monitoring. It is
shown that the near-perfect accuracies obtained under random splitting of
overlapping windows are largely a data-leakage artefact: under a leakage-free,
recording-wise protocol the accuracy falls by approximately $40$ percentage
points. The smartphone MEMS channel is characterised as a measurement instrument,
revealing that the on-device gravity compensation discards diagnostically useful
low-frequency content. An accuracy--resource trade-off analysis further shows
that a resource-frugal configuration ($25$~Hz sampling, three raw channels, a
$4$~s window) is not only sufficient but superior to the naive configuration
under honest evaluation, and a communication link budget together with an
edge-model footprint identifies deployable operating points for Bluetooth Low
Energy, Zigbee, NB-IoT, and LoRaWAN nodes. The study provides a reproducible and
deployment-honest reference for low-cost IoT-based condition monitoring of
induction motors.
\end{abstract}
