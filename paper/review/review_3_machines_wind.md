# Reviewer 3 (electrical machines / wind-turbine drivetrains) — RED manuscript "Alternative-dependent diagnosability"

## Recommendation: Major revision

The design method (SDR, MDE, detectable share, six-step co-selection) is a genuine, well-packaged contribution and the demonstration is unusually transparent (open code, sensitivity, bootstrap). But the machine-physics reading of the results is asserted rather than tested: the "control structure decides where the fault is visible" claim, the measured-vs-commanded voltage difference behind the headline "voltage transducers are decisive for the PMSG", and the role of the resistor-limited fault emulation are unexplained or explained incorrectly. Several dataset descriptions (units of the severity axis, WFSG trial accounting, phase-to-phase faults inside the transfer test, onset definitions) need correction. None of this requires new experiments — most is text or a re-run of the released pipeline — but the design implications must be re-scoped afterwards.

## Summary

The paper treats diagnosability as a property of (architecture, control, observation suite) and measures it with a signal-to-drift ratio: the relative change of a feature under a fault of known extent, normalised by the 95th percentile of the healthy relative change between adjacent intervals. From it follow a minimum detectable extent and a detectable share per alternative, suite and fault class, a co-selection procedure, and a transfer test across a product family. The demonstration uses the InnovaPower benchmarks: a 2.5 kVA PMSG and a 2.5 kW SCIG on one bench (20 kHz, FOC, 2.6 Ω tapped-winding shorts, 24 tap pairs × 9 operating points, 9 healthy trials each) and a 2 kVA salient-pole WFSG on another (4 kHz, 3L-NPC, 1–34 Ω). Findings: the same tap pair is a different physical event in each machine; the PMSG's signature sits in the terminal-voltage negative sequence, the SCIG's in the d-axis 2fe ripple and current negative sequence, the WFSG's additionally in the field current; the cheapest adequate suite differs per machine; detectors transfer only when self-referenced.

## Major comments

**M1. The fault emulation is resistor-limited, and the "physical severity" axis mixes units (Secs. 5.1, 5.4, Fig. 4, Sec. 6.1).** With a 2.6/2.83 Ω series resistor on a few-kVA winding the fault current is essentially e·V_phase/R_f: volts-per-turn × shorted turns × speed. The PMSG–SCIG difference at equal taps is therefore the ratio of terminal volts per turn (PMSG ≈158 V phase at 1800 rpm vs the SCIG's converter-set flux), and the speed dependence of Sec. 5.4 is a property of the resistor, not the machines. A real bolted turn fault is limited by the leakage reactance of the shorted turns, saturates with speed, reaches many times rated current and — the first-order topology difference — cannot be de-excited in a PMSG but can in an SCIG or WFSG. None of this is said. Moreover `I1` in the pipeline is the Hann-projected fundamental amplitude, i.e. peak (FAULT_TURNS_D01_D04_S1800_T52: pre-fault Ia RMS 2.08 A, I1 = 2.80 A), while the fault current is RMS: "1.86 / 0.80 times the pre-fault stator current" are RMS/peak means (RMS/RMS ≈ 2.6 / 1.1; medians 1.34 / 0.60); the Fig. 4 x-axis "(RMS)" is wrong; "7.1 A against 2.8–3.7 A" are peak values with truncated ranges (7.0–8.2 A and 2.8–4.7 A; means 7.5 / 3.7 A, ratio 2.0, not 2.5). Fix the units, state that severity here is resistor-limited, contrast with a bolted fault, and turn the volts-per-turn relation into a design-stage predictor of physical severity.
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M2. The "controller masks the current asymmetry" mechanism is asserted, not tested (Secs. 5.2, 6.1).** Both machines run the same B-Box FOC with PI current loops; what differs is the angle source (encoder vs rotor-flux observer, whose estimate is itself modulated at 2fe by the asymmetry), loop bandwidth relative to 2fe, and the healthy baseline of each feature. Table 2 shows the D2/D1 null — a controller output, no sensor involved — is nine times noisier on the PMSG, so the "signal level on identical sensors" explanation of Sec. 6.4 cannot be the whole story. The direct test is in the data: report the absolute healthy baselines of I2/I1, V2/V1, D2/D1 and ΔV2/ΔI2 under fault (the negative-sequence impedance of Lee et al. 2003, cited but unused): a current-regulated drive gives a large |ΔV2/ΔI2|, a weakly regulated one the machine's Z2. Cite the closed-loop literature that established masking (Tallam, Habetler & Harley, IEEE TIA 39(3) 2003; Cruz & Cardoso, IEEE TIA 40(5) 2004).
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M3. Measured vs commanded voltage, and the fragility of "VTs are decisive" (Secs. 5.5, 6.2, abstract).** A VSI imposes the terminal voltage; the machine does not "produce" a different one. The fundamental difference between measured V2 and D2·Vdc/2 comes from dead-time/inverter nonlinearity (which converts a negative-sequence *current* into a negative-sequence voltage error), DC-link ripple and the VT chain — so V2/V1 may partly re-express I2 through the inverter rather than give a cleaner view of the fault. Delete "the difference between what the converter commands and what the machine produces". Quantitatively the headline rests on little: at three cycles V2/V1 and D2/D1 have median SDR 2.5 vs 2.4 for inter-turn faults; the MDE gap (2.8 vs 7.4 %) arises because the ≈3 % cluster sits at SDR ≈ 1; and Table A4 shows that at α = 0.90 current transducers alone reach 2.8 % on the PMSG — "no ordering of the alternatives reverses" is true, but the suite conclusion does. Soften "decisive/superfluous", state the α-sensitivity, and explain the V2–D2 difference physically (dead-time voltage ∝ Vdc·t_d·f_sw, checked against operating point).
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M4. The null captures sub-second non-stationarity and the denominator conflates baseline with signature (Sec. 3.2).** Adjacent 0.3 s intervals do not represent a reference that is months old (thermal state, load, ageing, grid conditions), so every MDE is an optimistic bound for in-service CM. With |φ̄_R| in the denominator, features whose healthy value is a noise floor (Id 2fe under id = 0 control; I2 in a symmetric machine) get a large null purely from their baseline level, which is machine- and converter-specific, not a property of the signature. Fix: add a between-trial null from the nine healthy trials and report MDE/DS under it; discuss the denominator and show that a z-score normalisation gives the same ranking, or explain why not.
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M5. Observation suites and features vs industrial practice (Secs. 4.3, 6.2).** (i) The machine-side converter already carries three phase-current sensors; "3 CT" is a question of data access, bandwidth and sensor class, not added hardware — say so, or define it as a CMS with its own CTs. (ii) Standard wind-generator observables are absent and unlisted: winding RTDs, frame vibration at 2fe (an accelerometer is not blind where a filtered torque transducer is), stray flux, partial discharge on MV windings, circulating current between parallel branches, and above all the neutral-point/zero-sequence voltage — the most sensitive PMSM turn-fault indicator (Urresty, Riba & Romeral, EPSR 2012), needing one wire, and likely to overturn "VTs are decisive for the PMSG". (iii) The 3-CT suite lacks the angle-free Park's-vector modulus 2fe component (EPVA, Cruz & Cardoso), the twin of your dq 2fe ripple. (iv) I0/I1 is identically zero for an isolated-neutral star winding; drop it. Say the suite conclusions hold "among the suites considered".
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data (EPVA, Z2); the rest text

**M6. WFSG harmonisation and trial accounting are inconsistent (Secs. 4.1, 4.4; Tables 1, 2, A1, A6; Fig. 3).** (a) Sec. 4.4 says the null pools 198 WFSG records; Table 2 and the code use 618, including 180 phase-to-phase faults. (b) The transfer test's WFSG positives include those AB/AC faults at 11–34 Ω, a class absent from the PMSG/SCIG data; the 0.98 AUC is inflated by this as well as by the missing healthy trials. (c) At 2.83 Ω only four inter-turn and four inter-winding tap pairs exist; "the same 24 cases" holds only when 1–11.3 Ω are pooled; Table A1 mixes a DS over all impedances with an MDE at 2.83 Ω. (d) Three-cycle windows at 23.9–55.7 Hz leave 0–2 fault windows per trial (all 22 trials at 350 rad/s have fewer than three; two have none). (e) F starts at relay command + 30 ms on the PMSG/SCIG — ≈55 ms of healthy time before the ≈85 ms relay pick-up lies inside F — but at the measured fault-current onset on the WFSG; harmonise on the latter. (f) WFSG reference halves are 0.25 s against faults of 0.08–0.24 s, not "the same duration as F".
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M7. Scaling to MW generators and the missing DFIG (Secs. 1, 2.4, 6.2, 6.4).** The masking of M2 depends on 2fe relative to the current-loop bandwidth. A direct-drive PMSG such as the IEA 15 MW runs at a fundamental of order 10 Hz, not 60 Hz, so current-side signatures will be masked far more and command/voltage-side ones dominate; a medium-speed PMSG or 4–6-pole SCIG sits elsewhere. State this ratio as a design parameter the concept team can compute. The DFIG — dominant onshore in Polinder 2006/2013 — has a grid-connected stator with no stator current regulation, exactly where the control-structure argument reverses; its absence must be discussed. Positively, form-wound MV windings with few turns per coil make a single-turn fault 3–10 % of a branch, so the tested extents are the relevant range, and parallel paths make the inter-branch class relevant. For the design-stage instantiation, FE turn-fault models (Vaseghi, Takorabet & Meibody-Tabar, IEEE T-Mag 2009) can inject *bolted* faults, but the null (noise, drift, controller jitter) cannot be simulated — say where it will come from.
Addressable: text

**M8. Overclaiming in the design implications (Sec. 6.2, abstract, conclusions).** "Not worth specifying a torque transducer on any of the three" — it was available on two and its bandwidth/filtering is not stated. "The field current detected every inter-winding fault" — at mixed impedances, and only with a static exciter and slip rings; brushless exciters do not expose the main field current (cite Neti & Nandi, IEEE TIA 45(3) 2009). Terminal-voltage V2/V1 sits in the "dq/controller" group of Table 3, so the signature-location statistic says "controller" for a transducer measurement.
Addressable: text
