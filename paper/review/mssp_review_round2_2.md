The shortening to 31 pages removed the appendix tables, the per-phase and decomposition tables and the severity figure, replacing them by sentences; most of those sentences check out against the released CSVs, but several do not, and the removed displays are where the discrepancies sit.

## Remaining comments

**Major**

1. Resampling unit of the decision table (lines 316–318, 888; Table 5). Within a level the bootstrap resamples the 36 (or 18) operating-point trials individually, treating the four (two) tap pairs of a level as exchangeable, while Table 3 resamples pairs. With the null and feature fixed, I obtain for the smallest inter-turn level P = 0.20 (trial) vs 0.13 (pair) for the PMSG voltage suite and 0.85 vs 0.97 for the SCIG drive-internal suite, i.e. the SCIG cell changes from uncertain to meets with the resampling unit. The 1.00 and 0.00 cells are unaffected. Fix: state the unit and the exchangeability assumption in the Table 5 note and report the pair-resampled probability for the four uncertain cells. Does not block acceptance.
2. "Stated power" is a pooled share, not the power at e_req (lines 164–165, 193–195). DS(e ≥ e_req) pools every level above the requirement, so its value depends on how many large extents the matrix happens to contain. At the required level itself the share is 0.64 (PMSG voltage residual, inter-turn 3.0), 0.78 (SCIG duty-cycle residual) and 0.78 (PMSG duty-cycle residual, inter-winding 2.8) against pooled 0.88, 0.93 and 0.96. Line 429–430 juxtaposes "per-level median 1.1" with "share 88 %", which invites the misreading that 88 % of the smallest faults are detected. Fix: define DS_req at the level e_req (or report both), and say so where the 0.9 is introduced. Does not block acceptance, but changes two sentences of the discussion.
3. Conclusions and Section 6.1 misstate the WFSG verdict (lines 571–574, 657–658): "the middle tested inter-turn level being met by drive-internal quantities on all three machines" and "at the smallest level no suite on any bench meets it" contradict Table 5, where both WFSG cells are n.t.; only the largest level is tested there (line 479–481 says so correctly). "Not testable" must not be read as "met" or "not met". Must be corrected; a wording fix.

Evidence removed by the shortening that the text now asserts incorrectly (checks 7–9 above): the WFSG fault-current ratios, the severity-axis comparison and the operating-envelope sentence are not reproducible from the released tables as written. Either restore a compact table (one row per fixed feature and speed; one line of severity-axis medians) or rewrite the sentences from the tables. Blocks acceptance only in the sense that numbers in the text must match the data the paper says reproduces them.

## Length and presentation

The 31-page version is readable and self-contained for the main argument: Tables 3–6 carry the intervals and probabilities the claims rest on, and I could verify every entry of those tables. What the shortening removed is the evidence behind the robustness sentences of Sections 5.3, 5.4 and 5.7 (per-pair spread, severity axis, operating envelope, window and quantile sensitivity), and three of those sentences no longer match the data (checks 7–11). A single compact table of per-speed medians and shares for the fixed features, or a sentence per claim rewritten from the CSVs, would close the gap without a supplement. Captions: Fig. 4 names its window and the WFSG pooling; Fig. 5 does not name its window length; Table 5's note is complete except for the WFSG's screening status and the resampling unit. Fig. 1 is legible; the R ≥ 2|F| box should match the demonstration. The decision table is now the right object for the headline, and the "n.t." cells are a genuine improvement, provided the discussion respects them.

## Recommendation

**Minor revision.** The statistical repairs I asked for have been made and verified against the released tables: the null is resampled, the intervals are cluster-based, the decision table is a joint-event probability with a stated power, and the oracle is calibrated. What remains is a definitional point (pooled versus per-level power), a stated assumption (trial versus pair resampling within a level), one factual misstatement in the conclusions about the WFSG, and a handful of sentences that replaced removed displays and no longer match the data. All are fixable from the existing tables. Confidence: high on the statistical points, medium on the machine-specific interpretation.
