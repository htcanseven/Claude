# Reviewer 1 (design theory and methodology) — *Research in Engineering Design*

Manuscript: "Alternative-dependent diagnosability: a design-stage method for co-selecting system architecture and monitoring suite under a detectability requirement, with a demonstration on three wind-turbine generator topologies" (frozen v1, 32 pp.)

## Recommendation: Major revision

The paper has a genuine design-methodological kernel: it moves detectability from a post-hoc signal-processing concern into architecture selection, proposes a drift-calibrated, unit-free statistic that makes alternatives comparable, and adds a family-level transfer test. It is well written, candid about its limits, and reproducible. It is not yet a RED paper for four fixable reasons: the "design-stage" claim is asserted but never instantiated, and the statistic's denominator is exactly the quantity a design-stage model cannot supply; the selection step that carries the "so what" is never executed in the demonstration; the positioning omits the design community's own failure-reasoning line and overclaims novelty against model-based diagnosability; and three headline claims (control structure, transfer rule, robustness) are stated more strongly than the evidence supports, with one internal table inconsistency. All of this is addressable by rewriting and re-running the released pipeline on the same data.

## Summary of the contribution

The author proposes that diagnosability be treated as a property of (architecture, control structure, observation suite) and evaluated during architecture selection. The core instrument is the signal-to-drift ratio (SDR): the relative change of a feature between a healthy reference interval and a faulted interval, divided by the α-quantile of the relative change the healthy system shows between adjacent reference intervals, pooled per alternative. From the SDR the paper derives a minimum detectable extent (MDE), a detectable share (DS) and a "signature location", and embeds them in a six-step procedure (frame, generate trials, compute, map, select, assess transfer). The procedure is instantiated on public bench data of a PMSG, an SCIG and a WFSG with identical tapped winding faults. Findings: equal design-level extent is not equal physical severity; the fault surfaces in different measurement groups per machine; the cheapest suite reaching the smallest extent is drive-internal for the SCIG but requires voltage transducers for the PMSG; and a learned detector transfers across machines only when features are referenced per trial to their own healthy interval. Robustness to window length and null quantile, and bootstrap intervals, are reported.

## Major comments

**M1. Positioning and novelty claim.** §1 para 1 states that "for electromechanical systems there is no equivalent method" to testability analysis. This is contradicted by the paper's own §2.2: structural diagnosability analysis and cost-aware sensor placement (Krysander & Frisk 2008; Frisk et al.; Blanke et al.; Zhang & Rizzoni 2015, applied to an EV drive) answer "which faults are detectable with which sensor set" from a model structure at the design stage. More seriously for RED, the design community's own line on failure at the conceptual stage is absent: function–failure mapping (Tumer & Stone, RED 2003; Stone, Tumer & Van Wie, JMD 2005), the FFIP/functional-failure-reasoning framework for evaluating conceptual architectures (Kurtoglu & Tumer, JMD 2008; Kurtoglu, Tumer & Jensen, RED 2010), and PHM feature-quality metrics (monotonicity/prognosability/trendability, Coble & Hines 2009), which are the closest relatives of the SDR. What is actually new is (i) an empirical, drift-calibrated magnitude of detectability that structural analysis cannot give, (ii) the co-selection framing, and (iii) the family transfer test. State exactly that, delete the "no equivalent method" claim, and add a short comparison table (model-based structural vs. this method: inputs, outputs, cost handling, stage). The uncited Suh (2001) in refs.bib suggests an angle worth one paragraph: the current controller and the monitor share the same DP (current asymmetry), a coupling in the axiomatic-design sense that explains why signatures relocate.
Addressable: text

**M2. The "design-stage" claim is not credible as framed.** Title, abstract and §3.1 promise a design-stage method; the demonstration is entirely test-stage on built machines, and the paper concedes in §6 that the design-stage instantiation is future work. The deeper problem is unaddressed: the SDR numerator (fault signature) is simulable by FE/circuit models, but the denominator (sensor noise, thermal drift, controller activity, operating-point wander) is a property of hardware and environment that a design-stage simulation will not reproduce, so a simulated SDR is optimistic by an unknown factor. Reframe honestly: "a method for architecture–monitoring co-selection whose test-stage instantiation is demonstrated"; add a subsection stating what a design-stage instantiation must supply (a null from prior hardware of the same class, a "drift library", or noise models) and how the bench data could validate it; move "design-stage" out of the title.
Addressable: text

**M3. A metric more than a method: steps 1, 2, 5, 6 are not reusable as written.** §3.4 declares these "design decisions the method structures but does not take", yet gives no structure: how to elicit e_req and α (they should link to fault growth rate and consequence, i.e. the FMEA severity/occurrence side the paper invokes in §2.1); how many operating points, extents and healthy trials step 2 needs (the "reference ≥ 2× fault duration" rule appears only in §5.3); what the cost model c(s) is (never more than ordinal); how to aggregate over multiple failure-mode classes (the demo treats two classes separately; a requirement covers all of F); and how to decide when bootstrap intervals straddle the requirement (SCIG I2/I1 MDE 2.8 [2.7, 7.4]). Specify each with a default rule and a worked example.
Addressable: text

**M4. Step 5 is never executed, so the "so what for the designer" is not shown.** No requirement is stated, no cost is given, and no (architecture, suite) pair is selected in §4 or §5. Moreover, in this demonstration every alternative reaches the smallest tested extent with some suite, so diagnosability would not have changed the architecture choice, only the monitoring specification (three VTs, a negligible cost at MW scale). Say this plainly, and add a decision table: for e_req ∈ {3, 5, 8}% and α ∈ {0.90, 0.95, 0.99}, the cheapest suite per alternative classified as meets/uncertain/fails using the bootstrap intervals, with an ordinal cost. That table, not the feature rankings, is the design result. Then state the condition under which the method changes an architecture decision (no affordable suite meets the requirement for one alternative).
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M5. The derived quantities are not requirement-writable as defined.** DS (§3.3) pools all extents from 2.3 % to 40 %, so it reflects the benchmark's test matrix, not the alternative; define DS conditional on e ≥ e_req or per extent cluster. MDE as min{e : median SDR ≥ 1} is not monotone-safe; define it as the smallest extent above which all tested extents are detectable, or state the monotonicity assumption. The fixed feature is chosen on the same 108 trials it is evaluated on (§3.2), an in-sample selection over 31 candidates; use nested selection or acknowledge the bias.
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M6. "The control structure decides where the fault becomes visible" is an interpretation, not a result.** Both PMSG and SCIG run FOC on the same bench (§4.1); the paper never separates the machine physics (magnetising current, back-EMF) from controller action, nor the SDR numerator from its denominator. Table `tab:null` shows the SCIG null for I2/I1 is 5× lower and for I_d 2f_e 35× lower than the PMSG's, so the SCIG/PMSG ratios of 3.1 and 12.6 (§4.3) may be largely denominator. Report δ and q_α separately for the key features; give the controller bandwidths relative to 2f_e if the controller claim is kept; otherwise soften to "the machine–drive combination". Also, Table `tab:groups` and Fig. `fig:features` file V2/V1 under "dq/controller" although it is a terminal measurement, while §3.3 promises four groups including terminal voltages; the signature-location statistic as tabulated therefore cannot support the mechanism it is cited for.
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M7. The transfer implication conflates two referencing rules.** §4.6 and Table `tab:transfer` show that z-scoring on the target's healthy trials (a commissioning baseline) transfers poorly (logistic S→P 0.39 AUC), while referencing each window to its own trial's immediately preceding reference interval transfers well. §5.1 (third claim) and §5.2 then prescribe "deviations from the unit's own reference behaviour ... established at commissioning", which is the rule that failed. The rule that worked presupposes an abrupt onset with a healthy interval seconds earlier, i.e. a short-horizon change detector, not condition monitoring of a slowly developing fault. In addition, reference windows normalised against their own mean/std are used as negatives, so the classifier partly learns "is this window inside the normalising interval"; report AUC/TPR on the healthy trials' fault-time windows only (or hold the normalising windows out of the negatives). Rewrite the implication accordingly.
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M8. Scope conditions are understated and the generality claim overstated.** §5.3 says nothing in §3 is specific to electrical machines and that run-to-failure gearbox or cycling battery trials would serve. The method as defined requires abrupt, seeded faults with a stationary operating point and a healthy interval immediately before the fault; features with non-zero healthy value (δ divides by |φ̄_R|); and an extent scale realisable in every alternative. Run-to-failure data meet none of the first set. Write an explicit scope box in §3.5 (fault-onset type, trial structure, feature class, extent scale) and make the physical-severity axis of §4.1 part of step 1/4 rather than a post-hoc observation.
Addressable: text

**M9. Internal inconsistency in the three-alternative suite table and fragility of the headline.** Appendix Table `tab:suites3` assigns I2/I1 (7.4 %/57 %) or D2/D1 to the PMSG's voltage-including suites, although Tables `tab:topfeat` and `tab:sens` give V2/V1 as the strongest PMSG feature at 3 cycles (2.5 median SDR, 2.8 %/73 %); likewise V_d^cmd is chosen for the SCIG where I_d 2f_e is stronger. Either the "common feature set" silently drops features, the screen removed them, or the table is wrong; state the rule and fix. Note also that at 3 cycles the SCIG's 3 CT MDE is 7.4 %, the same as the PMSG's, so the abstract's "superfluous for the SCIG" holds at 5 and 8 cycles only; the robustness statement should say that orderings are robust while MDEs move by one extent cluster.
Addressable: re-analysis of existing data

**M10. Length and structure.** 32 pages, twelve tables and nine figures for two failure-mode classes. The four findings are restated five times (abstract, §1 list, §4 headings, §5.1, §6); §4.2 is a wall of numbers that belongs in tables; the method section (1,200 words) is a quarter of the results. Cut 20–25 %, merge `tab:mde` and `tab:boot`, and shorten the 40-word title. This also reduces the desk-reject risk that the paper reads as a condition-monitoring study.
Addressable: text

## Claims not supported by the presented evidence

- "For electromechanical systems there is no equivalent method" (§1) — contradicted by the cited structural-analysis literature and by FFIP-type work.
- "The control structure decides where in the drive the fault becomes visible" (abstract, §5.1) — machine physics and null level are not separated.
- "The findings are robust to the method's tuning" (abstract) — orderings are; MDEs move by an extent cluster and the SCIG 3 CT result changes at 3 cycles.
- "A learned detector transfers only when its features are referenced to the monitored unit's own healthy behaviour" and the commissioning-baseline prescription (§5.1, §5.2) — the baseline variant did not transfer; the per-trial variant did, with leakage-prone negatives.
- "A formal definition of alternative-dependent diagnosability" (contribution 1) — not given.
- "The cheapest observation suite meeting a requirement is architecture-specific" — no requirement or cost is stated, so neither "cheapest" nor "meeting" is operationalised.
- "Nothing in Section 3 is specific to electrical machines" and applicability to run-to-failure trials (§5.3) — unshown and, as defined, false for gradual faults.
