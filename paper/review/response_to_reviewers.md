# Response to reviewers and change log

Manuscript: *Alternative-dependent diagnosability: co-selecting system architecture and monitoring suite under a detectability requirement, demonstrated on three wind-turbine generator topologies* (Research in Engineering Design).

Three reviewer reports were obtained on the pre-review version (`review/main_v1.pdf`, 32 pages): a design-theory reviewer (R1), a signal-based FDI / statistics reviewer (R2) and an electrical-machines / wind-turbine reviewer (R3). All three recommended major revision and judged every comment addressable by rewriting or by re-running the released pipeline on the same public data. The revised manuscript (`main.pdf`, 42 pages) implements all of them; the points below say what was changed, where, and what changed in the results as a consequence. Every number in the revised paper was regenerated by `scripts/run_all.sh`.

## Summary of the main changes

1. **Title, framing and positioning.** "Design-stage method" is removed from the title and abstract; the method is presented as a co-selection method whose test-stage instantiation is demonstrated, with a new subsection (3.5) on what a design-stage instantiation must supply (a measured null from hardware of the same class). The overclaim "no equivalent method for electromechanical systems" is deleted; the function–failure / FFIP line (Tumer & Stone 2003; Stone et al. 2005; Kurtoglu & Tumer 2008; Kurtoglu et al. 2010), PHM parameter-suitability metrics (Coble & Hines 2013) and the closed-loop masking literature (Tallam et al. 2003; Cruz & Cardoso 2004; Urresty et al. 2012; Neti & Nandi 2009) are added, with a positioning table (Table 1).
2. **Definitions.** The SDR is positioned statistically (known-onset fixed-sample mean-shift test; standardised effect size; DS as empirical power); δ is now defined against the half of the reference interval adjacent to the fault, as the code computes it; the reliability screen is a one-sided binomial test at the nominal rate (≥ 3 of 9 false alarms at α = 0.95) plus exclusion of features whose null quantile exceeds one; MDE follows the limit-of-detection convention with equal extents pooled into one level; DS is defined for e ≥ e_req; nested and oracle variants are reported with the oracle's false-alarm rate; alternative-dependent diagnosability is formally defined (Section 3.3).
3. **Procedure.** Default rules for steps 1, 2, 5 and 6 (requirement elicitation, trial design, cost ordering, aggregation over failure-mode classes, decision under uncertainty) and a decision table (Table 11) over e_req ∈ {3, 5, 8, 12} % and α ∈ {0.90, 0.95, 0.99} with bootstrap verdicts meets / uncertain / fails.
4. **Corrected data handling.** Phase-to-phase WFSG trials (180) are excluded everywhere; trial counts are stated per table (438 at all impedances; 198 at 2.83 Ω covering four tap pairs per class); the physical-severity axis is RMS/RMS (the fundamental amplitude was a peak value); the zero-sequence current is dropped; the terminal-voltage features form their own measurement group; self-referenced features are normalised on the first half of the reference interval and every transfer metric is reported on all windows and on out-of-reference windows, with healthy trials spread across cross-validation folds and seeded models.
5. **Results that changed.** Under pooled extent levels and the limit-of-detection convention the inter-turn MDE of the SCIG through I2/I1 is 7.4 %, the same as the PMSG's (formerly 2.8 % vs 7.4 %); the PMSG's V2/V1 MDE is 3.8 % (formerly 2.8 %). The claim "the control structure decides where the fault is visible" is withdrawn: the new decomposition (Table 6) shows that the SCIG's higher SDR on current and d-axis features is entirely a quieter healthy null, its relative changes under fault being smaller than the PMSG's. The headline is now stated as "voltage transducers lower the PMSG's detectable extent (7.4 → 3.8 % inter-turn, 9.7 → 2.3 % inter-winding) and add nothing for the SCIG", with the decision table showing that for a requirement near 8 % all three alternatives are equally diagnosable with drive-internal quantities. A commissioning-baseline null (Table 14) shows inter-turn signatures at SDR ≈ 1 when the reference is a healthy trial recorded at another time, which reframes the transfer finding as a short-horizon rule.
6. **Scope and validity.** A scope section (3.6), a rewritten threats-to-validity section, and wording scaled to one machine per alternative throughout the abstract, discussion and conclusions.

## Reviewer 1 (design theory)

**M1 (positioning, novelty).** Done. Section 1 no longer claims that no equivalent method exists; it states what structural diagnosability and FFIP provide and what is new (an empirical, drift-calibrated magnitude comparable across alternatives; the co-selection framing; the family transfer test). Section 2.1 adds the function–failure / FFIP paragraph and the axiomatic-design reading of the controller–monitor coupling (Suh 2001); Section 2.2 adds PHM parameter-suitability metrics and Table 1 (inputs, outputs, sensing cost, stage, alternatives compared).

**M2 (design-stage claim).** Done. "Design-stage" is removed from the title and abstract. Section 3.5 (Design-stage instantiation) states that a simulation can supply the numerator but not the null, that the null must come from measured healthy records of hardware of the same class, and how bench trials validate the pair; Section 6.1 says the same for the "silence vs signature" distinction.

**M3 (steps 1, 2, 5, 6 not reusable).** Done. Section 3.4 gives default rules: e_req from fault growth relative to the inspection interval, α from the tolerable per-trial false-alarm rate, ordinal cost by default and monetary when available, extents bracketing e_req and envelope corners, nine healthy trials for the binomial screen, aggregation over failure-mode classes (a suite must meet every class), and the resolution of uncertain verdicts.

**M4 (step 5 never executed).** Done: Table 11 (decision table) and Section 5.5. As the reviewer anticipated, diagnosability would not have changed the architecture decision in this demonstration; the paper now says so and states when it would (no affordable suite meets the requirement for an alternative).

**M5 (DS pools extents; MDE not monotone-safe; in-sample feature selection).** Done. DS is defined conditional on e ≥ e_req and appears so in the requirement quantities (`R_requirement_quantities.csv`, columns for e ≥ 5 % and e ≥ 8 %) and in the decision table; MDE uses the limit-of-detection convention; the nested (leave-one-tap-pair-out) selection is reported next to the in-sample one (Table A1) and changes no share by more than five points.

**M6 (control structure claim; numerator vs denominator; V2/V1 grouped as controller).** Done. Table 6 decomposes every SCIG/PMSG ratio; the claim is withdrawn and replaced by "machine–drive combination" wording (Sections 5.2, 6.1, abstract). The terminal-voltage features form their own group (Table 7). Table 8 adds the incremental negative-sequence impedance ΔV2/ΔI2.

**M7 (two referencing rules conflated).** Done. Section 5.6 and 6.1 now say that the commissioning baseline transferred only partly and was harmful in one direction, that the rule that worked is a short-horizon one, and Table 14 quantifies the between-trial null. Every transfer metric is also reported on out-of-reference windows (they agree within 0.02).

**M8 (scope).** Done: Section 3.6 (scope conditions) and Section 6.3 rewritten; run-to-failure data are explicitly excluded; the physical-severity axis is part of step 1.

**M9 (suite table inconsistency; fragility).** Done. The inconsistency was caused by the reliability screen removing V2/V1 on the PMSG at three cycles (2 of 9 false alarms under τ = 0.2). The screen is now a binomial test, which passes 2 of 9 and fails 3 of 9; the selection rule is stated in every suite table; Table A2 marks screened features; Section 5.7 states which conclusions depend on window length and α.

**M10 (length).** Partly done. The four findings are stated once each in the abstract, Section 1 and Section 6.1, and the MDE and bootstrap tables are merged (Table 9); Section 5.2 is shorter. The manuscript is nevertheless longer than v1 because the reviewers asked for a positioning table, a decision table, the decomposition, the impedance table, the baseline-null table and the screen table; we judged these additions more valuable than the length saved and moved one figure (operating-point grid) out.

m1 done (formal definition, Section 3.3). m2 done (kVA vs kW explained in Table 2 caption). m3 done (one rule per table, stated in Section 4.2 and every caption). m4 done (the screen is not applicable to the WFSG; stated in Sections 3.2, 4.4 and 6.4). m5 done (demonstration numbers moved to Section 4.4). m6 done (Section 3.2 now says the SDR characterises one specific detector). m7 done (per-trial vs per-window false-alarm units stated; the relay pick-up time inside F stated as a conservative bias). m8 done (terms fixed in Section 3.1). m9 done (figures regenerated without in-figure titles and with the paper's feature notation). m10 done (the design-level severity figure is in Section 5.3). m11 done (Rocha et al. cited for the bench; Blessing & Chakrabarti for the support evaluation; Suh for the coupling; uncited entries are not printed; self-citations trimmed to one sentence). m12 done (funding and repository statements completed; WFSG transfer rows footnoted as optimistic).

## Reviewer 2 (FDI / statistics)

**M1 (MDE implementation vs definition).** Confirmed and fixed. Consequences: SCIG inter-turn MDE via I2/I1 becomes 7.4 % (as the reviewer computed); PMSG V2/V1 becomes 3.8 %; the abstract, Sections 5.3, 5.5, 6.1 and the conclusions are rewritten accordingly, and the SDR ratios and shares now carry the comparison.

**M2 (fragile screen).** Confirmed and fixed with the binomial rule (Section 3.2). The screen is applied in the sensitivity analysis (Table A4 marks screened features) and its outcomes are listed (Table A5). The flip is stated in Section 5.7.

**M3 (5 % reading).** (a) Fixed: Eq. (1) now references R2. (b) Done: the null exceedance by speed is reported (0–16 %, Section 5.4) and the 5 % reading is qualified as an average over the envelope. (c) Partly done: the 55 ms of healthy time inside the PMSG/SCIG faulted interval is stated as a conservative bias (Sections 4.4, 6.4); re-extracting the PMSG/SCIG features with the onset located from the fault current would require a full re-run of the feature extraction on the raw data and was not done in this revision; we note it as future work. Healthy-trial false-alarm counts are reported (Tables A2, A5).

**M4 (statistical positioning).** Done: Section 3.2 ("What the SDR is statistically") and Section 2.2; the contradiction between Sections 3.2 and 3.5 is removed; "how early" is deleted from the introduction and Section 3.6 states that the method evaluates detectability, not time to detection.

**M5 (transfer negatives normalised in-sample; folds; seeds).** Done. Normalisation uses the first half of the reference interval; metrics are reported on all windows and on out-of-reference windows (Tables 12–13); the false-alarm rate at the 1 % threshold on healthy fault-time windows is reported (0–3 %); healthy trials are grouped by speed in the cross-validation; all gradient-boosting models are seeded; hyperparameters are stated in Section 4.4; the TPR at 1 % FPR is described as an ROC point.

**M6 (WFSG counts).** Done: phase-to-phase trials excluded; counts stated per table (Section 4.2, Tables 2, 5, A1, A2).

**M7 (ill-conditioned relative change).** Done: features with null quantile above one are excluded as uninformative (this removes the SCIG torque features); the absolute-change variant is reported (Section 5.7: Spearman 0.92–0.98 with the relative ranking, same top-three features). The |ln| form was not adopted because the absolute variant already answers the question.

**M8 (oracle multiplicity).** Done: the oracle's false-alarm rate on healthy trials is reported beside it (Tables 10, A1; 44–78 % for the large suites) and the oracle is described as an upper bound only.

**M9 (bootstrap units).** Done: cluster bootstrap over tap pairs with the same resampled pairs for both machines (matched ratio); Wilson intervals for shares; MDE resampled within extent levels; the null quantile is held fixed and this is stated (Section 4.4, 6.4). Table 9 shows the MDE intervals wherever MDEs are compared.

**M10 (claims exceed n = 1).** Done: the abstract, Sections 6.1–6.2 and the conclusions are scaled to the units tested; the sensitivity analysis now covers the screen and the two nulls.

## Not addressed in this revision (stated in the paper as limitations or future work)

* Re-extraction of the PMSG/SCIG features with the fault onset located from the measured fault current, and of the angle-free Park's-vector (EPVA) features: both need a re-run of the feature extraction on the raw records; the paper states the resulting bias as conservative and lists the features as future work.
* A population-level ranking of the topologies (replicated machines or simulated populations): not possible on the public data; the wording is scaled to one unit per alternative.
* Re-estimation of the null quantile inside each bootstrap resample: not done; stated.
