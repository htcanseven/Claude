# Reviewer 1 report (electrical machines and drives)

## Summary of the submission

The author proposes the signal-to-drift ratio (SDR): the relative change of a window feature between the 0.36 s before a switched fault and the faulted interval, divided by the 95th percentile of the relative change between the two halves of the healthy reference interval, pooled over all records of a machine. From it he derives a minimum detectable extent (MDE), a detectable share and a signature location per machine, sensor suite and fault class, a decision table for the cheapest adequate suite, and a detector transfer test. It is applied to open benchmarks: a PMSG and an SCIG on one FOC bench with 24 tapped shorts, and a wound-field machine on another bench. Claimed findings: the same tap-pair fault is a different physical event per machine; the signature relocates (terminal voltage in the PMSG, d-axis controller states in the SCIG, field current in the WFSG) and the SCIG's advantage is a quieter null; voltage transducers lower the PMSG's inter-turn MDE from 7.4 to 3.8 % and add nothing for the SCIG; a detector transfers only when self-referenced to each trial's preceding healthy interval.

## Assessment against MSSP criteria

**Originality and advance over established methods.** The SDR is, as the author states (lines 121–126, 206–211), a standardised mean shift with an empirical null; only the within-trial drift null is new. The real novelty is empirical: the first quantitative comparison of winding-fault signatures across three generator types on common tapped faults, which would merit MSSP if the physics held up; the statistic alone would not.

**Technical soundness.** This is where the manuscript fails at present. Checked against the released per-trial tables and code, the central physical conclusions rest on sequence-magnitude features that confound the fault with the inherent asymmetry (the inter-turn results follow the faulted phase), on a relative normalisation whose decomposition is an artefact of the healthy feature level, on extent "levels" that the author's own fault-current table shows to be the same physical fault, and on an "impedance" formed from magnitude increments (Major 1–4).

**Significance and generality.** One machine per type, resistor-limited faults, 40–60 Hz machines at ≤60 % load, and a third bench differing in converter, fault resistance, duration, sampling and operating points: Section 6.4 says so; the abstract, highlights and 6.2 do not.

**Clarity and presentation.** Precise writing, but about a third of the paper (2.3, 3.1, 3.4–3.6, 6.3, Table 1, Fig. 1) is design-methodology scaffolding that an MSSP reader does not need and that is not demonstrated; several table notes are stale (Minor 1). Cut to ~25 pages.

**Reproducibility.** Excellent: public data, released pipeline, every table traceable to a CSV.

## Major comments

**1. The sequence features are magnitudes, and the inter-turn results follow the faulted phase, not the machine (App. A.1 lines 879–882; lines 536–538; Fig. 5).** V2/V1, I2/I1 and D2/D1 are |X−|/|X+|. For inter-turn faults the fault-induced negative-sequence phasor is smaller than the inherent one (PMSG: healthy V2/V1 0.0009, change ≈ 0.00026), so the magnitude rises or falls with the angle between them, which rotates ±120° with the faulted phase. Inter-turn V2/V1 median SDR by faulted phase A/B/C is 1.6/2.8/25 (PMSG) and 1.3/7.4/26 (SCIG); phase-A faults decrease V2/V1 in 64 % of PMSG trials, phase-B faults in 100 % of SCIG trials; D13–D16 (phase A) and D17–D20 (phase C), both 11.6 % at identical fault current (5.87/5.90 A), give SDR 4.2 vs 21 (PMSG) and 2.3 vs 33 (SCIG); all four ≤3.8 % pairs are in phase A. The "mixed sign" increments (line 536), the extent–SDR correlation (line 561) and Fig. 5 all carry this confound, which is why Kliman et al. [6], Arkan et al. (IEE Proc. EPA 148, 2001) and Lee et al. [7] use the phasor residual. Fix: use |ΔĪ2|, |ΔV̄2| (magnitude of the complex phasor difference, referenced to the positive-sequence angle), report inter-turn results per faulted phase, recompute Figs. 4–5 and Tables 4–7, and state that the 2fe ripple of i_d, i_q is the same negative-sequence current in the rotating frame, so the current and controller groups differ by normalisation and angle source, not physics.

**2. The extent levels are not physical and the headline MDE difference does not survive (lines 383–387; 5.3; Table 5; abstract; Highlight 4).** The author's fault-current table gives 1.41–1.44 A (PMSG) and 1.09–1.16 A (SCIG) for the 2.7, 2.8, 2.8 and 3.8 % pairs, and equal currents within the 7.4/7.7 and 11.6/12.0/12.1 % clusters; by lines 396–397 equal resistor-limited current means equal shorted turns. The PMSG's "MDE 3.8 %" on V2/V1 is one pair, D21–D22 (median 1.45); the other three give 0.26–1.17. Pooling the four equivalent pairs gives median SDR 0.83 (PMSG) and 0.61 (SCIG): neither machine detects the ~3 % fault, and the inter-turn MDE of V2/V1, I2/I1 and Vq 2fe is ~7.5 % on both. At 7.4 % the PMSG pair D06–D07 is undetectable on V2/V1 (median 0.51, share 44 %), hidden by pooling. The voltage-transducer claim thus holds only for inter-winding faults, and only at ≥5 cycles (Table A.2: 9.7 % at 3 cycles). Table 5's Wilson share intervals (±8–10; line 665) treat 108 correlated trials as independent; the tap-pair bootstrap gives ±20 (PMSG V2/V1: [55, 94]). Fix: define levels by shorted turns from the data papers, bootstrap MDE and shares over tap pairs, show per-pair results, and rewrite abstract, Highlight 4, 5.5 and 6.2.

**3. Table 4's "signature versus silence" decomposition is an artefact of the relative normalisation (lines 502–511; 697–699; abstract).** Numerator and denominator both carry 1/|φ̄_R|, so their cross-machine ratios are ratios of healthy levels. In absolute units the fault-induced inter-turn changes are equal, V2/V1 2.6e-4 vs 3.0e-4 and I2/I1 1.35e-3 vs 1.04e-3 (PMSG vs SCIG), and the SCIG nulls are 1.2× (V2/V1), 3.3× (I2/I1, D2/D1) and 6× (Id 2fe) quieter, not 2.6–35×; the "factor 35" is a 5.8× larger healthy 2fe ripple in the SCIG's i_d. "The PMSG's relative changes are the larger ones" says only that its inherent asymmetry is smaller. Moreover D2/D1 on the SCIG fails the author's own screen at 3, 5 and 8 cycles (3/9, 4/9, 5/9 false alarms, lines 920–923) yet anchors the "controller output that involves no sensor" argument (lines 503–506), which is physically wrong anyway: duty cycles are PI outputs of the sensed currents and the angle. Fix: decompose in per-unit terms with healthy levels shown, exclude screened features, rewrite the conclusion.

**4. The "incremental negative-sequence impedance" is not an impedance, and the drive parameters that would explain the relocation are missing (lines 530–544, 694–696, 744–748).** The code forms ΔV2/ΔI2 from magnitude increments, undefined for inter-turn faults (PMSG IQR −2 to 16 Ω) and not an impedance for inter-winding ones. Under current control the ratio is dominated by the controller's closed-loop output impedance at the negative-sequence frequency [10], as Cheng, Zhang and Habetler (IEEE TIE 58, 2011) exploit. The paper never gives the current-loop gains or bandwidth of either drive, nor the SCIG flux observer's structure and bandwidth, although its angle estimate is modulated at 2fe by the negative sequence and can itself generate the SCIG's dq signature. Fix: use the complex phasor ratio; compare it with the passive negative-sequence impedance (15 and 7 Ω are ~0.7 and 0.4 pu) and the controller transfer function at 2fe; report Kp, Ki, bandwidth, switching frequency and observer; then the 10 Hz direct-drive claim can be computed rather than asserted.

**5. The WFSG bench does not support "identical faults" (abstract; Highlight 2; 4.1–4.2; Table 2; Table A.1).** Rf 1–11.32 Ω vs 2.6 Ω, faults of 0.06–0.28 s vs 0.4 s, a 3L-NPC converter, converter-side voltages at 4 kHz with undocumented filtering. Table A.1's WFSG inter-turn shares (78–98 %) are computed on 11.6–12.1 % faults only, whereas the PMSG/SCIG shares cover 2.7–12.1 %; at 11.6–12.1 % the PMSG and SCIG detect 100 % on V2/V1, so lines 602–604 read backwards. Dataset [26] is titled "under healthy and faulty conditions" and the author's inventory script expects HEALTHY_GER files, yet Table 2 says "none": clarify, and use any healthy records. Fix: present the WFSG as a qualitative third case at matched extents, or document the above and drop "identical".

**6. Known-onset, self-referenced detection is not condition monitoring, and the transfer claim hides the operating point (3.2, 5.6, 5.7, 6.1–6.2, Highlight 5).** Every design quantity is referenced to the healthy seconds before an abrupt switched fault; a turbine monitor does not know the onset, the fault persists, and the comparison is against a baseline. Table A.3 then puts the inter-turn signatures at SDR ≈ 1 with shares of 20–59 %, i.e. at the limit of any practical monitor on these features. "Transfers in all six directions with AUC 0.80–0.98" (lines 639–640) conceals Table A.4's TPR at 1 % FPR of 0.35 (SCIG→PMSG) and 0.41 (two machines→PMSG). Fix: lead with the commissioning-baseline numbers and TPR at fixed FPR; define the self-referencing rule in operation (sliding reference, horizon, reset) or present it as a change-point result; temper abstract and highlights.

**7. Sensor suites, severity axis and literature.** (i) The angle-free I2/I1 is the extended Park's vector approach (Cruz and Cardoso, IEEE TIA 37, 2001); say so. (ii) Zero-sequence voltage needs only an artificial neutral and works under FOC (Hang et al., IEEE TPEL 30, 2015); stray flux (Henao et al., IEEE Ind. Electron. Mag. 8, 2014) is the other established route; both belong in 4.3 and 6.2. (iii) Missing: Tallam et al., IEEE TIA 43, 2007; Zafarani et al., IEEE JESTPE 6, 2018; Shah, Nandi and Neti, IEEE TIA 45, 2009, which contradicts the unsupported DFIG assertion in lines 748–750; Yang et al., IEEE TIE 57, 2010. (iv) The severity axis If/Is (Fig. 5 right) ignores the shorted turns: the MMF asymmetry scales with If × e, and inter-winding faults are circulating-current faults whose loop spans both branches. (v) "Scaled from the IEA 15 MW turbine" (lines 400–403): state the rule; a torque ratio of 1.5 is not region-2 T ∝ ω², and 8 N·m is ~60 % of rated torque.

## Minor comments

1. Table 5 note defines "–" but none occurs; Table A.2 footnote a is unused; Table 7 note defines "exc", which never appears, and its suite ordering omits "+ torque".
2. Lines 520–521: "(6.7 and 74; 79 and 100 %)" mixes Id 2fe's share (79) with PId 2fe's (78 in Table 5).
3. Lines 206–211: a z-score of the mean shift, not Cohen's d (the denominator is the spread of differences of interval means).
4. Lines 232–234 say an absolute-change variant "is used" for noise-floor features; compare3.sdr_from_windows applies the relative form to every feature. State which is used where.
5. R1/R2 are split by window start time, so windows starting just before 0.62 s extend into R2 and narrow the null; assign by window end.
6. Lines 293–294: the step-2 rule R ≥ 2|F| is violated by the demonstration (0.68 < 0.72 s).
7. Screen (lines 236–242): with n = 9, a feature with a 20 % false-alarm rate passes 74 % of the time; state the power and interval (PMSG V2/V1 at three cycles: 2/9 = 22 % [6, 55]).
8. Null exceedance of I2/I1 concentrates at SCIG 1800 rpm (16 %), where the 59.6 Hz stator frequency nearly coincides with the 60 Hz grid: check for a beat with the grid-side converter or prime mover.
9. Table 2 / 4.1: add rated currents, switching frequencies, current-loop gains, observer type and transducer/anti-aliasing bandwidths (at 4 kHz the NPC's switching components alias into V2 unless filtered).
10. Reference [36]: "2026b" is a leaked BibTeX label; the declaration mentions LLM literature retrieval, so verify every reference.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The data, openness and question suit MSSP, but the central physical findings are, on the author's own released numbers, artefacts of magnitude sequence features, relative normalisation and non-physical extent levels, and the cross-bench and wind-turbine claims exceed the evidence. A re-analysis with phasor-residual features, physical levels, per-unit decomposition and documented drive parameters may still yield a publishable comparison; acceptance cannot be assumed. Confidence: high.
