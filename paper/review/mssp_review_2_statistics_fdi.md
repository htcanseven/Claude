# Reviewer 2 report (change detection and FDI statistics)

## Summary of the submission

The paper proposes the signal-to-drift ratio (SDR): the relative change of a feature between a healthy reference interval and the adjacent faulted interval, divided by the 95th percentile of the relative change between the two halves of the reference interval, pooled over all trials of a machine. From it follow a minimum detectable extent (MDE), a detectable share (DS), a signature location, a bootstrap decision table selecting the cheapest sensor suite, and a transfer test for learned detectors under three referencing rules. The method is applied to open benchmark data from a PMSG, an SCIG and a WFSG with identical tapped inter-turn and inter-winding short-circuits. The claimed findings are that the signature relocates with the machine–drive combination, that the PMSG–SCIG difference is a difference of healthy nulls, that voltage transducers help the PMSG only, and that a detector transfers only with self-referenced features.

## Assessment against MSSP criteria

**Originality and advance.** The statistic is a mean-shift statistic studentised by an empirical quantile of its own null, i.e. the sensitivity index d′ (Basseville's Gaussian KL detectability, the fault-to-noise ratio of model-based FDI) with the noise scale replaced by a pooled drift quantile; the author concedes this (lines 121–126). The new parts are the calibration, the requirement quantities, the decision table and the three-machine comparison; they meet the MSSP bar only if statistically sound, and several are not yet (comments 1–5).

**Technical soundness.** The qualitative findings are supported. The quantitative instruments are fragile (comments 1–5): the null quantile's sampling variability is ignored in every interval, the MDE is a grid-valued statistic decided by levels at the threshold, the decision table rests on a fixed null and in-sample selection, the Wilson intervals ignore tap-pair clustering, and nine healthy trials cannot support the "5 % per-trial false alarm" wording.

**Significance and generality.** The three-generator comparison on identical faults is valuable and new. But the headline sensor-suite conclusion is not supported by the paper's own decision table at the confidence it demands (comment 3), the "comparable across machines" and "detector-free" claims are overstated (comment 6), and the known-onset, immediately-preceding reference favours short-horizon detectors by construction (comment 7).

**Clarity and presentation.** Well written and candid in Sections 5.7 and 6.4; the candour has not reached the abstract, highlights and conclusions, and several numbers in the text disagree with the tables (comment 5, minor comments).

**Reproducibility.** Excellent: code, data and per-trial tables are open; every number I queried could be checked.

## Major comments

1. **Statistical identification of the SDR (Section 3.2, lines 206–212; Eqs. 1–2).** "Cohen's d on interval means, divided by 1.96" is wrong: Cohen's d is scaled by the within-interval standard deviation, whereas the SDR denominator is a quantile of the *between-interval* drift, which equals the GLR's σ/√N only for white noise. The 1.96 presumes a zero-mean Gaussian δ′; from the released per-trial tables q0.95(|δ′|)/(1.96 sd) is 0.95–1.11 for the key features, but the excess kurtosis reaches 4.8 (PMSG V2/V1), so at α = 0.99 the denominator is the 2nd–3rd largest of 225 values. More important, the denominator is an estimate held fixed in every bootstrap (lines 456, 928): resampling the 225 null values (iid or by tap pair) gives 95 % intervals for q0.95 spanning a factor 1.2–1.5; no reported interval carries this. The pooled null is also a mixture over operating points and classes; the per-speed exceedance of 0–16 % (line 585) shows the per-trial size varies threefold. **Fix:** describe the SDR as a mean-shift statistic calibrated by the empirical quantile of its own drift null; resample the null with the numerator in the tap-pair bootstrap; relate the measure in Section 2.1 to d′, the Mahalanobis/KL detectability of [15] and the fault-to-noise ratio of model-based FDI. A threshold-free companion, the AUC of |δ| (fault trials) against |δ′| (null), costs nothing and would link Sections 5.3 and 5.6.

2. **MDE convention on the coarse grid (Section 3.3; Tables 5, A.2; lines 651–652).** The limit-of-detection rule makes the MDE a discrete statistic decided by levels at the threshold: PMSG inter-winding V2/V1 at the 3.2 % level has median SDR 1.03 at five cycles and 0.81 at three, which alone moves the MDE from 2.3 to 9.7 % (attributed to window length at line 651); SCIG inter-turn V2/V1 has median 1.04 at 2.7 % but 0.49 and 0.53 at 2.8 % and 3.8 %, so "7.4 % [7.4, 7.4]" hides that the smallest level is detected. Tap-pair location dominates: PMSG inter-turn V2/V1 has median 5.2 at the 7.4 % level (three pairs) and 1.4 at 7.7 % (one pair). The bootstrap "over operating points within each extent level" cannot see this: levels realised by one pair (3.8, 7.7, 12.0, 12.1 %) yield intervals such as "3.8 [2.8, 3.8]" with false precision. **Fix:** report the MDE as a bracket (largest undetected, smallest level above which all are detected), resample tap pairs, or fit log SDR on log extent with tap-pair random effects and read a continuous MDE with an interval.

3. **Decision-table semantics and the headline claim (step 5; Table 7; abstract; highlights).** "Meets when the bootstrap probability that MDE ≤ e_req is at least 0.95" is a one-sided percentile-bootstrap bound of a grid-valued statistic, computed with the null fixed and the fixed feature selected on the same trials (revision.py selects the feature before bootstrapping). Percentile intervals for such a statistic with nine trials per level have no coverage guarantee, and "median SDR ≥ 1" is a 50 % power criterion, not what the requirement sentence (lines 277–279) implies. Consequence: at α = 0.95 the table never selects the PMSG voltage suite (uncertain at 3 and 5 %, drive-internal meets at 8 %); it is selected only at α = 0.90, e_req = 5 %. The abstract, highlights and conclusions nevertheless claim that "voltage transducers lower the permanent-magnet machine's detectable extent", resting on the five-cycle point estimates of Table 6. **Fix:** define the verdict as a percentile bound; nest the feature selection inside the bootstrap; add a power condition (e.g. DS ≥ 0.9 for e ≥ e_req); resample the null; align the abstract with Table 7.

4. **Reliability screen and false-alarm claims (lines 236–242, 657–660; Appendix A.2).** With nine healthy trials the binomial screen has size 0.8 % and passes a feature with a true 20 % (30 %) false-alarm rate with probability 0.74 (0.46). At three cycles the selected fixed features show 2/9 false alarms (PMSG V2/V1; SCIG Id 2fe, V2/V1, Iq 2fe; Wilson 6–55 %), so the "per-trial false-alarm rate ≤ 5 %" is undemonstrated for exactly the features Table 7 uses. The oracle false-alarm rates of 44–89 % are what multiplicity predicts (1 − 0.95^k = 56–76 % for k = 16–28 features), so the oracle shares are uninformative as reported. **Fix:** calibrate the oracle on the null of max_f SDR_f (Westfall–Young); give Wilson intervals for the healthy false-alarm rates; note that "up to three features removed per setting" is the 1.5 expected by chance across 30 features.

5. **Intervals that do not mean what the text says; verified inconsistencies.** (a) Table 5's Wilson intervals assume 108 independent trials; the author's own tap-pair cluster bootstrap (H_bootstrap.csv) gives PMSG inter-turn V2/V1 76 [55, 94] against Wilson [67, 83], and line 665 quotes the narrower one. (b) The S/P ratios differ between Table 4 (1.40, 1.91, 0.78, 12.95) and Table 5 (1.1, 1.49, 0.97, 11.8) for the same feature, class and window, because Table 5 reports the median of the bootstrap distribution rather than the full-sample estimate. (c) Line 605 "the nested selection changes no share by more than five points" and lines 781–782 "immaterial here" are contradicted by Table A.1 (WFSG 3 CT inter-turn 98 → 86).

6. **Overclaims (abstract; lines 42–43, 199–201, 223–231; Table 1).** By the author's own statement (lines 217–219) the SDR characterises one detector, a known-onset threshold on interval means; "detector-free" should become "requires no trained detector". The screen and the nuisance-transient check need healthy trials, so "needs no dedicated healthy recordings" is false. Comparability across machines is dominated by the null (Table 4: null ratios up to 35 against numerator ratios 0.3–0.7) and by its horizon (Table A.3: inter-turn median SDRs fall to ≈ 1, DS to 20–59 %, against a commissioning baseline). The decomposition and the between-trial null are part of the result, not robustness checks, and belong in the abstract and beside every MDE. The WFSG rows (no healthy trials, 81 of 438 trials with fewer than three windows, a reference/fault ratio of 1.75 against the R ≥ 2|F| rule of Fig. 1, the extent–impedance confound) should be marked provisional in Fig. 4 and Tables 7, A.1 and A.4, not only in Section 6.4.

7. **Transfer experiment (Sections 4.4, 5.6; Tables 8, A.4).** (a) No uncertainty is given for any AUC although overlapping windows make the effective sample 216 trials; differences of 0.02 are interpreted (lines 627–628, 635). (b) The hard negatives, contactor operation without a fault, are 141 of ~21,000 negatives (0.7 %), so the AUC and the 1 % FPR threshold are insensitive to contactor artefacts and the "0–3 %" check (line 637) has an effective n of nine. (c) TPR at 1 % FPR contradicts the AUC story: SCIG→PMSG 0.35, suites 0.45–0.50 self-referenced and 0.02–0.03 under the commissioning rule (F_suite_transfer.csv); "transfers in all six directions" should be tempered. (d) The commissioning competitor is weak: z-scoring pooled over operating points, whereas the natural rule is per-operating-point z-scoring against the healthy trial at that point (the baseline of Table A.3); without it, "transfers only when self-referenced" is not established. (e) Self-referencing is the SDR's own assumption (known onset, immediately preceding reference), so step 6 partly restates step 3; a short-horizon self-referenced monitor adapts to a slowly growing incipient fault, the realistic case, and recommending it as the family specification (lines 726–728) needs that caveat. (f) Lines 643–645: 0.95 and 0.90 are within-machine values; cross-machine ranges are 0.82–0.92 and 0.62–0.86. Report trial-level metrics with cluster-bootstrap intervals.

## Minor comments

1. Table 7 note: "exc: WFSG field current" appears, but no "exc" entry exists in the table.
2. Lines 652–655 vs Table A.2: tightening α lowers shares by 0–8 points (SCIG Vq 2fe 60 → 59; SCIG IW 100 → 100), not "3–9"; the MDEs that move are one inter-winding (PMSG V2/V1, 2.3 → 9.7 %, two levels) and one inter-turn (Vq 2fe, 7.4 → 7.7 %), not "two inter-winding MDEs up one level".
3. Line 606: oracle false-alarm "44–78 % for the large suites" matches neither Table A.1 (67 and 89 % for 3 CT + 3 VT + drive) nor Table 6's five-cycle values (78 % for every drive-containing suite).
4. Eq. (1): R1 (0.32 s), R2 (0.36 s) and F differ in duration, and windows assigned by start time make R1 overlap R2 by up to n_c − 1 cycles; state the effect on the null.
5. Line 203: say that the null pools the reference intervals of 216 fault and 9 healthy trials over all operating points and classes, and that α = 0.99 is set by the top 2–3 values.
6. Table 5: give MDE endpoints on the tested grid only (9.5 % for PMSG I2/I1 is an interpolation artefact) and name the resampling unit in each column header.
7. Figs. 4–5: give an impedance legend for the WFSG markers (unreadable at print size) and say in Fig. 4's caption that the WFSG is pooled over confounded impedances.
8. Line 579: the 1800 rpm range is 15.5–20.5 (D_operating_point_grid.csv); "trial", "record" and "recording" are used interchangeably.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The data, question and openness are strong and the qualitative findings credible, but the instruments that carry the quantitative claims (fixed null, grid-valued MDE with operating-point resampling, decision table with in-sample selection, Wilson intervals, nine-trial screen) do not support the precision claimed, and the abstract's sensor-suite and comparability claims are not what the paper's own Table 7 and Table A.3 show. All of this is repairable from the released per-trial tables without new experiments. Confidence: high on the statistical points, medium on the machine-specific interpretation.
