# Royal Society International Exchanges — Global Round 3 (2026/R3): Application Review

**Documents reviewed:** `International_Exchanges_Global_Programme_Scheme_Notes.pdf` (ref **AKB/07/2026**, "International Exchanges Scheme 2026 – Global Round 3", 20 pp.), cross-checked against the [live scheme page](https://royalsociety.org/grants/international-exchanges/) on 27 July 2026.
**Proposed application:** Nur Sarma (Durham, lead applicant) + Hüseyin T. Canseven (LUT, co-applicant), **36-month duration**.

---

## 1. Verdict

The scheme is a good match for what you want to do — bilateral UK–Finland visits, seed funding, engineering is in remit, and Finland is eligible. But **the plan as described has two problems, one of which is fatal as stated**:

| # | Issue | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **36 months is not an option.** The scheme offers only 3 months, 1 year, or 2 years. | **Blocker** — must change to 2 years |
| 2 | **The scheme funds new collaborations only**, and you published together in June 2026. | **Serious eligibility risk** — resolve before writing |

Neither is a reason to abandon the application, but #2 needs a direct answer from the Royal Society before you invest effort, and #1 changes your project design.

---

## 2. Blocker: there is no 36-month option

The scheme notes are explicit and repeat this in three separate places:

> **§1.5 Duration and Start of Award** — "Awards are available for either **up to 3 months, 1 year or 2 years**. Activities must start no earlier than 5 months after the closing date."

> **§11.2, Research Proposal → End Date** — "This must be **either 3 months, 1 year or 2 years** from the start date."

> **§11.2, Financial Details → Duration** — "Specify the duration of your project: **3 months, 1 year or 2 years**."

The three funding options, in full:

| Option | Duration | Max total | Max consumables | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-off visit | 1 week – 3 months | **£3,000** | £0 | Single visit, either direction |
| Multiple visits | within **1 year** | **£6,000** | £1,500 | Must be bilateral |
| Multiple visits | within **2 years** | **£12,000** | £3,000 | Must be bilateral |

**What to do instead.** Apply for the **2-year / £12,000** option. Then note this sentence in §1.5:

> "Award holders can apply for **up to one-year no-cost extension**."

So the route to a 36-month project is: apply for 2 years, and request a no-cost extension during the award. Two caveats — it is **no-cost** (the budget stays £12,000, so you must not plan 36 months of activity against a 24-month budget), and it is requested after the award is made, not at application. Do **not** write "36 months" anywhere on the form; the End Date field will reject it and it signals you haven't read the notes.

Note also that "the start of the project" is defined generously — "any activity undertaken to progress the project, which includes booking flights or planning a schedule of activities" — so the clock starts earlier than your first visit, which effectively gives you a little more runway than it looks.

## 3. Serious risk: "new collaborations only" versus your joint paper

This is the issue that most needs your attention. The requirement appears twice, and in the second instance it is an **eligibility condition**, not merely an aim:

> **§1.2 Aims** — "This scheme is **not intended to support continued research activities** between a UK applicant and a co-applicant who was a former colleague or PhD student or to support **other existing or recent collaborations between the applicant and co-applicant**. Rather, the scheme is intended to stimulate new collaborations."

> **§4.3 Eligible Activity** — "**Applications must be new collaborations.** This scheme is not intended to be used to extend an existing or recent project or collaboration."

Against this you have: *Canseven, Ercire, Cömert, Ünsal, **Sarma**, "Advanced Fault Detection of Permanent Magnet Faults in Offshore Wind Turbine Generators Using FEA and Deep Transfer Learning," Machines 2026, 14(6), 665* — published **June 2026**, three months before the deadline. That is, on the plain reading, a "recent collaboration between the applicant and co-applicant."

One clause does work in your favour — §4.1's hard bar is narrower than §4.3's:

> "The applicant and co-applicant **cannot be former colleagues or PhD student/supervisor**."

You are not former colleagues and there is no supervisory relationship, so you clear the *hard* eligibility bar. The risk lives in the softer §4.3 wording, which a panel could apply either way.

**How to handle it — in order of preference:**

1. **Ask the Royal Society directly, now.** The notes explicitly invite this: "If there is any doubt about the eligibility of a project, contact the Grants team via international.exchanges@royalsociety.org" (also +44 20 7451 2666, Mon–Thu 10:00–16:00). Send a short, factual email: one co-authored paper published June 2026 arising from a specific piece of simulation work; the proposed project is X, which is new in question, method and data. Ask whether this is eligible. Get it in writing. This costs one email and protects weeks of effort — and their answer determines whether you proceed at all.
2. **If they say yes, frame the project as a genuine departure.** The published work was FEA-generated fault signatures classified by deep transfer learning — computational, using a public reference generator model. A defensible "new collaboration" project is one that is *experimental* and uses capabilities neither of you has yet combined: for example, embedding fibre-optic (FBG) strain and thermal sensing into a physical machine and validating fault signatures on the Durham test rig against LUT-designed hardware. Different research question, different method, different data, new equipment. Be transparent about the prior paper in the career/publications section — concealing it would be far worse than declaring it.
3. **If they say no,** you lose nothing but an email, and the alternatives in the main report (Post Doc Pool, MSCA, RCF/EPSRC parallel grants, COST STSMs) are unaffected. COST Action CA25138 SAFEWIND in particular funds precisely this kind of exchange with no novelty requirement.

My honest read: this is a real risk, perhaps the single biggest determinant of whether this application is worth writing. Resolve it first.

## 6. Budget design (2-year option, £12,000)

Split: **max £3,000 consumables**, remainder travel and subsistence (so ~£9,000 travel).

**Eligible consumables:** visa charges, police registration fees, postage and printing, books, reagents, computer software, stationery for overseas visitors in the UK, fieldwork expenses, animal/plant licence fees.

**Explicitly ineligible** (a longer list than most people expect): publication costs, **computer hardware**, bench fees, **conference registration fees, travel to conferences**, departmental costs/overheads, access charges, medical insurance, excess baggage, dependents' expenses, **any contribution towards salaries**, and **travel to a third country** (so you cannot meet at a conference in a third country on this grant), plus costs of participants not at the applicant organisations.

⚠️ **Two practical notes.** Software is eligible but **hardware is not** — if the project needs FBG interrogator components or sensors, that is not fundable here, so design the science around existing kit at both sites. And **visa charges are eligible**: if Hüseyin's passport requires a UK visitor visa, budget it explicitly (Finnish residence does not by itself remove a visa requirement).

The travel justification field requires a **provisional visit plan** in a prescribed format:

> "e.g. Last name of individual 1: Length of visit, Estimated date of visit, Destination, Cost… i.e. Professor Smith: 2 months, November 2026, Rome, £600"

An illustrative shape for £12,000 over two years (adjust to real fares; economy/APEX rates are expected):

| Traveller | Length | When | Destination | Indicative cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canseven | 2 weeks | Apr 2027 | Durham | £2,000 |
| Sarma | 1 week | Sep 2027 | LUT, Lappeenranta | £1,300 |
| Canseven + LUT doctoral researcher | 2 weeks | Mar 2028 | Durham | £3,400 |
| Sarma + Durham PhD student | 1 week | Sep 2028 | LUT | £2,300 |
| **Travel subtotal** | | | | **£9,000** |
| Consumables (visas, software, printing) | | | | £3,000 |

Including a PhD student on each side is worth doing: it is explicitly encouraged, it strengthens the "benefits to institutions" and "long-term collaboration" answers, and it costs nothing extra in assessment terms.

## 7. How it will be assessed, and how to write it

**Assessment:** two panel members with the closest expertise score it against six criteria — "Successful applications should be strong in **all** respects":

1. The research background of the UK and overseas scientists
2. The strength of the scientists and institution in the area of proposed research
3. **The mutual benefit of the collaboration**
4. The strength of the proposal
5. **The potential contribution to the UK science base**
6. **The potential for long-term collaboration**

Criteria 3, 5 and 6 are where applications are actually won or lost — 1 and 2 you already satisfy on paper. Note also: **no feedback is given to unsuccessful applicants**, and the Royal Society does not publish success rates for this scheme (I checked; they have declined to release them, and the scheme is one of their highest-volume calls — so assume it is competitive).

**Subject tags matter mechanically:** "The subject group and areas that you select… play a vital role in matching your application with appropriate reviewers" — select 1–4, and pick electrical/electronic engineering and energy tags precisely, since a mis-tagged application can land with a reviewer who has no feel for electrical machines.

**Word limits — the proposal is very short:**

| Field | Limit |
|---|---|
| Project title | 20 words |
| Field of specialisation | 20 words |
| Summary of current research (each applicant) | 200 words |
| Abstract | 400 words |
| Lay summary | 250 words |
| **Research proposal** | **500 words** |
| Participants | 200 words |
| Benefits to individuals/institutions | 200 words |
| Benefits to UK | 200 words |
| Benefits to overseas country | 200 words |
| Data management and sharing | 200 words |

500 words for the science means one clear question, one method, one validation route — no work-package structure, no literature review.

**Content pointers for your specific case:**

- **Lay summary (250 words)** — the notes stress this: "the Committee is composed of a broad range of experts who will not all be specialists… Committee members place particular importance on this part." Write it about offshore wind turbines breaking down far out at sea and being expensive to reach — not about interharmonics or FBG multiplexing. This is the single most under-invested field in most engineering applications.
- **Benefits to the UK (200 words)** — concrete and specific: UK offshore wind at ~16 GW operational with a record 8.4 GW awarded in CfD Allocation Round 7 (Jan 2026); Durham's proximity to ORE Catapult at Blyth; skills for the North East offshore cluster. Say what the UK gets that it does not have: access to LUT's PM generator design and prototyping capability and the Lappeenranta drivetrain test ecosystem.
- **Benefits to Finland (200 words)** — Finland's offshore programme is at the starting line (Tahkoluoto plus its extension, Korsnäs, the 2026 EEZ auctions), and Durham brings two decades of turbine condition-monitoring and reliability experience Finland has no equivalent of. The cold-climate/icing angle is genuinely distinctive.
- **Mutual benefit and long-term collaboration** — this is where you name the follow-on funding explicitly: MSCA Staff Exchanges (deadline 15 April 2027), Horizon Europe cascade calls from the €93M wind project, and parallel RCF/EPSRC bids. The scheme's own stated aim is "seed corn funding to… strengthen applications for further funding", so naming the specific instruments and their dates is answering the question they asked.

## 8. Submission mechanics — who does what

Everything runs through **Flexi-Grant** (https://grants.royalsociety.org); paper applications are not accepted. Nur must initiate it from her own account.

Four people must complete sections before submission is even possible:

1. **Hüseyin** (co-applicant) — career summary, publications, funding history, diversity monitoring
2. **Durham Head of Department** — must confirm "The applicant/co-applicant will be provided with adequate space and access to resources within my department"
3. **LUT Head of Department** — same confirmation for the Finnish side
4. **Durham's institutional approver** — research office delegated authority; must approve **by the deadline**

Optional but recommended: invite Durham's **Research Support** role to check the budget before submission (it can be revoked if not completed, so it won't block you).

Other mechanics: **ORCID is mandatory at submission** for both of you — register in advance if either lacks one. Both applicants must complete the **diversity monitoring** form or the system treats the application as incomplete. And a useful safety valve: if a participant submits their section prematurely, the lead applicant can **re-open** it from the Participants tab.

⚠️ **Disclosure of AI use.** §9 requires it: "Where generative AI tools are used in funding proposals their use should be acknowledged in the application by **naming the AI source and specifying how the content was generated (for example by listing the prompt used)**." If you use any AI assistance — including this review's suggestions — in drafting the application text, declare it in the form as required. This review itself is background analysis rather than proposal text, but keep the rule in mind as you write.
