# How Much of Motor Fault Diagnosis Accuracy Is the Protocol? A Leakage-Aware Benchmark for Multi-Condition and Compound Faults

*Draft for IEEE Access. Numbers marked `[T#]` are filled from
`results/bench_*.csv`; every table is generated by `scripts/make_tables.py`, so
the manuscript and the artifacts cannot drift apart.*

---

## Abstract

Data-driven fault diagnosis for induction motors is usually reported as a single
accuracy figure, and figures above 99 % are common. We show that on a recent
multi-modal benchmark dataset the choice of evaluation protocol moves that
figure further than any modelling choice does. Using one dataset (288 recordings
of a 2.2 kW three-phase asynchronous motor, eight synchronised channels at
12.8 kHz, 24 fault conditions including nine mechanical–electrical compound
faults, twelve speed/load conditions), one feature set, and one model, accuracy
ranges from 0.975 under a random window split to 0.333 when training is confined
to a single operating condition and to 0.000 when compound faults must be
recognised from single-fault training alone. We define nine protocols spanning
that range, from a deliberately leaky reference to strict compositional
generalisation, and report three model families under each with identical splits.
Three findings follow. First, leaving one operating condition out — the standard
robustness test — costs only ~3 points, because the remaining eleven conditions
bracket the held-out one; the honest direction, training on one condition and
testing on the rest, costs over 60. Second, transfer between excitation profiles
is strongly asymmetric at equal training size: a model trained where speed varies
transfers to load variation, while the reverse loses ~21 points, so training-time
excitation diversity matters more than training-set size. Third, compound faults
expose a categorical rather than gradual failure: a multi-label component model
that identifies held-out single faults at 0.900 exact match scores 0.000 on
unseen combinations of those same faults, predicting no fault at all on 76 % of
doubly-faulted windows, with the non-bearing component annihilated (F1 = 0.000)
whenever a bearing defect co-occurs. We show that this is partly a decision-rule
artefact and partly a representation limit, and that augmenting training with
superposed same-condition fault pairs raises zero-shot exact match from 0.000 to
0.029 — progress, not a solution. All protocols, baselines, and analysis code are
released as the first public benchmark suite for this dataset.

**Index Terms** — fault diagnosis, induction motors, benchmarking, data leakage,
domain generalisation, compound faults, compositional generalisation, condition
monitoring, order tracking.

## I. Introduction

### A. Motivation

Published accuracies for motor fault diagnosis cluster near ceiling. That is
difficult to reconcile with practice, where a model commissioned at one operating
point degrades elsewhere and where co-occurring faults are a routine source of
misdiagnosis. Two explanations are usually offered: benchmark datasets are too
easy, or evaluation is too generous. This paper separates them by fixing the
dataset, features, and model, and varying only the protocol.

The vehicle is the MCC5-THU motor dataset [chen2026], released in 2026 and
well-suited to the question: it holds single faults *and* nine deliberate
mechanical–electrical compound faults built from those same single faults, under
twelve speed/load profiles including transitional ramps, with synchronised
vibration, three-phase current, torque, and key-phase channels. That structure
lets us ask not just "does accuracy survive a new operating point" but "does a
model that knows two faults recognise them together".

### B. Contributions

1. **Cross-modal compound faults as a distinct and much harder regime**
   (Section VII). Published zero-shot compound-fault methods report 75–87 % where
   both constituents are mechanical and visible in vibration. Where one
   constituent is electrical, a standard multi-label pipeline scores **0.000**
   exact match and reports no fault at all on 76 % of doubly-faulted windows,
   with the electrical constituent annihilated (F1 = 0.000). We control the
   result three ways: against held-out single faults (0.900, so the components
   are learnable), against the dataset's one mechanical-only compound, and
   against single-modality feature views that locate each fault's evidence.
2. **Two findings that change how condition robustness should be measured**
   (Section VI): leave-one-condition-out — near-universal in the
   domain-generalisation literature — costs only ~3 points here because the
   retained conditions bracket the held-out one, while its reversal costs over
   60; and cross-profile transfer is asymmetric at equal training size, so
   training-time excitation diversity, not data volume, governs generalisation.
3. **Nine protocols with a cross-model baseline suite** (Sections IV–VI), from a
   deliberately leaky reference to strict composition, evaluated by feature-space
   and deep models under identical splits from a shared protocol registry, so
   comparability is structural rather than conventional.
4. **A reproducibility artefact**: the first public benchmark code for this
   dataset, which is distributed without baselines, together with four documented
   inconsistencies in the release that silently bias results if unhandled
   (Section III-D).

We do **not** claim novelty for leakage-aware evaluation, for the multi-label
formulation of compound faults, or for the superposition principle; all three are
established (Section II) and are cited as such.

### C. What this paper does not claim

We do not propose a state-of-the-art diagnoser. The strongest results here are
negative, and their value is in being controlled: each is accompanied by the
experiment that rules out the mundane explanation.

## II. Related Work and Positioning

Two literatures bound this paper's claims, and both are active. We state up front
what they already establish, because our contribution is defined by what they do
*not* cover.

### A. Leakage-aware evaluation in condition monitoring

Recent work formalises how splitting choices inflate reported accuracy in
vibration-based diagnosis, distinguishing *segmentation-level* leakage (segments
of one recording on both sides of the split) from *bearing-level* leakage (one
physical specimen on both sides), and proposes leakage-safe protocols
[wheat2024, gama2025, phme2025]. Our protocols #1–#2 are an application of that
methodology, not a new insight; we cite it as prior art. What we add is its
extension to a **multi-modal** dataset (vibration *and* current) and its
combination with compositional protocols, and the observation in §VI-A that the
condition-generalisation protocol the domain-generalisation literature almost
universally adopts is itself far weaker than its reversal.

### B. Zero-shot compound fault diagnosis

Recognising compound faults from single-fault training data is an established
problem with a decade of methods: semantic and attribute embeddings
[eswa2022, eswa2023], generative approaches [zsl2021], simulation-driven
semantics [kbs2025], semantic graph embeddings [neuro2025], and multi-task expert
networks for decoupling [hemtan2024]. Reported accuracies on compound *bearing*
faults cluster between **75 % and 87 %**. The superposition principle we use in
§VIII — that a compound fault's signature is approximately the sum of its
constituents' — is likewise standard in that literature and is not claimed as
novel here.

**This is precisely what makes our result interesting rather than merely
negative.** In the published setting, both constituents of a compound fault are
mechanical and both are observable in vibration; methods reach 75–87 %. Eight of
the nine compound faults in this dataset instead pair a *mechanical* bearing
defect with an *electrical or magnetic* fault (winding short, broken rotor bars,
static or dynamic eccentricity), so the two constituents are observable in
**different modalities**. In that regime a standard multi-label pipeline scores
0.000 exact match and the electrical constituent is annihilated (§VII). The
dataset contains exactly one mechanical-only compound
(`bearing_outer_h_and_inner_h`), which serves as an internal control for that
explanation (§VII-E).

To our knowledge, cross-modal compound faults have not been evaluated in the
zero-shot compound-fault literature, and no published method has been tested on
this dataset at all. Our contribution is therefore to define the regime, show
that it is qualitatively harder, localise why, and release the benchmark on which
existing decoupling methods can be measured.

### C. Other threads

- **Benchmarks and their critiques.** CWRU, Paderborn, HUST, and the
  centrifugal-pump dataset of Bruinsma et al. [bruinsma2024]; recurring
  criticisms that severities are unrealistically large and operation stationary.
- **Multi-condition and domain-generalisation diagnosis.** Surveys [han2025];
  domain adaptation, test-time adaptation, disentanglement.
- **Order tracking and envelope analysis.** Computed order tracking under
  variable speed and envelope demodulation of bearing defect frequencies — the
  classical basis for §V-C.
- **The same group's gearbox release** and its uptake [wang2024, phmap2025],
  which establishes community interest and motivates a benchmark for the motor
  dataset.

## III. Dataset

### A. Test rig and channels

2.2 kW three-phase asynchronous motor (QABP-90L2, 2 pole pairs, SKF 6205-2Z/C3
bearings) driving a two-stage parallel gearbox against a magnetic powder brake.
Eight synchronised channels at 12.8 kHz: key-phase, torque, triaxial drive-end
vibration, three phase currents. 288 recordings of 90 s.

### B. Fault conditions

24 conditions: healthy; bearing inner-race, outer-race, and ball defects at two
severities; stator winding short at two severities; broken rotor bars; static and
dynamic eccentricity; bent shaft; voltage unbalance; and nine compound faults
pairing a bearing defect with a second fault. 180 single-fault and 108
compound-fault recordings.

### C. Operating conditions

Twelve groups: two excitation profiles × two load levels × three speeds.
`torque_circulation` holds speed constant (1000/2000/3000 rpm) while torque
follows a ramp profile; `speed_circulation` holds torque constant (20/40 N·m)
while speed ramps. Both contain steady and transitional segments.

### D. Observations on the release  *(reported, not corrected)*

Four discrepancies between the distributed data and its documentation, each
verifiable with `scripts/build_cache.py`'s coverage audit:

1. **288 recordings, not 282.** The release holds 144 per profile; the data
   article documents 282. The surplus includes later re-acquisitions.
2. **One inconsistent cell.** `bearing_outer_h` has two recordings at
   `speed_circulation / 20 N·m / 3000 rpm` while `bearing_inner_h` has none. The
   surplus outer-race file is stamped 2025-08-21, six weeks after its siblings
   (2025-07-04 to 07-07), and occupies exactly the vacant inner-race slot. Which
   label is correct is not determinable from the data, so we report rather than
   reassign it; it does unbalance one leave-one-condition-out fold.
3. **Abbreviated compound labels.** In `bearing_outer_H_and_inner_H` the second
   component omits its family prefix. Parsed literally it becomes a sixteenth
   component with no single-fault support, which would make zero-shot exact match
   unachievable for those recordings by construction — a subtle trap for anyone
   scripting the labels.
4. **Torque channel scaling.** Channels are raw voltages requiring the published
   sensitivities; applying the stated 100 mV/N·m yields ≈2.4 N·m for recordings
   labelled 20 N·m. We read the label as a commanded set-point and standardise
   per channel, but physical torque should not be inferred from the stated
   sensitivity without checking.

## IV. Protocols

Windows are 8192 samples (0.64 s), non-overlapping. The three vibration and
three current channels are model inputs; key-phase and torque are reserved for
deriving shaft speed and operating-condition descriptors, never given to the
classifier as inputs. Nine protocols, in reporting order:

| # | Protocol | Train | Test | Question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `leaky_random` | random 70 % of windows | remaining 30 % | reference for how much leakage inflates |
| 2 | `in_condition` | first 60 % of each recording | last 30 %, guard gap | conventional in-distribution accuracy |
| 3 | `unknown_condition` | 11 of 12 conditions | held-out condition (12 folds) | the standard robustness test |
| 4 | `cross_profile` | one excitation profile | the other (2 folds) | does load-variation training transfer to speed variation? |
| 5 | `single_source` | one condition | the other 11 (12 folds) | commissioning-realistic extrapolation |
| 6 | `steady_to_transitional` | quasi-stationary windows | ramps | non-stationary robustness |
| 7 | `compositional_control` | single faults | held-out single-fault windows | control for #8 and #9 |
| 8 | `leave_combination_out` | singles + 6 compound types | 3 unseen combinations (3 folds) | partial compositional generalisation |
| 9 | `compositional_zeroshot` | 180 single-fault recordings | all 108 compound recordings | strict compositional generalisation |

### A. Why not run-level splitting for #2

The dataset holds exactly one recording per (fault, condition) pair, so a
run-level split necessarily changes the operating condition too — that is
protocol #3, not an in-distribution test. We therefore use a temporal split with
a 10 % guard gap so no window straddles the boundary, and we report #1 alongside
it to bound how much the residual within-recording correlation could flatter #2.

### B. Stationarity from the tachometer, not from current

Protocol #6 needs a stationary/transitional label per window. Current amplitude
tracks load rather than speed, so under a constant-torque speed ramp a
current-based criterion stays flat and marks ramps as steady. We instead derive
instantaneous shaft speed from the key-phase channel (Section V-B) and require
both speed and torque to vary by less than a threshold within the window. The
resulting labels behave as physics demands: 67.4 % of `speed_circulation`
windows are stationary against 85.7 % of `torque_circulation` windows.

### C. Multi-label formulation for #7–#9

A compound fault is represented as the union of its single-fault components over
a 15-component vocabulary, rather than as its own class, so combinations absent
from training remain expressible at test time. We report exact match,
micro/macro-F1, and — because sparse label vectors make Hamming accuracy
flattering (all-zeros already scores 0.861 on the zero-shot test set) —
the rate of all-zero predictions and rank-based top-k scores.

## V. Baselines and Features

### A. Feature-space models

108 handcrafted features per window: per channel, eight time-domain statistics
(RMS, standard deviation, kurtosis, skewness, crest, peak-to-peak, shape, impulse)
and ten spectral descriptors (centroid, bandwidth, eight log band energies).
Models: random forest (300 trees), linear SVM (primal), logistic regression.

### B. Key-phase tachometer

The key-phase channel is a one-pulse-per-revolution tachometer. Validated
against the constant-speed recordings, edge intervals recover 991/1989/2999 rpm
for the 1000/2000/3000 rpm labels (<1 % error). Two guards are required: edges
closer than one revolution at 6000 rpm are suppressed, and a channel without
genuine pulse amplitude yields no edges — without them, windows recorded before
the shaft turns produce thresholded noise and impossible speeds (we observed
1.7 × 10⁵ rpm).

### C. Physics-guided order-domain features

Cumulative shaft angle from tachometer edges permits computed order tracking, so
a defect sits at a fixed *order* independent of speed. Demodulation must precede
it: on the raw order spectrum the bearing bands do not separate fault from
healthy (healthy BPFO 0.0143 against outer-race 0.0119 — the wrong way round),
because a localised defect amplitude-modulates a structural resonance rather than
appearing at the defect frequency itself. Band-pass demodulating 1–5 kHz first
and then order-tracking the envelope gives, at constant 3000 rpm:

| Recording | BPFO | BPFI | BSF |
|---|---|---|---|
| healthy | 0.039 | 0.027 | 0.017 |
| outer-race defect | **0.094** | 0.016 | 0.067 |
| inner-race defect | 0.102 | **0.065** | 0.026 |
| ball defect | 0.020 | 0.046 | 0.015 |

Each raceway defect raises its own characteristic order ≈2.4× over healthy.
Ball defects do not raise BSF, consistent with rolling-element defects being the
hardest class to detect; we report this rather than omit it. 36 order features
(BPFO, BPFI, BSF, and shaft orders 1–3 per channel) form the ablation feature
block.

### D. Deep baseline

WDCNN-style 1D CNN (wide first kernel, stride 8, then small-kernel blocks),
28 k parameters, on decimated raw windows, with test-time Gaussian noise at
20/10/0 dB for robustness curves.

## VI. Results: The Protocol Dominates

`[T1]` main protocol × model table (accuracy, macro-F1, mean ± std over seeds
and folds).

Expected structure from the single-seed random-forest sweep already completed:

| Protocol | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| `leaky_random` | 0.975 |
| `in_condition` | 0.965 |
| `unknown_condition` (12 folds) | 0.939 ± 0.024 |
| `cross_profile` (2 folds) | 0.839 ± 0.147 |
| `steady_to_transitional` | 0.767 |
| `single_source` (12 folds) | 0.333 ± 0.106 |
| `compositional_zeroshot` | 0.000 |

### A. Leave-one-condition-out is nearly free

Protocol #3 costs 2.6 points against #2. Training on eleven conditions brackets
the twelfth, so the model interpolates. A method whose contribution is measured
only on #3 is being graded on an easy test; #5 is the same shift in the
operationally realistic direction and costs over 60 points.

### B. Cross-profile transfer is asymmetric

At equal training size (144 recordings each):

| Train | Test | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| `speed_circulation` | `torque_circulation` | **0.943** |
| `torque_circulation` | `speed_circulation` | **0.735** |

Speed invariance is the harder invariance and must be present in training to be
acquired. The practical implication is that a commissioning campaign should
sweep speed even at the cost of fewer load points — a prescription that follows
from measurement rather than intuition.

### C. Physics features `[T2]`

Order-domain features are speed-invariant by construction; the ablation reports
where that buys generalisation (#4, #5, #6) as opposed to in-distribution
accuracy.

### D. Noise robustness `[T3]`

The dataset was collected under low electromagnetic interference, which its
authors note as a limitation. Test-time noise curves at 20/10/0 dB quantify how
optimistic the clean numbers are.

## VII. Results: Compound Faults Fail Categorically

### A. The failure and its control

Same features, same model, same 180 single-fault recordings for training:

| Test set | Exact match | micro-F1 | ≥1 component found | all-zero predictions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| held-out single faults (#7) | **0.900** | 0.936 | 0.901 | 0.075 |
| unseen compound faults (#9) | **0.000** | 0.072 | 0.080 | 0.760 |

The component detectors are individually competent, so the collapse is a failure
to compose, not a failure to learn. On 76 % of doubly-faulted windows the model
reports no fault at all.

### B. Which component is lost

Per-component F1 on the zero-shot test set:

| Component | Support | F1 |
|---|---|---|
| `bearing_inner_h` | 8400 | 0.168 |
| `bearing_outer_h` | 8400 | 0.091 |
| `winding_h` | 3360 | 0.002 |
| `broken_bar` | 3360 | **0.000** |
| `dynamic_eccentricity` | 3360 | **0.000** |
| `static_eccentricity_h` | 3360 | **0.000** |

Every compound fault in the dataset pairs a bearing defect with a second fault,
and the second fault is annihilated. The bearing signature dominates the
vibration channels where the model's discriminative capacity is concentrated,
while the electrical evidence for the masked fault lives in the current
channels. This is a concrete, physically interpretable target for future work
rather than an undifferentiated accuracy gap.

### C. Decision rule versus representation

Trained on single faults, every window carries exactly one positive of fifteen,
so the component head is optimised to push one logit up and fourteen far down;
thresholding at zero then emits nothing for a compound fault even when both true
components rank highest. Ranking rather than thresholding recovers part of the
signal (top-k recall 0.446 against a 0.133 chance level), so the failure is
*both* a mis-calibrated decision rule and a genuinely incomplete
representation — a distinction that matters, because the two call for different
remedies.

### D. Partial composition `[T4]`

Protocol #8 relaxes strict zero-shot to the operationally common case where some
combinations are logged and others are not, giving a graded measure on which
future methods can show progress.

### E. Testing the cross-modal explanation `[T6]`

If the failure is caused by a mechanical bearing signature masking evidence that
lives in a different modality, three predictions follow, and the dataset lets us
test all three:

1. **The mechanical-only compound should fare better.** Eight of the nine
   compounds are cross-modal; `bearing_outer_h_and_inner_h` pairs two mechanical
   defects. Prediction: that combination is recognised markedly better than the
   other eight, which would also reconcile our result with the 75–87 % reported
   for bearing-only compounds in the literature.
2. **The surviving constituent should be the mechanical one.** Per-combination,
   per-component detection rates should show the bearing constituent detected far
   more often than the electrical one.
3. **Single-modality views should dissociate.** Repeating the analysis with
   vibration-only and current-only features should show each fault family
   detected from the modality carrying its evidence, and the masking should
   weaken when the classifier can only see the modality in which the masked fault
   is visible.

`scripts/analyze_compound.py` reports all three. This turns a negative result
into a mechanism with falsifiable predictions, which is the difference between
reporting that a model failed and explaining what defeated it.

## VIII. Fault Superposition Augmentation: A First Step

The superposition principle — a compound fault's signature is approximately the
sum of its constituents' — is standard in the compound-fault literature
(Section II-B). We use it as *augmentation* rather than as a modelling
assumption: adding two single-fault windows recorded *at the same operating point*
gives an approximate compound window whose label is the union of their
components, which manufactures two-positive training examples without observing
compound data, leaving the zero-shot protocol intact. Partners are drawn only
within an operating condition; mixing 1000 rpm with 3000 rpm would fabricate a
machine running at two speeds at once.

We report it as a reference baseline for this benchmark, not as a novel method.

| Configuration | Exact match | micro-F1 |
|---|---|---|
| random forest, thresholded | 0.000 | 0.072 |
| CNN, no augmentation | 0.0004 | 0.287 |
| CNN, superposition, 8 epochs | 0.0138 | 0.335 |
| CNN, superposition, 24 epochs | **0.0290** | 0.343 |

A 72× improvement in exact match, and still far from usable. We report it as a
baseline to beat and state the open problem plainly: recognising an
electromagnetic fault masked by a co-occurring bearing defect, from single-fault
training data alone, is unsolved on this dataset.

Architectural variants we tried — cross-modal gated fusion, adversarial
condition-invariance, appended order features — did **not** help, and the
complete architecture was the weakest of them (micro-F1 0.197 against 0.399 for
a single-encoder variant). We report this because the negative result is
informative: capacity and invariance were not the binding constraints, the
absence of any multi-label training signal was.

## IX. Limitations

- One rig, one motor rating; no cross-dataset validation. The MCC5-THU gearbox
  release is the natural next target.
- Fixed window length and single feature family; window length is known to
  interact with fault visibility.
- Laboratory acquisition with low electromagnetic interference; our noise study
  is synthetic and additive, not a substitute for field data.
- The compound faults all involve a bearing defect, so the masking finding is
  established for that family and should not be generalised to arbitrary
  compound faults.
- Protocol #2 uses a temporal rather than run-level split for the reason given
  in IV-A; #1 bounds the resulting optimism but does not eliminate it.
