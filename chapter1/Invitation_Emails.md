# Invitation emails

Short-form invitations in the author's own register, one per invitee. The long-form
version with the terms sheet, credit model, budgets and schedule is in
`Contributor_Invitation.md`; use that after the publisher has approved, when the
detailed scope is settled. These emails deliberately promise less.

**Attach to every one:** the draft letter of support, and nothing else for the moment.
Chapter 1 and the annotated table of contents go to the publisher, not to the invitees,
so each email carries its own description of the invitee's part instead. When those
documents do eventually circulate, delete the Status column from the contributor table
first, so that nobody reads that a colleague is "suggested".

**Tailor the opening.** The Belahcen email that went out added a personal line after the
first sentence — that the topic is closely aligned with a project the two already share.
One such sentence, specific to the recipient, is worth more than any amount of template.

**Personalise the attached letter too.** Send each invitee a draft with their own name,
department and field already filled in, as was done for Belahcen, rather than a form with
brackets. It is easier to correct a draft than to compose one, so more of them come back.
Any factual claim about the person's own record stays a bracket for them to confirm.

**Fill in before sending:** the reply date, the submission date, and the signature.
Say who "we" is at least once if the recipient does not know all three authors.
