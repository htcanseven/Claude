Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Physics-Informed Diagnosis of Rotor Winding Inter-Turn
Short-Circuit Faults in Synchronous Generators Using Stray Magnetic Field Signals," for
publication in *IET Electric Power Applications* as an original research article.

The manuscript addresses non-invasive diagnosis of rotor winding inter-turn short circuits in
salient-pole synchronous generators using external stray magnetic field measurements. The topic
falls within the journal's scope on rotating electrical machines and generators, and it builds
directly on work published in *IET Electric Power Applications*, including the overviews of
stray-flux monitoring techniques by Mazaheri-Tehrani and Faiz and of synchronous-generator fault
detection by Mostafaei and Faiz.

The practical difficulty we address is that the stray field of a grid-connected generator varies
strongly with excitation and reactive power, so a diagnostic model validated at one operating
point can fail at another. The paper contributes:

1. a first-order rotor-MMF model of the fault signature, showing why fundamental-normalized
   harmonic ratios reduce excitation dependence — and where this reduction breaks down, in
   under-excited operation, where part of the fundamental is set by the grid rather than by the
   field current;
2. a leave-one-operating-point-out evaluation of stray-field feature representations and learning
   models across seven excitation and power-factor conditions, testing generalization to unseen
   operating conditions rather than to random data splits;
3. an ordinal severity-calibration stage that corrects the systematic misclassification of mild
   faults as healthy at the most under-excited condition, without using held-out data.

All results are obtained from an openly available measurement dataset (CC BY 4.0,
DOI 10.17632/d75sb25f7m.1). The limitations of the study — a single 10 kVA laboratory machine at
a fixed apparent power — are stated explicitly in the manuscript.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration
for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare no conflicts of
interest.
